# ARTICLE IV — THE LEGISLATIVE POWER

> **Draft note:** This version removes provisions that repeated Articles I and III and replaces them with cross-references, so that qualifications, terms, oaths, and rights limits are stated in one place only.

## Section 1 — Vesting of Legislative Power

### IV.1.a — Congress

The legislative power of the United States is vested in a Congress consisting of a House of Representatives and a Senate.

Congress shall exercise only legislative powers granted to it by this Constitution.

### IV.1.b — Enumerated Legislative Powers

Congress shall have power to:

1. enact laws within the jurisdiction granted to the federal government by this Constitution;
2. lay and collect taxes, duties, imposts, and excises, subject to this Constitution;
3. appropriate public funds;
4. borrow money on the credit of the United States;
5. issue currency and regulate its value;
6. regulate interstate and foreign commerce and other matters expressly placed within federal jurisdiction;
7. establish courts inferior to the Supreme Court;
8. establish executive departments and administrative agencies as authorized by law;
9. establish uniform rules concerning naturalization and citizenship where authorized by this Constitution;
10. make laws for the territories and the federal capital district, subject to VII.2.f and VII.3.c;
11. provide for the common defense;
12. declare war and authorize the sustained use of armed force;
13. raise, organize, regulate, and maintain the armed forces, subject to civilian constitutional control;
14. provide for the execution of federal law;
15. enforce Articles I, II, and III against every level of government, by laws that establish uniform standards of governmental conduct protecting the rights those Articles establish and that provide remedies for their violation; such laws may give greater protection than those Articles require, but shall not reduce it;
16. prohibit discrimination because of protected status (I.4.a) by private actors in employment, housing, education, credit, and public accommodations, and provide remedies for it, subject to the freedoms of Article I, Section 1;
17. enact laws necessary and proper to carry into execution powers vested by this Constitution in the federal government; and
18. exercise such other legislative powers as are expressly granted by this Constitution.

No law shall enlarge a power beyond the authority granted by this Constitution.

### IV.1.c — Legislative Limits

Every law enacted by Congress is subject to Article I. A law that restricts a constitutional right shall satisfy I.4.c.

Congress shall not exercise executive or judicial power except where this Constitution expressly permits a limited legislative function.

Congress shall not delegate its entire legislative authority to any person, office, agency, or other institution.

Any delegation of legislative authority shall state:

1. the purpose of the delegation;
2. the subject matter governed;
3. the authority delegated;
4. the standards governing its exercise; and
5. the limits upon that authority.

No delegated authority shall exceed the authority Congress itself possesses.

## Section 2 — House of Representatives

### IV.2.a — Composition

The House of Representatives shall consist of members elected from districts established under Article III, Section 4.

### IV.2.b — Election

Representatives shall be elected by the qualified voters of their respective districts.

### IV.2.c — Qualifications

A Representative shall be a citizen of the United States and shall satisfy the age eligibility and other qualifications established by III.6.

A Representative shall have resided in the constituent jurisdiction (VII.2.a) containing the district represented for at least one year immediately before the election. A change in district boundaries under Article III, Section 4 shall not disqualify a Representative who satisfies this requirement.

### IV.2.d — Terms

Representatives serve the term established by III.5.a and are subject to the term limits of III.5.b and III.5.c.

### IV.2.e — Vacancies

When a vacancy occurs in the House, the affected district shall elect a successor according to law.

A temporary appointment may be made only under IV.4.e.

---

## Section 3 — Senate

### IV.3.a — Composition

Each constituent jurisdiction (VII.2.a) shall elect two Senators.

Each Senator shall possess one vote.

### IV.3.b — Election

Senators shall be elected by the qualified voters of the jurisdiction they represent.

### IV.3.c — Qualifications

A Senator shall be a citizen of the United States and shall satisfy the age eligibility and other qualifications established by III.6.

A Senator shall have resided in the jurisdiction represented for at least one year immediately before the election.

### IV.3.d — Terms

Senators serve the term established by III.5.a and are subject to the term limits of III.5.b and III.5.c.

Senate elections shall be staggered so that approximately one-half of the Senate is elected every two years.

### IV.3.e — Vacancies

When a vacancy occurs in the Senate, the affected jurisdiction shall elect a successor according to law.

A temporary appointment may be made only under IV.4.e.

---

## Section 4 — Organization of Congress

### IV.4.a — Officers

Each House shall choose its officers and shall establish rules governing its proceedings.

No rule of either House may conflict with this Constitution.

### IV.4.b — Quorum

A majority of the members of either House shall constitute a quorum to conduct legislative business.

A smaller number may adjourn from day to day and may compel the attendance of absent members in a manner established by the rules of that House.

### IV.4.c — Journals

Each House shall maintain a public journal of its proceedings.

The votes of individual members upon final passage of legislation shall be recorded.

Temporary withholding of information shall be permitted only when constitutionally necessary for national security or the protection of individual rights and shall be limited in scope and duration.

### IV.4.d — Legislative Sessions

Congress shall assemble at least once each calendar year.

Neither House shall adjourn for an extended period without the consent of the other House where such adjournment would prevent the constitutional functioning of Congress.

### IV.4.e — Temporary Appointments

A vacancy in either House may be filled by temporary appointment only where necessary to preserve representation until the successor elected under IV.2.e or IV.3.e assumes office. The appointment shall terminate when the elected successor assumes office.

The Election Authority (III.3) shall make every temporary appointment. The appointee shall satisfy the qualifications for the office.

Where the vacancy arose from the death of a member:

1. if the member was affiliated with a political party at the time of death, the appointee shall be affiliated with the same party and shall be chosen from nominees submitted by that party; and
2. if the member was not affiliated with any political party at the time of death, the appointee shall not be affiliated with any political party.

An appointee required to be affiliated with a party shall have been continuously affiliated with that party for at least one year immediately before the vacancy. An appointee required to be unaffiliated shall have been continuously unaffiliated with any political party for at least one year immediately before the vacancy.

If a party required to submit nominees submits no qualified nominee within fourteen days after the vacancy, the Election Authority shall appoint a qualified person affiliated with that party.

No appointee shall be required to vote according to the direction of any political party, and no appointment shall be revoked because of how the appointee votes.

## Section 5 — Legislative Procedure

### IV.5.a — Passage of Bills

A bill shall become law only after:

1. passage by both Houses in identical form;
2. presentation to the President; and
3. signature by the President, expiration of the review period in IV.7.d without veto, or enactment over a veto under IV.7.e.

### IV.5.b — Public Notice

A final bill shall be made publicly available before it becomes law.

Congress shall establish reasonable procedures ensuring that the public and members of Congress have meaningful opportunity to examine proposed legislation before final passage.

Emergency legislation may be enacted under expedited procedures established by law, but no emergency procedure shall suspend a constitutional right or eliminate the constitutional requirements for enactment.

### IV.5.c — Single Subject

Each bill shall address a reasonably identifiable legislative subject.

Congress shall not combine unrelated legislation for the purpose of concealing its substance, evading constitutional review, or preventing meaningful legislative consideration.

### IV.5.d — Legislative Record

Congress shall maintain a permanent public record of:

1. proposed legislation;
2. final legislation;
3. recorded votes;
4. committee reports;
5. amendments adopted by either House; and
6. other legislative materials required by law.

---

## Section 6 — Legislative Committees, Investigations, and Subpoenas

### IV.6.a — Investigative Authority

Each House and its committees may conduct investigations reasonably related to a lawful legislative purpose.

An investigation shall not be used as a substitute for a criminal prosecution or judicial proceeding.

### IV.6.b — Constitutional Limits

Congress shall not investigate, compel testimony, or demand records for the purpose of punishing a person for exercising a constitutional right.

### IV.6.c — Subpoenas

A legislative subpoena shall:

1. identify the lawful legislative purpose of the inquiry;
2. identify the person or records sought with reasonable particularity;
3. comply with applicable constitutional privileges; and
4. remain subject to judicial review.

A court may invalidate a subpoena that exceeds Congress's constitutional authority.

---

## Section 7 — Presidential Action on Legislation

### IV.7.a — Presentation

Every bill passed by Congress shall be presented to the President.

### IV.7.b — Approval

The President may sign the bill into law.

### IV.7.c — Veto

The President may veto a bill by returning it to the House in which it originated with written objections.

The President shall not veto a bill selectively by deleting or altering individual provisions.

### IV.7.d — Failure to Act

If the President neither signs nor vetoes a bill within ten days after presentation, excluding days of the President's religious leave (I.0.a), the bill shall become law unless Congress has adjourned in a manner that prevents its return.

A day counts as religious leave for this purpose only if the President publicly designated it in advance. The President shall make this designation upon taking office and may amend it once each calendar year. An amendment shall not apply to a bill presented before the amendment was made public.

No more than ten days of religious leave shall be excluded from the review period of any one bill.

### IV.7.e — Veto Override

Congress may override a presidential veto by a two-thirds vote of the members voting in each House, provided that the constitutional quorum requirements are satisfied.

Upon such vote, the bill shall become law.

## Section 8 — Legislative Immunities and Ethics

### IV.8.a — Oath

Members of Congress shall take the oath required by III.6.c before exercising legislative authority.

### IV.8.b — Speech and Debate

Members of Congress shall receive such legislative immunity as is necessary to preserve independent legislative deliberation.

Such immunity shall not extend to criminal conduct, corruption, or acts unrelated to legitimate legislative activity.

### IV.8.c — Conflicts of Interest

Congress shall establish neutral and publicly enforceable rules concerning financial conflicts of interest, disclosure of relevant financial interests, and ethical conduct by members and officers of Congress.

Such rules shall not be used to suppress lawful political activity or discriminate on the basis of political viewpoint.

### IV.8.d — Public Accountability

Members of Congress remain subject to this Constitution and to generally applicable criminal law.

Holding legislative office shall not place a person above the law.

## Section 9 — Impeachment

### IV.9.a — Impeachable Officers

The President, Vice President, federal judges, and other civil officers established by this Constitution may be impeached.

### IV.9.b — Grounds

Impeachment shall be permitted only for treason, bribery, corruption, serious abuse of constitutional authority, or other grave misconduct established by law consistent with this Constitution.

Impeachment shall not be used merely because an officer:

1. exercised a lawful constitutional power;
2. made an unpopular decision;
3. expressed an unpopular opinion; or
4. belongs to or supports a particular political party.

### IV.9.c — House of Representatives

The House shall possess the sole power to initiate impeachment.

Articles of impeachment shall identify the alleged misconduct with sufficient particularity to provide meaningful notice to the accused.

A majority vote of the House shall be required to impeach.

### IV.9.d — Senate Trial

The Senate shall possess the sole power to try an impeachment.

Senators shall act under oath or affirmation during an impeachment trial.

The accused shall receive lawful notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard.

Conviction shall require the affirmative vote of two-thirds of the Senators present.

### IV.9.e — Judgment

Judgment upon conviction shall extend only to:

1. removal from the office held; and
2. disqualification from holding federal office, when imposed pursuant to constitutional or lawful procedures.

Impeachment shall not prevent criminal prosecution or civil liability for conduct independently subject to such proceedings.

### IV.9.f — Presidential Impeachment Trial

When the President is tried, the Supreme Court judge with the longest current continuous service shall preside over the Senate. Where two or more judges have equal service, the presiding judge shall be chosen among them by lot.

No person may preside over an impeachment proceeding in which that person has a direct personal conflict of interest. Where the judge who would preside is disqualified, the judge next in length of service shall preside.

---

## Section 10 — Appropriations and Public Funds

### IV.10.a — Appropriations Required

No money shall be withdrawn from the Treasury except pursuant to an appropriation made by law.

### IV.10.b — Public Purpose

Public funds shall be used only for lawful public purposes authorized by this Constitution or by legislation enacted pursuant to it.

### IV.10.c — Transparency

Congress shall establish procedures for public accounting of federal revenue, expenditures, obligations, and debt.

Exceptions for temporary national-security secrecy shall be narrowly construed and subject to lawful oversight.

---

## Section 11 — Legislative Continuity

### IV.11.a — Continuity

Congress shall establish procedures necessary to preserve constitutional legislative government during disasters, attacks, widespread emergencies, or other circumstances that temporarily prevent ordinary legislative operations.

### IV.11.b — Preservation of Representation

No continuity procedure shall permanently replace elected representation with executive appointment or military authority.

Temporary emergency procedures shall terminate when ordinary constitutional legislative operations can reasonably resume.

### IV.11.c — Succession

Congress shall establish laws governing temporary vacancies and inability to perform legislative duties, consistent with the requirement that legislative authority ultimately remain vested in elected representatives.

---

## Section 12 — Subordination of Legislation to the Constitution

### IV.12.a — Invalid Legislation

A law enacted in violation of this Constitution shall have no legal effect to the extent of the violation. Neither presidential approval nor enactment over a veto shall make an unconstitutional law constitutional.

No legislative enactment may repeal, suspend, or nullify a constitutional provision except through the process established by Article IX.

### IV.12.b — Judicial Review

A court of competent jurisdiction may determine whether an act of Congress exceeds constitutional authority, and may provide a remedy under II.9.c.
