# ARTICLE VIII — SUPREMACY, RATIFICATION, AND TRANSITION

## Section 1 — Supremacy of This Constitution

### VIII.1.a — Supreme Law

This Constitution is the supreme law of Concordia.

Every law, treaty (V.7.b), regulation, executive order, and governmental action, at every level of government, is subordinate to it. *(For federal and state law, see also VII.3.b; for legislation, IV.12; for judicial review, VI.3.)*

The judges of every court, in every constituent jurisdiction, are bound by this Constitution, notwithstanding anything in the law of any jurisdiction to the contrary.

### VIII.1.b — Oath of Office

Every person holding legislative, executive, or judicial office, at every level of government, shall swear or affirm to support this Constitution before exercising the authority of the office. *(This extends III.6.c and IV.8.a to appointed officials and to officials of the constituent jurisdictions.)*

### VIII.1.c — No Religious Test

No religious test, and no profession of religion, belief, or non-belief (I.0.a), shall be required as a qualification for any public office or public trust.

Every oath under this Constitution may be sworn or affirmed, with or without reference to a deity, as the person chooses.

### VIII.1.d — Plain Meaning

This subsection and VIII.1.e govern how every court and every governmental official reads this Constitution and every law, regulation, and executive order, at every level of government.

**Ordinary meaning:** Words have the meaning an ordinary, reasonable reader would have understood them to have when the provision was adopted, read in the context of the whole provision and the document in which it appears. A word the document defines has its defined meaning. A technical term of law, science, or a trade has its technical meaning where the context shows it is used in that sense.

**Consistent meaning:** A word or phrase has the same meaning everywhere it appears in the same document, unless the context clearly requires otherwise.

**Text controls:** Where the meaning is clear, it shall be applied as written. No reading shall add a term, condition, or exception that the text does not contain, or remove one that it does, except as VIII.1.e permits. Statements of what the drafters privately intended shall not override clear text.

**Aids to reading:** Headings, "In plain words" summaries, summaries required by VIII.1.f, and notes in italics or parentheses help explain the text but do not change it. Where one of them conflicts with the text, the text controls.

**Ambiguity:** Where the text can reasonably be read in more than one way:

1. the reading that preserves the greater protection of a constitutional right controls (VI.3.d);
2. otherwise, a provision that imposes a penalty, tax, fee, or duty on a private person shall be read in that person's favor; and
3. otherwise, the reading that best serves the provision's purpose, as shown by its text, controls.

### VIII.1.e — Absurd Results

**Definition:** An absurd result is a result of applying a provision, read under VIII.1.d, that no reasonable person who adopted the provision could have intended, because it:

1. makes the provision impossible to obey or carry out;
2. puts the provision in conflict with another provision of the same document so that both cannot be obeyed;
3. defeats the provision's own purpose, as shown by its text; or
4. is so contrary to common sense that no reasonable reader would accept it as what the provision requires.

A result is not absurd because it is harsh, costly, unpopular, or unwise, or because the court or official would have chosen differently.

**Rule:** Where applying a provision as written would produce an absurd result, a court or official shall adopt the reading that departs least from the text and avoids that result. Where no reading can avoid it, the provision shall not be applied to the extent of the absurd result, and the rest shall be given effect (VI.3.c). The person claiming an absurd result bears the burden of showing it.

**Limits:** This subsection shall not be used to:

1. narrow a right established by this Constitution, create an exception to it, or read a restriction on it more broadly (I.4.c);
2. enlarge a power of government or extend a limit of time or scope on one; or
3. create or increase a penalty, offense, tax, fee, or duty that a person would not bear under the text.

Where the only way to avoid an absurd result would do one of these things, the text applies as written until it is corrected: for this Constitution, by a correction amendment (IX.3.d); for any other provision, by the body that made it.

**Reasons and correction:** A court or official that relies on this subsection shall state in writing the result found absurd, why it is absurd, and the reading adopted. An official's decision is subject to judicial review (VI.3.a). Every court decision relying on this subsection, and every finding that a result is absurd but cannot be avoided, shall be sent to the body that made the provision: for this Constitution, to Congress, as a possible drafting error under IX.3.d.

### VIII.1.f — Plain Language

**Duty:** Every law, regulation, and executive order, every amendment proposed to this Constitution, every measure put to the voters, and every official form or notice that tells a person what the person must do, may do, or is entitled to, shall be written in plain language: clear to an ordinary adult reader, with its terms defined where needed, and no longer than its subject requires.

**Summary:** Every law enacted by Congress shall be published with a plain-language summary, adopted with it, of what it does and whom it affects. The summary is not law (VIII.1.d).

**Fair notice:** No person shall be fined, taxed, or denied a benefit under a provision so unclear that an ordinary person could not reasonably understand what it requires. *(For criminal law, see II.7.a.)*

**Reliance on official explanations:** No person shall be penalized for an act taken in good faith reliance on a written statement of the law given to that person, or published to the public, by the government body responsible for it, if the statement was wrong. The person shall comply with the law as correctly stated from the time the person is told of the error.

A failure to use plain language does not by itself make a provision invalid.

## Section 2 — Ratification

### VIII.2.a — Referendum in Each Jurisdiction

The Founding Convention shall submit this Constitution to each jurisdiction it proposes as a constituent jurisdiction. Each such jurisdiction shall hold a referendum on ratification.

A jurisdiction ratifies this Constitution if a majority of the votes cast in its referendum approve it.

Every ratification referendum shall be free, fair, and secret, and shall satisfy the standards of III.1 and III.2 as far as they can apply before this Constitution takes effect.

Every adult who would be a citizen under VII.1 when this Constitution takes effect may vote in the referendum of the jurisdiction where the person resides.

A jurisdiction may hold its referendum at any time within one year after the Constitution is submitted to it.

### VIII.2.b — Entry into Force

This Constitution takes effect on the thirtieth day after it has been ratified by at least two-thirds of the jurisdictions to which it was submitted, if those ratifying jurisdictions together contain a majority of the total population of all the jurisdictions to which it was submitted.

Population shall be determined by the most recent official count available in each jurisdiction.

This Constitution binds only the jurisdictions that ratify it.

A jurisdiction that ratifies after this Constitution takes effect, but within the period in VIII.2.a, becomes an original constituent jurisdiction (VII.2.a) on the thirtieth day after its ratification. A jurisdiction may join after that period only by admission under VII.2.e.

## Section 3 — Transition to the First Government

### VIII.3.a — Interim Government

From the day this Constitution takes effect until the first President and the first Congress take office, the Founding Convention shall act as the interim national government.

The interim government is bound by this Constitution, including Articles I, II, III, and X.

The interim government shall exercise only the powers necessary to:

1. conduct the first selections and elections under this Section;
2. maintain essential public services and public order; and
3. conduct the national government's ordinary business until the first Congress and President take office.

The interim government shall not declare war, ratify a treaty, or amend this Constitution. It may use force only to repel an armed attack.

Every law the interim government enacts expires ninety days after the first Congress convenes, unless Congress reenacts it.

The officials of each constituent jurisdiction remain in office until their successors are elected under Articles III and VII, and are bound by this Constitution in the meantime.

A member of the Founding Convention may be a candidate in the first general election only after resigning from the Convention. The resignation shall take effect before the person files as a candidate.

### VIII.3.b — First Selections

Within sixty days after this Constitution takes effect, the interim government shall conduct the first public drawings of the Election Authority and the districting commission under III.3.f. For these first drawings, any person eligible to vote in a ratification referendum under VIII.2.a may apply. Challenges to eligibility shall be heard by the courts of the constituent jurisdictions.

Within one hundred eighty days after this Constitution takes effect, the interim government shall fix the number of Representatives, and the districting commission shall adopt the first House districts, apportioned under VII.2.b, and shall assign every constituent jurisdiction to one of the nine judicial circuits under VI.2.j. The assignment is subject to judicial review for compliance with VI.2.j.

The Judicial Conduct Commission shall be selected under VI.4.d within one year after the first Congress convenes.

### VIII.3.c — First Elections

The Election Authority shall hold the first general election within one year after this Constitution takes effect. The interim government shall not postpone it (III.9.e).

At the first general election, voters shall elect the President and Vice President, every member of the House of Representatives, every Senator, and all nine judges of the Supreme Court.

For each constituent jurisdiction, lot shall assign one of its two Senate seats a first term of two years, so that one-half of the Senate is elected every two years (IV.3.d). The first Supreme Court terms are assigned under VI.2.e.

Every person elected at the first general election takes office on the seventy-fifth day after the election.

### VIII.3.d — Term Limits

Service in any office before this Constitution takes effect, and service in the interim government, does not count toward any term limit under this Constitution.

A shortened first term assigned under this Section, VI.2.e, or VII.2.e, does not count as a full term for purposes of III.5.b, except as VI.2.e provides for the Supreme Court.

### VIII.3.e — Existing Law

The laws of each constituent jurisdiction remain in force to the extent they are consistent with this Constitution. Any law, or part of a law, inconsistent with this Constitution has no legal effect from the day this Constitution takes effect.

**Hidden provisions in inherited law:** Every law that remains in force under this subsection shall meet the single-subject rule of IV.5.c, measured against the law as it was enacted, whether by a legislature, by the voters, or by any other lawmaking authority. A provision that does not relate to the subject stated in the law's title, or, where the title states none, to the matter its principal provisions address, is an unrelated provision.

1. **Review:** Within one year after this Constitution takes effect, each constituent jurisdiction shall publish a list of every provision of its laws that may be unrelated, with the reasons.
2. **Re-enactment:** An unrelated provision keeps its effect until two years after the first Congress convenes (VIII.3.c), so that it may be enacted again in a law whose subject it relates to, by the jurisdiction's legislature, or by Congress where the matter is now within federal jurisdiction. At the end of that period it has no legal effect, whether or not it was listed or challenged.
3. **Challenge:** Any person affected by an unrelated provision may ask a court to decide whether it is unrelated, at any time. A finding takes effect at the end of that period, or on the date of the finding if later.
4. **Restored law:** A law that an unrelated provision amended, repealed, or suspended is restored as it stood before. A restored offense or penalty applies only to conduct after it is restored (II.7.a). A final judgment, completed transaction, or payment made before then stands.
5. **Reliance:** No person shall be penalized for an act taken in good faith reliance on an unrelated provision before it lost effect.

For a jurisdiction that joins after this Constitution takes effect (VIII.2.b, VII.2.e), every period in this paragraph runs from the day it joins.

The debts and obligations of a constituent jurisdiction remain its own unless Congress by law provides otherwise.

A treaty or agreement between a constituent jurisdiction and a foreign government lapses one year after this Constitution takes effect, unless Concordia adopts it under V.7.b. *(See VII.3.d.)*

The existing courts of each constituent jurisdiction continue, and may hear federal questions, until Congress establishes inferior courts under VI.1.b.

### VIII.3.f — Persons in Custody

Every person in custody on the day this Constitution takes effect is entitled to judicial review of that custody under II.3.c within ninety days, and to the protections of Article II from that day forward.

No person shall remain in custody for conduct that is no longer criminal, or under a sentence this Constitution prohibits. *(See II.7.a and II.7.d.)*

### VIII.3.g — Existing Citizens

Every person who, on the day this Constitution takes effect, is a citizen of a ratifying jurisdiction under that jurisdiction's law becomes a citizen of Concordia on that day.

For purposes of VII.1.a, a parent who was a citizen of a ratifying jurisdiction when a person was born before that day is treated as having been a citizen of Concordia.

This subsection applies in the same way to a jurisdiction that joins later under VIII.2.b or VII.2.e, on the day it joins.
