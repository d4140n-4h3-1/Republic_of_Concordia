# ARTICLE X — THE RIGHT TO KEEP AND BEAR ARMS

## Section 0 — Why This Article Is Last

### X.0.a — The Last Safeguard

This Article is placed last because it is the last safeguard of this Constitution.

Every Article before it is meant to make this one unnecessary: rights that no amendment may reduce, independent elections, fixed and independent courts, limited emergency powers, civilian control of the armed forces, and a demanding process for amendment. The people should first defend this Constitution by voting, speaking, assembling, going to court, and amending.

The right in this Article exists for the defense of persons and, if every other safeguard has failed, for the defense of this constitutional order itself. It is not a means of settling political disagreement.

---

## Section 1 — The Right

### X.1.a — Right to Keep and Bear Arms

**Right:** Every person who has reached the age of majority (I.4.a) and lawfully resides in the United States shall have the right to keep and bear arms, subject only to the conditions of this Article.

**Definitions:**

* **Arms** means weapons that an individual can carry and use, including firearms, ammunition, and their parts.
* **Arms** does not include explosive, incendiary, chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear weapons, or any weapon designed to fire more than one shot with a single function of the trigger. These may be regulated or prohibited by law.
* **Keep** means to possess arms in one's home or property.
* **Bear** means to carry arms outside one's home or property.

The purposes of this right include defense of self and others under I.3.a, defense of the home, hunting, sport, and the last resort in X.4.

### X.1.b — Standard

The conditions in Section 2 are express constitutional exceptions and apply as written. Every other restriction on this right shall satisfy I.4.c.

---

## Section 2 — Conditions

### X.2.a — License to Purchase

A law may require a person to hold a license to purchase before acquiring arms by sale or other transfer. No license shall be required to keep or bear arms that a person lawfully holds.

A license to purchase shall be issued to every applicant who:

1. is not a prohibited person under X.2.b;
2. passes a background check; and
3. completes a course in safe handling, storage, and the law of self-defense.

The course shall be offered free of charge, at reasonable times and places, and shall not exceed `[DECIDE: maximum course length in hours]`.

A license to purchase is valid for five years and may be renewed. It may be suspended only while the holder is a prohibited person under X.2.b.

The license shall be issued or denied within thirty days. A license not acted on within that time is issued by operation of law. A denial shall be in writing, shall state the reason, and is subject to judicial review.

The fee shall not exceed the reasonable cost of processing, and shall be waived for any applicant for whom it would be a hardship.

No license shall be denied for any reason not stated in this subsection.

### X.2.b — Prohibited Persons

A person is prohibited from keeping or bearing arms only while the person:

1. is serving a sentence, including parole or probation, for a violent offense;
2. has been convicted of a violent offense against a family or household member, or of a violent offense committed with a weapon, and has not had the right restored under X.2.c;
3. is subject to a protective order issued after notice and a hearing, in which a court found that the person is a credible threat to the physical safety of a specific person; or
4. has been found by a court, after notice, a hearing, and the opportunity to be represented by counsel, to present a substantial and imminent danger of physical harm to themselves or others. Such a finding lasts no more than one year, and may be renewed only after a new hearing.

In an emergency, a court may issue a temporary order under item 4 without prior notice, but a full hearing shall be held within fourteen days.

No person shall be prohibited because of protected status (I.4.a), viewpoint, political belief or affiliation, or the lawful exercise of any other constitutional right.

### X.2.c — Restoration

A person prohibited under X.2.b(2) may petition a court for restoration of the right after five years without a further violent offense. The court shall restore the right unless the government shows that the person remains a danger to the physical safety of others.

### X.2.d — Places

A law may prohibit bearing arms, other than by law enforcement officers on duty, in:

1. polling places, counting centers, and offices of the Election Authority, during voting and counting;
2. courthouses;
3. the chambers of any legislature while in session;
4. schools and their grounds;
5. places of custody; and
6. secured areas of airports and other places where every person is screened for weapons.

A law may also prohibit bearing arms at a public assembly or demonstration, if the law applies equally to every assembly regardless of viewpoint.

Any other prohibition on bearing arms in a place shall satisfy I.4.c.

### X.2.e — Storage

A law may require that arms be stored securely, by a lock or a locked container, wherever a minor or a prohibited person lives or is likely to have access to them. Such a law shall not prevent a person from having arms immediately available for self-defense.

### X.2.f — Transfers

A law may require that arms be sold or otherwise transferred only to a person who holds a valid license to purchase, confirmed at the time of transfer, other than a transfer between family members or a temporary transfer for immediate self-defense, hunting, or sport under the transferor's supervision.

Confirming a license shall show only whether it is valid. No record of the arms transferred shall be sent to or kept by any government (X.3.b).

### X.2.g — Minors

A minor may use arms under the direct supervision of a parent, legal guardian, or licensed instructor, for training, hunting, or sport.

## Section 3 — Protections Against Abuse

### X.3.a — No General Disarmament

No government shall order the general surrender, confiscation, or destruction of lawfully held arms.

Arms may be seized only:

1. from a prohibited person under X.2.b, by court order;
2. as evidence, under a particularized warrant (II.6.c) or a lawful exception to the warrant requirement; or
3. where the arms themselves are not arms under X.1.a, or are unlawfully possessed.

Arms seized as evidence from a person who is not prohibited shall be returned when they are no longer needed as evidence.

### X.3.b — No Registry of Arms

No government shall keep a central list of the arms owned by any person. Records of licenses and background checks are stored personal information under II.6.e, shall be used only to carry out this Article, and shall not be disclosed except under a particularized warrant.

### X.3.c — Equal Application

No condition of this Article shall be applied selectively because of protected status, viewpoint, political belief or affiliation, or the lawful exercise of any other constitutional right.

### X.3.d — Taxes

No tax or fee on arms, ammunition, or licenses shall be imposed for the purpose of making them unaffordable.

---

## Section 4 — The Last Resort

### X.4.a — Right of Resistance

Against any person or group who seeks to abolish this constitutional order by force or by other unconstitutional means, every citizen has the right to resist, including by arms, when no other remedy is available.

### X.4.b — Limits

The right of resistance does not apply to:

1. disagreement with a law, policy, election result, or judicial decision made under this Constitution;
2. any wrong that a court, an election, impeachment, or another process under this Constitution remains able to remedy; or
3. any act against a person who is not taking part in the attempt to abolish this constitutional order.

The right of resistance exists only to restore this Constitution, and ends when the constitutional order is restored.

### X.4.c — Intimidation Prohibited

No person shall use, display, or carry arms to intimidate a voter, a candidate, an election official, a judge, a juror, a witness, a legislator, or any other official in the performance of a constitutional duty.

No private armed group shall act as police or as a military force, or take part in elections or the enforcement of law. This does not prohibit lawful clubs for sport, hunting, or training.

---

## Section 5 — Status of This Article

### X.5.a — Protection

The right established by this Article binds every level of government (VII.3.c), may be enforced by Congress (IV.1.b), shall not be suspended or narrowed by an emergency declaration (V.6.c), and is protected from amendment in the same manner as the rights of Articles I, II, and III (IX.3.a).
