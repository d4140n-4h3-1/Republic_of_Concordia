# ARTICLE III — DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENT AND ELECTIONS

## Section 1 — Voting Rights

### III.1.a — Right to Vote

**Right:** Every citizen who has reached the age of majority (I.4.a) shall have the right to vote in elections for which the citizen is legally eligible.

**Definitions:**

* **Citizen** means a person recognized as a citizen under this Constitution and applicable constitutional law.
* **Voter** means a citizen legally eligible to vote in a particular election.
* **Election** means a constitutionally authorized process through which voters select candidates for public office or decide a matter submitted to the electorate.
* **Voting right** means the right of an eligible voter to cast a ballot and have that ballot counted according to law.
* **Legal eligibility** means satisfaction of constitutionally established requirements for voting in the particular election.

No government shall deny, abridge, or substantially burden the voting right except pursuant to an express constitutional provision that also satisfies I.4.c.

Voter registration and identity verification procedures are permitted when reasonably necessary to administer elections, provided they do not impose unnecessary barriers to voting.

No person's vote shall receive greater or lesser legal weight because of political belief, political affiliation, protected status, wealth, property ownership, or social status.

### III.1.b — Voting Rights During Custody

Every otherwise eligible citizen shall retain the right to vote while incarcerated, detained, arrested, awaiting trial, or otherwise in government custody.

A conviction, sentence, arrest, detention, pending criminal proceeding, or other custodial status shall not, by itself, suspend or diminish the voting right.

Government shall provide reasonable means for persons in custody to exercise their voting rights.

No criminal sentence or custodial order may suspend the voting right unless this Constitution expressly authorizes such suspension.

### III.1.c — Equal Suffrage

**Right:** Each lawful vote shall have substantially equal weight.

**Definitions:**

* **Equal suffrage** means the principle that eligible voters possess substantially equal voting power.
* **Vote dilution** means an intentional or objectively demonstrated governmental action that substantially reduces the effective voting power of a group of eligible voters without constitutional justification.

Government shall not intentionally or substantially dilute the voting power of eligible voters without constitutional justification.

An electoral district or electoral system that produces a substantial and durable dilution of a group's voting power shall be presumed constitutionally invalid unless the government demonstrates that the result is reasonably necessary to satisfy neutral constitutional criteria.

No electoral district shall be created or maintained for the purpose of substantially diluting the votes of a group of eligible voters sharing a protected status (I.4.a).

**Fixed constituencies:** The election of two Senators by each constituent jurisdiction (IV.3.a) and the election of one Supreme Court judge by each judicial circuit (VI.2.f) are express constitutional exceptions to this subsection. Differences in population among constituent jurisdictions or among circuits do not violate it.

### III.1.d — Secret Ballot

**Right:** Every voter shall have the right to cast a ballot in secrecy.

Government shall establish reasonable safeguards preventing officials, candidates, political organizations, employers, or other persons from determining how an individual voter voted, except where disclosure is voluntarily authorized by the voter or constitutionally required for the investigation of unlawful conduct.

No person shall be required to disclose how that person voted as a condition of exercising any constitutional right or receiving any governmental benefit.

### III.1.e — Freedom of Electoral Participation

**Right:** Every eligible voter shall have the right to participate peacefully in elections without governmental retaliation.

Government shall not punish, threaten, intimidate, coerce, or discriminate against a person because the person:

1. votes;
2. declines to vote;
3. supports or opposes a candidate;
4. supports or opposes a political party;
5. participates in lawful political activity; or
6. expresses a lawful political opinion.

### III.1.f — Accessibility of Voting

Government shall provide reasonable means for eligible voters to exercise their voting rights.

Election procedures shall not impose unnecessary barriers to voting.

Reasonable accommodations shall be provided where necessary to permit persons with disabilities or other constitutionally recognized limitations to participate independently and meaningfully.

### III.1.g — Constitutional Protection of Voting Rights

No statute, regulation, administrative action, custodial order, criminal sentence, election procedure, or governmental practice may suspend, diminish, or circumvent a voting right protected by this Article except where this Constitution expressly permits it and the restriction satisfies I.4.c.

Where a governmental action has multiple possible applications, it shall be interpreted and applied consistently with the greater protection of the voting right.

The government bears the burden of demonstrating constitutional authority for any restriction of a voting right.

## Section 2 — Elections

### III.2.a — Free and Fair Elections

**Right:** Every election shall be free, fair, regular, transparent, and administered according to law.

**Definitions:**

* **Free election** means an election in which voters may make electoral choices without governmental coercion or unlawful intimidation.
* **Fair election** means an election conducted according to neutral rules that provide eligible participants a meaningful and substantially equal opportunity to participate.
* **Regular election** means an election conducted at constitutionally established intervals.
* **Transparent election** means an election whose material procedures, counting methods, records, and results are reasonably available for lawful public examination.
* **Election integrity** means the preservation of the lawful eligibility, secrecy, accuracy, security, administration, counting, certification, and review of an election.
* **Unlawful electoral advantage** means a benefit to a candidate, political party, incumbent, or faction obtained through conduct that violates this Constitution, or through manipulation of rules, boundaries, timing, or administration that is not justified by a neutral and publicly stated purpose.

No government shall manipulate an election for the purpose of securing an unlawful electoral advantage.

Government shall administer elections neutrally and shall not favor or disfavor a candidate, political party, political belief, or electoral faction.

### III.2.b — Ballot Access

**Right:** A qualified candidate shall have a reasonable opportunity to appear on the ballot.

**Definitions:**

* **Qualified candidate** means a person who satisfies the constitutional and lawful qualifications for the office sought.
* **Ballot requirement** means a condition that a candidate must satisfy to appear on an election ballot.

Ballot requirements shall be neutral, publicly established, uniformly applied, and reasonably related to legitimate electoral administration.

Government shall not establish or enforce a ballot requirement whose primary purpose is to prevent an otherwise qualified candidate from seeking elected office.

A ballot requirement shall not be applied selectively to advantage or disadvantage a particular candidate, political party, incumbent, or electoral faction.

Material ballot requirements shall be established sufficiently before the election to permit qualified candidates and voters to understand and comply with them.

### III.2.c — Election Administration

Election administration shall be conducted by the Election Authority and election administrators acting on its behalf, operating under neutral and publicly established rules.

Election administrators shall:

1. administer elections according to this Constitution and law;
2. protect the integrity and secrecy of ballots;
3. maintain accurate election records;
4. count lawful ballots accurately;
5. provide required recount and audit procedures;
6. certify results according to established law; and
7. remain subject to judicial review.

No election administrator may alter constitutional election requirements through administrative practice.

No election administrator may selectively enforce an election rule for the purpose of advantaging or disadvantaging a candidate, political party, incumbent, or electoral faction.

Administrative convenience shall not justify violation of a constitutional voting right or election requirement.

### III.2.d — Election Rules

Material election rules shall be established sufficiently before an election to permit voters, candidates, political organizations, and election administrators to understand and comply with them.

**Material election rule** means a rule governing voter eligibility, candidate eligibility, ballot access, voting procedures, ballot counting, certification, recounts, or another matter capable of materially affecting an election.

No government shall materially alter an election procedure for the purpose of advantaging or disadvantaging a candidate, political party, incumbent, or identifiable electoral faction.

Once voting in an election has begun, no material election rule shall be changed for that election except:

1. to correct a demonstrated constitutional violation;
2. to correct an objectively demonstrated administrative error;
3. to address an objectively demonstrated circumstance that makes compliance with the existing rule impossible; or
4. where the change is otherwise expressly authorized by this Constitution.

A change under this subsection shall be limited to what is reasonably necessary to restore or preserve constitutional election integrity.

This subsection does not prevent a court from ordering a correction under III.4.d or III.9.b.

### III.2.e — Election Integrity

Government shall establish procedures reasonably designed to preserve the integrity of every election.

Such procedures shall protect against:

1. unlawful alteration, destruction, concealment, or substitution of ballots;
2. inaccurate counting or tabulation;
3. unlawful interference with ballot secrecy;
4. unauthorized access to election systems or materials;
5. unlawful manipulation of election records;
6. fraudulent certification of election results; and
7. other intentional conduct materially designed to corrupt the lawful administration or result of an election.

Election-security measures shall themselves comply with this Constitution and shall not unnecessarily burden the voting right.

No election-security measure may be used as a pretext to suppress lawful electoral participation or provide an unlawful electoral advantage.

Where election integrity and another constitutional right come into conflict, the government shall use the least restrictive lawful means reasonably available to protect election integrity.

Nothing in this section shall permit the government to create a power not otherwise granted by this Constitution.

## Section 3 — Independent Election Authority

### III.3.a — Establishment

An independent constitutional election authority shall administer and oversee elections as provided by this Constitution.

**Definitions:**

* **Election Authority** means the constitutionally established institution responsible for administering and overseeing elections.
* **Election administrator** means a person exercising lawful authority on behalf of the Election Authority.
* **Election oversight** means supervision of election administration for compliance with this Constitution and applicable law.

The Election Authority shall exercise only powers granted by this Constitution or by legislation consistent with this Constitution.

### III.3.b — Independence

The Election Authority shall operate independently of candidates, political parties, elected officials, and other persons whose electoral interests could materially conflict with its constitutional duties.

No elected official, candidate, political party, or governmental institution may direct the Election Authority to alter an election result, ballot determination, or other official electoral decision for political advantage, or direct the districting commission (III.4.c) to alter a district boundary for political advantage.

### III.3.c — Election Authority Duties

The Election Authority shall establish and administer procedures necessary to:

1. register eligible voters;
2. administer elections;
3. maintain election records;
4. establish uniform minimum ballot standards;
5. oversee counting and certification;
6. conduct or supervise required audits and recounts;
7. enforce constitutional election requirements within its lawful authority;
8. investigate suspected election violations;
9. preserve election materials; and
10. provide public information concerning election procedures.

Legislation may establish additional administrative procedures but shall not diminish constitutional voting rights.

### III.3.d — Removal and Discipline of Election Administrators

An election administrator shall not be removed, disciplined, threatened, or otherwise penalized solely for faithfully performing a constitutional election duty.

Removal or discipline may occur for corruption, serious misconduct, incapacity, neglect of constitutional duty, or other constitutionally or lawfully established grounds under a procedure providing due process.

No disciplinary procedure may be used as retaliation for an election administrator's faithful enforcement of this Constitution.

### III.3.e — Composition and Funding

The Election Authority shall consist of nine members serving staggered six-year terms.

No more than three members may be affiliated with the same political party at any time.

Members shall be selected under III.3.f, a process designed to prevent control of the Election Authority by any single political party, candidate, elected official, or political faction.

A member shall not simultaneously hold elected office, serve as a candidate for elected office, or exercise leadership of a political party.

The Election Authority shall receive funding sufficient to perform its constitutional duties.

Funding shall not be reduced or conditioned for the purpose of influencing an election decision or retaliating against the Authority for faithful constitutional enforcement.

### III.3.f — Selection of Members

This subsection governs the selection of members of the Election Authority and of the districting commission (III.4.c).

**Definitions:**
* **Affiliated** means registered or enrolled as a member of a political party, holding office in a political party, or having been either at any time during the preceding four years. A person affiliated with more than one party during that period counts as affiliated with each.
* **Selection pool** means the list of applicants verified as eligible for a body.

**Eligibility:** Any voter may apply. An applicant is ineligible if, at any time during the preceding four years, the applicant was an elected official, a candidate for elected office, a lobbyist, or an employee of a political party or candidate, or is otherwise barred by III.3.e or III.4.c. No person may serve on both bodies at once.

**Verification:** Eligibility shall be determined only by the objective criteria in this subsection. The list of applicants and the reasons for any rejection shall be made public. Any person may challenge a determination of eligibility in court before the selection is held.

**Selection by lot:** Members shall be selected from the selection pool by public random drawing. A drawn applicant who would cause more than three members to be affiliated with the same political party shall be passed over, and the drawing shall continue.

**Staggering:** Three seats on each body shall be filled every two years. For the first selection, all nine members shall be drawn, and lot shall assign three of them to terms of two years, three to terms of four years, and three to full six-year terms.

**Vacancies:** A vacancy shall be filled for the remainder of the term by drawing from the most recent selection pool, under the same rules.

**Service:** No person may serve more than one full term on the same body.

**Removal:** A member may be removed only by impeachment (IV.9) or, for incapacity, by order of a court of competent jurisdiction.

## Section 4 — Districting and Gerrymandering

### III.4.a — Prohibition of Gerrymandering

**Right:** Electoral districts shall not be intentionally manipulated to produce an unlawful electoral advantage.

**Definitions:**

* **Electoral district** means a geographically defined area whose voters collectively elect one or more representatives.
* **Districting** means the establishment or revision of electoral district boundaries.
* **Gerrymandering** means intentional manipulation of electoral district boundaries to provide an unlawful electoral advantage to a political party, candidate, or incumbent, or to substantially dilute the voting power of a group of eligible voters. A district drawn to remedy or prevent unlawful vote dilution is not gerrymandering.
* **Partisan gerrymandering** means gerrymandering intended to provide an unlawful electoral advantage based on political party or political affiliation.
* **Incumbent protection** means manipulation of district boundaries primarily to preserve a particular officeholder's electoral position.
* **Vote dilution** has the meaning given in III.1.c.

Partisan gerrymandering, intentional incumbent protection, and intentional vote dilution are prohibited.

A districting plan that produces a substantial and durable partisan advantage, or substantially dilutes the voting power of an identifiable group, shall be presumed constitutionally invalid unless the government demonstrates that the result follows from neutral districting criteria and is reasonably necessary to satisfy this Constitution.

### III.4.b — Neutral Districting

Electoral districts shall be established according to neutral, publicly disclosed criteria.

Electoral districts shall contain substantially equal population, except that a constituent jurisdiction guaranteed a single House seat by VII.2.b forms one district regardless of its population. This exception is the only permitted departure from equal population for House districts, and it does not violate III.1.c.

Districting shall, where reasonably practicable:

1. remain geographically contiguous;
2. maintain reasonable geographic compactness;
3. respect established geographic boundaries; and
4. avoid unnecessary division of communities.

Districting shall not intentionally protect incumbents or manipulate districts for partisan advantage.

No district shall be drawn with the primary purpose of predetermining an electoral outcome.

### III.4.c — Independent Districting

District boundaries shall be established by an independent districting commission operating under constitutionally established standards.

No legislator, elected executive, political party, candidate, or incumbent shall possess unilateral authority to establish electoral districts for the purpose of securing an electoral advantage.

The districting commission shall consist of nine members serving staggered six-year terms, selected under III.3.f.

No more than three members may be affiliated with the same political party at any time.

No member may be a current elected official, candidate, lobbyist for a political party, or incumbent whose electoral district would be affected by the commission's work.

The commission shall publish proposed district boundaries and the objective criteria used to establish them.

The commission shall provide reasonable public opportunity to review and comment upon proposed districts before final adoption.

The commission shall be funded sufficiently to perform its constitutional duties, and its funding shall not be manipulated for political advantage.

### III.4.d — Invalid Districts

A district established in violation of this Constitution shall have no legal effect to the extent of the constitutional violation.

A court with proper jurisdiction may order correction of an unconstitutional district and may require adoption of a lawful district before the affected election proceeds.

No unconstitutional district shall become constitutional solely because it has been used in a prior election.

## Section 5 — Terms of Elected Office

### III.5.a — Four-Year Terms

**Rule:** Except where this Constitution expressly establishes a different term, an elected public office shall have a term of four years.

**Definitions:**

* **Elected office** means a public office filled directly by voters through a constitutionally authorized election.
* **Term** means the constitutionally established period during which an elected official holds office.
* **Full term** means the entire constitutionally established period of an office.
* **Partial term** means service in an office for less than its full constitutionally established period.
* **Same elected office** means an elected office possessing substantially the same constitutional powers, jurisdiction, and constituency, regardless of a change in title or administrative structure.

Term limits for ordinary elected offices are governed by III.5.b and III.5.c.

### III.5.b — Term Limits

**Term limit:** No person may serve more than two terms in the same ordinary elected office. A term counts toward this limit if the person was elected to it or served more than one-half of it under III.5.c.

The limitation applies whether the terms are consecutive or separated by any period of absence.

A person shall not evade the term limit by:

1. temporarily resigning;
2. changing political affiliation;
3. seeking election under a different title for substantially the same constitutional office;
4. transferring authority to a subordinate and later returning;
5. using succession procedures for the purpose of avoiding the limit; or
6. otherwise restructuring the office without changing its constitutional substance.

### III.5.c — Partial Terms

A person who assumes an office through succession, vacancy, or special election shall serve the remainder of the existing term.

A partial term shall count toward the term limit when the person serves more than one-half of the full term, whether the person assumed office through succession, appointment, or special election, unless this Constitution expressly provides otherwise.

### III.5.d — Completion of Term

A person's eligibility to seek future office shall not affect the person's right to complete a lawfully acquired term.

Except where this Constitution expressly provides otherwise, reaching an age limit after election shall not terminate an existing term.

### III.5.e — Election Schedule and Start of Terms

A general election for federal offices shall be held every two years, on a date established by law in advance. The first general election is held under VIII.3.c.

Every federal term filled at a general election begins on the seventy-fifth day after that election, unless this Constitution expressly provides otherwise.

## Section 6 — Eligibility for Elected Office

### III.6.a — Age Eligibility

**Rule:** No person younger than thirty years of age, or sixty-five years of age or older, may be elected to an ordinary elected office.

**Definitions:**

* **Age at election** means the person's age on the date the election is conducted.
* **Ordinary elected office** means an elected public office other than a Supreme Court judgeship governed by Section 7.

A person elected while within the constitutional age range may complete the person's full term even if the person reaches or exceeds sixty-five years of age during that term.

### III.6.b — Other Qualifications

Additional qualifications for elected office may be established only by this Constitution or by legislation consistent with this Constitution.

No qualification may exclude an otherwise eligible person on the basis of protected status unless it satisfies I.4.c, other than the age requirements established in this Article.

No qualification shall be imposed for the primary purpose of preventing an otherwise eligible candidate from seeking office.

### III.6.c — Oath and Constitutional Duty

Every elected official shall, before exercising the powers of office, swear or affirm loyalty to this Constitution and a duty to faithfully perform the responsibilities of the office.

No elected official may lawfully exercise authority inconsistent with this Constitution merely because a statute, political organization, superior official, or electoral majority demands it.

## Section 7 — Supreme Court

### III.7.a — Supreme Court Judges

**Rule:** The Supreme Court shall consist of judges elected by the people of the judicial circuits (VI.2.f) according to procedures established by this Constitution and lawful election legislation.

**Definitions:**

* **Supreme Court judge** means a member of the highest constitutional court established under this Constitution.
* **Judicial election** means an election in which qualified voters select a person to serve as a judge.
* **Judicial term** means the constitutionally established period of service of a judge.

The Supreme Court shall consist of nine judges, one elected by the voters of each of the nine judicial circuits.

No Supreme Court judge shall be appointed by the executive, legislature, or any other governmental authority.

### III.7.b — Supreme Court Age Eligibility

A person shall be at least thirty-five years of age and younger than forty-seven years of age at the time of election to the Supreme Court.

No person who is forty-seven years of age or older may be elected to the Supreme Court.

This age requirement exists to permit completion of the full eighteen-year constitutional judicial term before the person reaches sixty-five years of age.

### III.7.c — Eighteen-Year Judicial Term

Each Supreme Court judge shall serve a single term of eighteen years.

A Supreme Court judge shall not be elected to a second eighteen-year term.

A judge who completes the constitutional term shall leave the Supreme Court upon expiration of the term unless this Constitution expressly provides otherwise.

### III.7.d — Judicial Independence

Supreme Court judges shall exercise judicial authority independently and impartially.

No elected official, political party, governmental institution, or private person may direct a Supreme Court judge to decide a case in a particular manner.

Judicial independence shall not exempt a judge from constitutional accountability or lawful disciplinary procedures.

### III.7.e — Judicial Incompatibility

A Supreme Court judge shall not simultaneously hold another elected office or exercise legislative or executive authority.

A person who has served as a Supreme Court judge shall not, for two years after leaving the Court, hold another elected or appointed governmental office.

Nothing in this subsection prohibits a former judge from engaging in private employment, private practice, teaching, scholarship, or other lawful private activity.

### III.7.f — Vacancies and Special Elections

A vacancy on the Supreme Court shall be filled by special election, held in the circuit whose seat is vacant, for the remainder of the unexpired judicial term.

The special election shall occur within six months after the vacancy unless an objectively demonstrated circumstance makes an election within that period impossible.

A person elected to fill a vacancy shall satisfy the age and qualification requirements applicable to a Supreme Court election at the time of the special election.

Service of one-half or less of an unexpired judicial term shall not count as a full judicial term for purposes of III.7.c.

Service of more than one-half of an unexpired judicial term shall count as the person's single judicial term.

### III.7.g — Judicial Qualifications

A candidate for Supreme Court judge shall:

1. be a citizen;
2. have reached thirty-five years of age and be younger than forty-seven years of age at election;
3. be licensed to practice law within the jurisdiction;
4. have at least ten years of professional legal or judicial experience;
5. have resided in the circuit whose seat is to be filled for at least one year immediately before the election; and
6. satisfy all other qualifications established consistently with this Constitution.

No qualification may be imposed for the purpose of controlling the political composition or judicial decisions of the Court.

## Section 8 — Election Integrity and Transparency

### III.8.a — Accurate Counting

Every lawful ballot shall be counted accurately.

The Election Authority shall establish procedures reasonably designed to:

1. prevent unlawful alteration or destruction of ballots;
2. detect counting errors;
3. preserve election records;
4. permit lawful recounts;
5. verify reported results; and
6. maintain a reliable record of the election.

### III.8.b — Election Records

Material election records shall be preserved for a constitutionally or lawfully established period sufficient to permit meaningful auditing, recounting, investigation, and judicial review.

Public access to election records may be limited only where necessary to protect ballot secrecy, personal privacy, election security, or another constitutional right.

### III.8.c — Election Audits

Elections shall be subject to reasonable post-election auditing procedures established before the election.

Audits shall be conducted according to neutral standards and shall not be selectively applied for political advantage.

### III.8.d — Election Fraud and Interference

**Definitions:**

* **Election fraud** means intentional unlawful conduct designed to corrupt, falsify, or materially misrepresent an election or its results.
* **Election interference** means intentional unlawful conduct designed to prevent, obstruct, coerce, corrupt, or materially distort lawful electoral participation or administration.
* **Ballot** means any paper, electronic, or other record by which a voter casts a vote, including a mail ballot, a provisional ballot, and the record produced by voting equipment.
* **Election record** means any record the Election Authority creates or keeps in administering an election, including voter registration records, poll books, ballots, tallies, audit records, chain-of-custody records, and certification documents.
* **Election system** means any equipment, software, network, database, or facility used to register voters, or to cast, store, transmit, count, or tabulate ballots, or to report or certify results.
* **Thing of value** means money, property, services, employment, a contract, a debt reduction, or any other material benefit. It does not include transportation to a polling place, time off work to vote, or food, drink, or comfort offered to everyone waiting to vote, when offered without regard to how a person will vote.
* **Intimidation** means using or threatening force, violence, economic harm, loss of employment, loss of housing, legal action known to be groundless, or disclosure of private information, to make a person act or refrain from acting in an election.
* **Election official** means an election administrator, a member or employee of the Election Authority, a member of the districting commission (III.4.c), or any person lawfully serving as a poll worker, counter, auditor, or observer.

Government shall establish penalties and procedures for election fraud and unlawful election interference consistent with due process.

No person shall be deprived of constitutional rights solely on the basis of an accusation of election misconduct without lawful procedure.

Penalties for election fraud or interference shall not restrict speech protected by I.1.a except as permitted by I.4.c.

### III.8.e — Prohibited Election Conduct

The following conduct, when done knowingly, is election fraud or election interference. Law shall make each punishable, with penalties consistent with II.7.

**Against the ballot:**

1. voting, or attempting to vote, in an election in which the person knows they are not eligible to vote;
2. voting, or attempting to vote, more than once in the same election;
3. casting, or attempting to cast, a ballot in the name of another person, living or dead, without that person's lawful authorization;
4. altering, destroying, concealing, substituting, or adding a ballot without lawful authority; or
5. obtaining or marking another voter's ballot without that voter's consent.

**Vote buying and coercion:**

6. giving, offering, or promising a thing of value in exchange for voting, not voting, or voting in a particular way;
7. requesting, accepting, or agreeing to accept a thing of value in exchange for voting, not voting, or voting in a particular way;
8. using intimidation against a person because the person votes, declines to vote, or votes in a particular way, or to make the person do so; or
9. requiring a person to reveal how they voted, or to show a marked ballot, as a condition of employment, housing, payment, or any other benefit (III.1.d).

**Against voters:**

10. using intimidation against a voter, or against a person lawfully helping a voter, in connection with registering or voting;
11. physically obstructing a voter's access to a polling place, ballot drop location, or registration office;
12. communicating to voters a statement of fact about the date, time, place, method, or eligibility requirements of voting, or about whether a voter's registration or ballot is valid, that the person knows is false, with the intent to cause voters not to vote or to cast an invalid ballot; or
13. filing a challenge to a voter's eligibility that the person knows is groundless, to prevent an eligible voter from voting.

**Against election administration:**

14. altering, destroying, concealing, or falsifying an election record without lawful authority;
15. accessing, altering, damaging, or disabling an election system without authorization;
16. reporting, tabulating, or certifying a result the person knows to be false;
17. refusing or failing to count, certify, or transmit a result as the law requires, for the purpose of changing or delaying the lawful result;
18. removing an eligible voter from the voter registration records, or refusing to register an eligible voter, for the purpose of preventing the voter from voting;
19. using intimidation against an election official to influence the performance of an official duty, or because of the official's lawful performance of that duty; or
20. directing, pressuring, or offering a thing of value to an election official to alter a result, ballot determination, or other official electoral decision (III.3.b).

**By election officials:**

21. applying or enforcing an election rule selectively to advantage or disadvantage a candidate, political party, incumbent, or electoral faction (III.2.c);
22. disclosing, or attempting to learn, how an individual voter voted, except as III.1.d permits; or
23. changing a material election rule after voting has begun, other than as III.2.d permits.

**Good-faith mistake:** No person shall be punished for voting, registering, or attempting to vote based on an honest and reasonable belief that they were eligible, including a belief based on information given by an election official. Such a vote may be corrected under the procedures of the Election Authority.

**False statements about voting:** Item 12 applies only to false statements of fact about how, when, where, or whether a person may vote. It does not apply to statements of opinion, political argument, predictions, satire, or claims about candidates, parties, or issues, however false or misleading. Item 12 is an express constitutional permission for purposes of I.4.c.

**Attempt and conspiracy:** Law may punish an attempt or a conspiracy to commit conduct listed in this subsection. Preparation alone is not an attempt. Mere association, communication, political cooperation, or shared political belief is not a conspiracy.

This subsection binds private actors as well as the government.

### III.8.f — Protections in Enforcement

The prosecution shall prove every element of an offense under III.8.e, including the required knowledge and intent, beyond a reasonable doubt (II.4.f).

No such offense shall be inferred solely from:

1. a clerical, registration, or administrative error;
2. a discrepancy between records that has an innocent explanation;
3. a person's political belief, party affiliation, or association; or
4. a person's protected status (I.4.a).

No person shall be removed from the voter registration records, denied a ballot, or have a ballot rejected because of an accusation of election misconduct, except after lawful procedure, notice, and an opportunity to be heard (II.1.a).

A challenge to a voter's eligibility filed by any person other than an election official shall state its factual basis under penalty of perjury.

No penalty for election fraud or interference shall suspend or diminish a person's voting right (III.1.b).

### III.8.g — Investigation

The Election Authority shall investigate suspected election fraud and interference (III.3.c) and shall refer a violation it finds probable to the appropriate prosecuting authority. Every election official who becomes aware of suspected election fraud or interference shall report it to the Election Authority.

An investigation shall not:

1. disclose how any individual voter voted, except as strictly necessary to investigate unlawful conduct concerning that ballot (III.1.d);
2. be opened, expanded, or ended to advantage or disadvantage a candidate, political party, incumbent, or electoral faction; or
3. delay the counting or certification of an election except by order of a court under Section 9.

After each election, the Election Authority shall publish a report stating the number of suspected violations investigated, referred, and found unfounded, without identifying any person who has not been charged.

### III.8.h — Reporting Persons and Remedies

No person shall be dismissed, disciplined, threatened, or otherwise penalized for reporting in good faith suspected election fraud or interference to the Election Authority, a court, or a prosecuting authority, or for testifying in a proceeding concerning it. An election official also retains the protection of III.3.d.

A voter, candidate, or election official harmed by conduct listed in III.8.e(6) through (23) may bring a civil action against the person responsible for damages, injunctive relief, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs. A court may issue an injunction during an election to stop ongoing intimidation or obstruction of voters.

A civil action under this subsection does not challenge the result of an election. An election result may be challenged only under Section 9.

## Section 9 — Election Challenges and Judicial Review

### III.9.a — Right to Challenge an Election

**Right:** A person directly and constitutionally affected by an alleged electoral violation shall have access to a judicial procedure capable of determining whether the election complied with this Constitution.

**Definitions:**

* **Election challenge** means a lawful proceeding seeking review or correction of an alleged constitutional or legal violation affecting an election.
* **Directly affected person** means a voter, candidate, officeholder, or other person whose legally protected electoral interest is materially affected by the alleged violation.

Government shall not prevent a lawful election challenge through unreasonable procedural barriers.

### III.9.b — Election Remedies

A court with proper jurisdiction may order a remedy reasonably necessary to correct an established constitutional violation, including:

1. correction of election records;
2. recount;
3. correction of a ballot or districting violation;
4. invalidation of an unconstitutional district;
5. correction or replacement of an unlawful electoral procedure;
6. a new election where necessary; or
7. another lawful remedy sufficient to restore constitutional compliance.

A remedy shall be proportionate to the established violation.

### III.9.c — Certification

Election results shall not be considered final until the constitutionally required counting, verification, and certification procedures have been completed.

Certification shall not prevent judicial review of a constitutional violation discovered afterward.

An election challenge shall ordinarily be filed within thirty days after certification.

Where fraud, concealment, or other intentional misconduct prevented reasonable discovery of the violation within that period, a challenge may be filed within one year after the violation was reasonably discoverable.

Absent fraud or intentional concealment, an election challenge shall not remove an official from office after the expiration of the challenged term.

Nothing in this subsection prevents judicial review or other remedies necessary to enforce a continuing constitutional violation.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Limits on Electoral Authority

### III.10.a — Constitutional Supremacy

**Right:** No governmental authority may diminish, circumvent, suspend, or nullify a right or limitation established by this Article except where this Constitution expressly permits it and the exception satisfies I.4.c.

No statute, regulation, administrative action, district map, election procedure, or governmental practice may create a broader power than this Constitution grants.

### III.10.b — Governmental Neutrality

Government shall remain neutral among lawful candidates, political parties, political beliefs, and electoral factions.

Government resources and authority shall not be used to provide an unlawful electoral advantage to a candidate, political party, incumbent, or political faction.

Government shall not condition governmental services, employment, benefits, contracts, licenses, or other governmental privileges upon political support or electoral participation.

### III.10.c — Anti-Circumvention

No governmental authority may evade a constitutional election requirement by:

1. changing the name of an office;
2. restructuring an office without changing its constitutional substance;
3. transferring authority to another governmental institution;
4. manipulating election timing;
5. manipulating district boundaries;
6. altering eligibility requirements for political advantage; or
7. creating an administrative procedure that produces substantially the same prohibited result.

Where a governmental action has multiple possible applications, it shall be interpreted and applied consistently with the greater protection of the constitutional right.

### III.10.d — Judicial Enforcement

The judiciary shall have authority to determine whether governmental actions affecting elections comply with this Constitution.

Where a governmental action conflicts with this Article, the Constitution shall prevail.

No official's claim that an unconstitutional electoral action was required by ordinary law shall prevent judicial correction of that action.

### III.10.e — Continuity of Democratic Government

Elections shall occur at constitutionally established intervals.

No election may be indefinitely postponed, cancelled, or replaced by appointment solely for governmental convenience, political advantage, or administrative difficulty.

Any constitutional emergency permitting alteration of an election shall be expressly authorized by this Constitution, limited to the circumstances expressly stated, and subject to constitutional limitations and judicial review.

No emergency declaration shall permanently suspend elections or convert an elected office into an appointed office unless another constitutional provision expressly authorizes such action.

A temporary inability to conduct an election shall not, by itself, extinguish the underlying voting right or permanently extend the authority of an elected official beyond the constitutionally authorized period.

## Section 11 — Campaign Finance and Foreign Interference

### III.11.a — Definitions

**Definitions:**

* **Contribution** means money, property, services, a loan, or another thing of value given or provided to a candidate, a candidate's campaign, or a political party for the purpose of influencing an election, including an electoral expenditure coordinated with a candidate, campaign, or party. A natural person's own volunteer time is not a contribution. A loan is a contribution to the extent it remains unpaid, unless made by a bank in the ordinary course of business on ordinary terms.
* **Coordinated** means made in cooperation, consultation, or concert with, or at the request or suggestion of, a candidate, campaign, or political party.
* **Electoral expenditure** means spending on a communication that:
  1. expressly advocates the election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate or political party; or
  2. refers to a clearly identified candidate and is distributed to voters within a period before an election established by law.
* **Independent expenditure** means an electoral expenditure that is not coordinated.
* **Organization** means a corporation, partnership, association, labor union, trust, or other entity that is not a natural person.
* **Citizen association** means an organization that is funded solely by contributions from natural persons who are citizens, each subject to the limits established under III.11.b, and that is not owned, controlled, or funded by any other organization, except as III.11.c permits for administrative costs.
* **News activity** means news reporting, commentary, or editorial content produced in the ordinary course of business by a press, broadcast, or online publication that is not owned or controlled by a candidate, campaign, or political party.
* **Foreign source** means:
  1. a foreign government, or a person acting on its behalf;
  2. a natural person who is not a citizen;
  3. an organization organized under the law of a foreign country or having its principal place of business outside the United States; or
  4. an organization in which foreign sources directly or indirectly hold a significant ownership interest, as defined by law, or in which a foreign source participates in deciding whether or how the organization makes a contribution or an expenditure concerning an election.
* **Ballot measure** means a matter submitted to the electorate for decision.
* **Candidate** means a person seeking an elected office in an election.
* **Campaign committee** means the single committee that a candidate designates to receive contributions and make expenditures on the candidate's behalf.
* **Election cycle** means, for a particular office, the period that begins on the day after the last general election for that office and ends on the day of the next general election for that office, including any special, runoff, or preliminary election held within that period.
* **Small contribution** means a contribution at or below an amount established by law, in total from one contributor to one recipient during an election cycle.
* **Federal office** means the office of President, Vice President, Senator, Representative, or judge of the Supreme Court.

*(Election and citizen are defined in III.1.a. Thing of value is defined in III.8.d.)*

### III.11.b — Contributions

**Natural persons:** Law shall limit the amount that a natural person may contribute to any one candidate or campaign committee per election cycle, to any one political party per calendar year, and to any one citizen association per calendar year.

**Organizations:** No organization shall make a contribution to a candidate, campaign committee, or political party, except that:

1. a political party may contribute to, and make coordinated expenditures for, its own candidates without limit; and
2. a citizen association may contribute to a candidate or campaign committee up to a limit established by law.

**Candidates' own funds:** A candidate may spend their own funds on their own campaign without limit, except as III.11.e provides for a candidate who accepts public matching funds.

**True source:** A contribution made through, or earmarked by way of, another person is a contribution by the original source, and counts toward both that source's limit and the intermediary's limit.

Every contribution limit shall:

1. apply equally to every candidate for the same office and to every political party;
2. be neutral as to viewpoint, political belief, and party;
3. be high enough to permit every candidate, including a challenger, to raise the funds necessary for an effective campaign;
4. not be designed or applied to favor incumbents; and
5. be adjusted for inflation at least every two years, as established by law.

This subsection is an express constitutional permission for purposes of I.4.c.

### III.11.c — Independent Expenditures

No organization shall make an independent expenditure in an election for federal office, except a political party or a citizen association.

Any organization may establish or sponsor a citizen association and pay its administrative costs. Administrative costs so paid are not a contribution, and shall not be used for any contribution or electoral expenditure.

No law shall limit:

1. the independent expenditures of a natural person who is a citizen from the person's own funds;
2. the independent expenditures of a citizen association;
3. news activity; or
4. communication by an organization about public issues that is not an electoral expenditure.

An independent expenditure that is in fact coordinated is a contribution and is subject to III.11.b.

Every person who makes independent expenditures remains subject to III.11.d.

This subsection is an express constitutional permission for purposes of I.4.c.

### III.11.d — Disclosure

**Reports:** Every campaign committee, political party, and citizen association shall file reports with the Election Authority at regular intervals established by law, and more frequently in the period immediately before an election. Each report shall state:

1. for each contributor whose contributions total more than a small contribution, the contributor's name, city, occupation, and the amount and date of each contribution;
2. the total amount of small contributions received; and
3. the amount, date, recipient, and purpose of each expenditure above an amount established by law.

**Independent expenditures and ballot measures:** Every person whose independent expenditures, or expenditures concerning a ballot measure, exceed an amount established by law shall report each such expenditure to the Election Authority promptly, within a period established by law. An organization making such a report shall also disclose the original source of each contribution or payment above an amount established by law whose funds were used for the expenditure, tracing funds through any intermediary, so that no contribution or expenditure may conceal its true source.

A person may give to an organization on the written condition that the gift not be used for electoral expenditures or expenditures concerning a ballot measure. A gift so restricted, and not so used, need not be disclosed under this subsection.

**Public database:** The Election Authority shall publish every report in a free, searchable public database promptly after receiving it, and before the election to which it relates.

**Small contributions:** Small contributions shall not be publicly disclosed in a manner that identifies the contributor. The Election Authority shall keep records of them sufficient to enforce this Section.

**Paid-for statements:** Every communication paid for by an electoral expenditure or an expenditure concerning a ballot measure shall state clearly who paid for it. A communication paid for by an organization shall also name its largest original sources of funds for the communication, as established by law.

**Advertising records:** A person who sells advertising and receives more than an amount established by law for electoral communications or communications concerning a ballot measure shall keep a public record of each such purchase, stating the buyer, the amount, the dates distributed, and the candidate or ballot measure concerned.

**Exemption:** A court shall exempt a contributor from public identification where the contributor shows a reasonable probability that disclosure would expose them to threats, harassment, or reprisal because of the contribution. The application and the contributor's identity shall be sealed. The exempted information shall remain available to the Election Authority.

### III.11.e — Public Financing

**Program:** A program of public matching funds is established for candidates for federal office and shall be administered by the Election Authority (III.3).

**Eligibility:** A candidate for federal office may participate if the candidate qualifies for the ballot and receives small contributions from at least a number of different citizens established by law for that office. The number shall be set so that a serious candidate without wealth or party support can reasonably meet it.

**Conditions:** A participating candidate shall:

1. accept no contribution from any natural person above a limit established by law, which shall be lower than the limit otherwise established under III.11.b;
2. accept no contribution from any citizen association;
3. spend no more of their own funds on their own campaign than an amount established by law;
4. use matching funds only for campaign purposes; and
5. return any unspent matching funds after the election.

**Match:** The program shall match, at a ratio established by law of not less than one to one, contributions from citizens who are natural persons up to an amount per contributor established by law, subject to a total per candidate per election cycle established by law for each office.

**Funding:** Congress shall appropriate funds sufficient to pay every match due. If funds are insufficient, the Election Authority shall reduce every payment by the same percentage. Funding shall not be reduced, delayed, or conditioned for the purpose of influencing an election.

**Equal terms:** The program shall be available on equal terms to every qualified candidate who agrees to its conditions, regardless of party or political belief.

**Voluntary participation:** Participation is voluntary. No candidate shall be penalized for declining to participate. A candidate may withdraw before receiving matching funds.

Any state or territory may establish a similar program for its own elections.

### III.11.f — Foreign Interference

**Rule:** No foreign source shall, directly or indirectly:

1. make a contribution;
2. make an electoral expenditure;
3. make an expenditure concerning a ballot measure; or
4. give money or another thing of value to any person or organization for the purpose of funding a contribution or expenditure described in this subsection.

No person shall knowingly solicit, accept, or receive from a foreign source a contribution or another thing of value for the purpose of influencing an election, including information, services, or other assistance offered by a foreign government, or knowingly help a foreign source make a contribution or expenditure prohibited by this subsection.

**Certification:** Every organization that makes a contribution, an independent expenditure, or an expenditure concerning a ballot measure shall certify to the Election Authority, under penalty of perjury, that it is not a foreign source and that none of the funds used came from a foreign source.

**Foreign offers:** A candidate or campaign that receives an offer of assistance from a foreign government, or from a person acting on its behalf, shall report the offer to the Election Authority within a short period established by law. The Election Authority shall share the report with the authorities responsible for national security, and shall not publish it in a manner that would interfere with an investigation.

Any communication intended to influence an election that is paid for, produced, or directed by a foreign government shall disclose that fact. A foreign government has no right under this Constitution to influence an election.

**Rights retained:** A natural person who is not a citizen retains every right under Article I, including the rights to speak, publish, and peacefully assemble about elections and public issues, and may volunteer their own time. This subsection restricts only the giving and spending of money and other things of value, and the conduct of foreign governments.

**Equal protection:** This subsection distinguishes on the basis of citizenship, not national origin. It applies equally to every citizen, however citizenship was acquired (VII.1.c).

This subsection applies to every election held within the United States. It is an express constitutional permission for purposes of I.4.c.

### III.11.g — Use of Campaign Funds

Contributions and public matching funds shall be used only for campaign purposes and for the ordinary expenses of holding the office sought.

No candidate, campaign committee, or political party shall use contributions for personal use, including personal living expenses, personal debts, or payments to the candidate or a family member for services not actually provided at fair market value.

### III.11.h — Supreme Court Elections

This Section applies to elections for judge of the Supreme Court, subject to VI.2.c. No political party shall make a contribution, coordinated expenditure, or independent expenditure for or against a candidate for the Supreme Court.

### III.11.i — Scope, Enforcement, and Limits

**Scope:** III.11.b, III.11.c, III.11.d, III.11.e, III.11.g, and III.11.h apply to elections for federal office. III.11.f applies to every election. Each state and territory shall establish rules for its own elections consistent with this Section, and may adopt stricter limits that satisfy III.11.b.

**Enforcement:** Congress shall enact laws enforcing this Section, including civil and criminal penalties consistent with due process (II.1.a) and II.7. The Election Authority shall audit reports, investigate compliance under III.3.c, and may impose a civil penalty only after notice and a hearing, subject to judicial review. Criminal penalties shall apply only to knowing and willful violations.

**Good-faith correction:** A recipient who returns a contribution that exceeds a limit, or that came from a foreign source, within a period established by law after learning of the violation shall not be penalized for receiving it, unless the recipient knowingly solicited it.

This Section binds private actors as well as the government.

No law or action under this Section shall:

1. restrict news activity;
2. restrict speech or spending other than as this Section expressly permits;
3. favor or disfavor a candidate, political party, political belief, or electoral faction (III.10.b);
4. restrict communication about public issues that is not an electoral expenditure; or
5. change a contribution limit, expenditure limit, disclosure requirement, or the terms of public financing for an election in which voting has begun (III.2.d). An inflation adjustment takes effect only at the start of an election cycle.
