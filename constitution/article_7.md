# ARTICLE VII — CITIZENSHIP AND THE UNION

## Section 1 — Citizenship

### VII.1.a — Citizenship by Birth

**Right:** Every person born within Concordia is a citizen, regardless of the citizenship or immigration status of the person's parents.

Every person born outside Concordia to at least one parent who is a citizen at the time of the birth is a citizen.

A child born within Concordia to a foreign diplomat who has diplomatic immunity is not a citizen by birth, unless the child would otherwise be stateless.

**Definitions:**

* **Within Concordia** means within any constituent jurisdiction (VII.2.a).

### VII.1.b — Citizenship by Adoption and Naturalization

A child adopted by a citizen becomes a citizen upon the adoption.

Congress shall establish uniform rules for naturalization (IV.1.b). No rule of naturalization shall distinguish among applicants on the basis of protected status (I.4.a) unless it satisfies I.4.c.

### VII.1.c — Equal Citizenship

All citizens possess equal rights and status, whether citizenship was acquired by birth, adoption, naturalization, or under VIII.3.g.

### VII.1.d — Loss of Citizenship

**Right:** No citizen shall be deprived of citizenship.

A citizen who has reached the age of majority may renounce citizenship only by a voluntary, informed, and formal act before an authority designated by law. No renunciation shall take effect if it would leave the person stateless.

Citizenship shall not be revoked as a punishment or because of any conduct.

Naturalization obtained through material fraud is void, but only if a court finds the fraud proven by clear and convincing evidence in a proceeding brought within ten years after the naturalization. The government bears the burden of proof.

## Section 2 — The Union

### VII.2.a — Composition

Concordia consists of its constituent states, the federal capital district, and its territories, each of which is a **constituent jurisdiction**.

The original constituent jurisdictions are the states, the capital district, and the territories that ratify this Constitution under Article VIII.

### VII.2.b — Representation in Congress

Each constituent jurisdiction is entitled to representation in the Senate (IV.3.a) and shall elect two Senators.

Seats in the House of Representatives shall be apportioned among the constituent jurisdictions according to population, and each constituent jurisdiction shall have at least one Representative. No House district shall cross the boundary of a constituent jurisdiction.

No law shall reduce or remove the representation of a constituent jurisdiction.

### VII.2.c — Citizens Living Abroad

A citizen living outside Concordia votes in federal elections in the constituent jurisdiction and House district where the citizen last resided.

A citizen who has never resided in Concordia votes where a citizen parent last resided.

The Election Authority shall provide reasonable means for citizens living abroad to register and vote (III.1.f).

### VII.2.d — Permanence of the Union

The Union is permanent. No constituent jurisdiction may secede from Concordia, and none may be expelled, except through Article IX.

### VII.2.e — Admission and Change of Status

A new state may be admitted, and a territory or the capital district may become a state, only with the approval of a majority of each House of Congress and a majority of the voters of the affected area in a referendum administered by the Election Authority.

No state shall be divided, merged with another, or have its boundaries changed without the approval of a majority of each House of Congress and a majority of the voters of each state affected.

A newly admitted constituent jurisdiction shall elect both of its Senators at the next general election after its admission, and lot shall assign one of its two seats a first term of two years, so that the staggering of IV.3.d is preserved.

### VII.2.f — The Capital District

The federal capital district is the seat of the national government.

The residents of the capital district shall elect their own local government, which shall have the powers of a state government except where Congress by law provides otherwise to protect the operation of the national government.

## Section 3 — The States and Territories

### VII.3.a — Reserved Powers

Powers not granted to the federal government by this Constitution, and not prohibited by it to the states, are reserved to the states and to the people.

### VII.3.b — Supremacy of Federal Law

This Constitution prevails over every law and governmental action.

A federal law within the powers granted by this Constitution prevails over a conflicting state or territorial law.

### VII.3.c — Democratic Government

Every state and territory shall have an elected legislature and an elected executive.

Articles I, II, III, and X bind every state and territory and every level of government within them (I.4.b).

Congress shall not govern a territory except through, or in addition to, its elected territorial government.

### VII.3.d — Limits on the States

No constituent jurisdiction shall:

1. enter into a treaty or alliance with a foreign government;
2. engage in war, unless actually invaded or in imminent danger of invasion that does not admit of delay;
3. impose a tax or other barrier on goods, services, or persons moving between constituent jurisdictions; or
4. coin money.

---

## Section 4 — Relations Among the Jurisdictions

### VII.4.a — Full Faith and Credit

Each constituent jurisdiction shall give full faith and credit to the public acts, records, and final judicial proceedings of every other constituent jurisdiction.

### VII.4.b — Equal Treatment and Free Movement

**Right:** Every citizen may travel to, reside in, and work in any constituent jurisdiction, and shall receive the same rights and privileges there as its own residents.

### VII.4.c — Interstate Return of Accused Persons

A person charged with a crime in one constituent jurisdiction who is found in another shall, on request, be returned to the jurisdiction where the charge is pending, after a judicial hearing satisfying II.1.a confirming the person's identity and the validity of the charge.

### VII.4.d — Disputes Between Jurisdictions

Disputes between constituent jurisdictions, or between a constituent jurisdiction and Concordia, shall be decided by the Supreme Court (VI.2.k).
