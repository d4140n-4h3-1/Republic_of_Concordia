# BILL — HOMICIDE

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Homicide Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to define murder, attempted murder, and the lesser offense of manslaughter, and to treat a mass shooting as its own offense.

A person who opens fire on a crowd intending to kill as many people as possible has done everything in their power to commit mass murder. Whether anyone dies often depends on chance: a jammed weapon, a quick response, a skilled surgeon. The culpability and the danger to the public are the same. This Act therefore permits a sentence of up to life imprisonment for a mass shooting even when no one is killed. That punishment is proportionate to the seriousness of the offense and the offender's culpability, and is not grossly disproportionate (Article II, Section II.7.b).

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every offense committed:

1. in the capital district or a territory (Article IV, Section IV.1.b(10));
2. on land or in a building owned or used by the federal government;
3. against a federal officer or employee because of their duties; or
4. in the course of travel or transport between constituent jurisdictions or into or out of Concordia.

Where federal jurisdiction depends on item 3 or 4, that fact is an element of the offense, to be charged and proven beyond a reasonable doubt (Article II, Section II.4.f).

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(6), IV.1.b(10), IV.1.b(14), and IV.1.b(17).

A state or territory may adopt this Act, or a law consistent with it, for offenses within its own jurisdiction.

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Person is defined in Article I, Section I.0.a. Firearm is defined in the Firearm Definition Act. Serious bodily harm is defined in the Use of Force Act, Section 4.e.)*

### 4.a Kill

To **kill** means to cause the death of a person.

### 4.b Intent to Kill

A person acts with **intent to kill** when their purpose is to cause the death of a person, or when they know that death is practically certain to result from their conduct.

### 4.c Extreme Indifference

A person acts with **extreme indifference** when they knowingly engage in conduct that creates a grave risk of death to another person, in circumstances showing that they do not care whether that person lives or dies.

### 4.d Premeditation

**Premeditation** means that the person formed the intent to kill, and reflected on it, before acting. The time needed for reflection may be short, but it must be enough for the person to have considered the decision.

### 4.e Mass Shooting

A person commits a **mass shooting** who discharges a firearm at or among people, with intent to kill:

1. three or more persons; or
2. persons gathered in a place, chosen without regard to who they are.

## Section 5 — Murder

### 5.a Offense

A person commits **murder** who kills another person:

1. with intent to kill; or
2. with extreme indifference.

### 5.b Aggravated Murder

A murder is **aggravated murder** where it meets the definition in Article II, Section II.7.e. Each aggravating circumstance is an element, to be charged and proven beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury (Article II, Sections II.4.f and II.4.j).

### 5.c No Murder Without a Guilty Mind

No person shall be convicted of murder because a death resulted from another offense the person committed, unless the person acted with intent to kill or with extreme indifference. A person who takes part in an offense in which another person kills is guilty of murder only if they intended the killing, or knowingly helped the killer with the purpose of bringing it about.

## Section 6 — Manslaughter

A person commits **manslaughter** who:

1. kills another person with intent to kill, in a sudden and intense loss of self-control caused by circumstances that would provoke a reasonable person to lose self-control, before there was time for that loss of control to pass; or
2. kills another person while honestly but unreasonably believing that the force was needed to defend themselves or another person (Article I, Section I.3.a).

Where there is evidence of either circumstance in a prosecution for murder, the government bears the burden of proving beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not exist (Article II, Section II.4.f).

Words alone, the discovery of infidelity, or the victim's sexual orientation or gender identity, are not circumstances that would provoke a reasonable person to lose self-control.

## Section 7 — Attempted Murder

A person commits **attempted murder** who, with intent to kill, takes a substantial step toward killing a person. Preparation alone is not a substantial step.

Attempted murder requires intent to kill. Extreme indifference is not enough.

A person who, by their own choice and not because of the risk of being caught, abandons the attempt before any person is harmed, and takes every reasonable step to prevent the killing, is not guilty of attempted murder.

## Section 8 — Mass Shooting

### 8.a Offense

A person who commits a mass shooting (Section 4.e) commits the offense of **mass shooting**, whether or not any person is killed or wounded.

### 8.b Attempted Mass Shooting

A person commits **attempted mass shooting** who, with the intent required by Section 4.e, takes a substantial step toward a mass shooting, such as arriving armed at the place chosen, but does not discharge a firearm at or among people. Preparation alone is not a substantial step.

Section 7's rule on abandonment applies.

### 8.c Relation to Murder

A person who kills in the course of a mass shooting may also be convicted of murder, or of aggravated murder, for each person killed. Each offense requires proof of a fact the others do not (Article II, Section II.4.g). The total punishment shall satisfy Article II, Section II.7.b.

### 8.d Relation to Attempted Murder

Every mass shooting includes an attempt to murder each person in the line of fire, so attempted murder requires no fact that a mass shooting does not. A person convicted of a mass shooting shall not also be convicted of, or punished for, attempted murder of any person in the course of the same shooting (Article II, Section II.4.g). The harm done to each person is weighed in sentencing for the mass shooting (Section 9.c).

The government may charge attempted murder in the alternative, and a person acquitted of a mass shooting may be convicted of attempted murder on the same facts.

### 8.e Serious Bodily Harm

Where the government charges and proves beyond a reasonable doubt that a mass shooting caused serious bodily harm to at least one person, the later parole limit in Section 9.b applies (Article II, Sections II.4.f and II.4.j).

## Section 9 — Penalties

### 9.a Maximum Penalties

| Offense | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Aggravated murder (Section 5.b) | Death, under Article II, Section II.7.e, or life imprisonment, with or without parole |
| Murder (Section 5.a) | Life imprisonment |
| Mass shooting (Section 8.a) | Life imprisonment |
| Manslaughter (Section 6) | Imprisonment for **fifteen years** |
| Attempted murder (Section 7) | Imprisonment for **twenty-five years** |
| Attempted mass shooting (Section 8.b) | Imprisonment for **twenty-five years** |

### 9.b Parole

A sentence of life imprisonment for any offense other than aggravated murder shall state when the person becomes eligible for parole, which shall be no later than:

1. **twenty years** after the sentence begins, for a mass shooting in which no person was killed and no serious bodily harm was proven under Section 8.e; and
2. **thirty years** after the sentence begins, in every other case.

Life imprisonment without parole may be imposed only for aggravated murder.

Parole eligibility does not guarantee release (Article II, Section II.8.c).

### 9.c Sentencing a Mass Shooting

In sentencing for a mass shooting, the court shall consider:

1. the number of people the offender intended to kill, and the number of people within range;
2. the number of shots fired, and the number of persons wounded;
3. the planning, including any target chosen for its vulnerability, such as a school or a place of worship; and
4. any reason the offender failed that was within their control, such as a choice to stop firing, under Section 9.d.

### 9.d A Choice to Stop

An offender **chose to stop** where they stopped firing, or stopped before firing, at a time when they were still able to continue: they had ammunition, a working weapon or one they could readily make work, and no person or obstacle was preventing them. A jam, an empty weapon, an injury, or being stopped by another person is not a choice, unless the offender could readily have continued and decided not to.

Where the offender raises evidence that they chose to stop, the court shall decide whether they did, by a preponderance of the evidence, and shall state its finding and its reasons. Where the court finds that they did, it shall give that choice substantial weight in setting the sentence and the parole date, and greater weight the earlier the offender stopped and the fewer persons were harmed.

A choice to stop is not a defense to a mass shooting. Section 7's rule on abandonment applies only where no person was harmed.

### 9.e Minors

For an offense committed while the offender was a minor, no sentence shall be death or life imprisonment without parole, and parole eligibility shall come no later than fifteen years after the sentence begins (Article II, Section II.7.d).

### 9.f No Mandatory Minimum

No law shall require a minimum sentence for an offense under this Act. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.c.

## Section 10 — What Is Not Homicide

None of the following is an offense under this Act:

1. self-defense or defense of others under Article I, Section I.3.a, including with an unlawful weapon (Defensive Use of Unlawful Weapons Act);
2. force that an officer uses lawfully under the Use of Force Act;
3. medical assistance in dying given under Article I, Section I.3.c;
4. an abortion, or any other decision about pregnancy under Article I, Section I.3.a; or
5. lawful resistance under Article X, Section 4.

## Section 11 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. extend capital punishment beyond aggravated murder under Article II, Section II.7.e;
2. permit any sentence for a minor offender that Article II, Section II.7.d forbids;
3. permit a conviction without proof of every element beyond a reasonable doubt; or
4. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 12 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 13 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, and applies only to offenses committed on or after that date (Article II, Section II.7.a).
