# BILL — AUDITOR-GENERAL

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Auditor-General Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to carry out Article IV, Section IV.10.g: to establish the nonpartisan panel that proposes candidates for Auditor-General, and to set how the office audits, reports, and follows up, so that every use of federal money can be checked by someone no official controls.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.10.c, IV.10.g, and IV.1.b(17).

## Section 4 — Selection Panel

### 4.a Members

The **Auditor-General Selection Panel** consists of five members chosen by public random drawing, under the eligibility, verification, and public-drawing rules of Article III, Section III.3.f:

1. three from applicants who have practiced as certified accountants or auditors for at least ten years; and
2. two from voters who apply.

No more than two members may be affiliated with the same political party. No person who has been employed by a body the Auditor-General audits, other than as an auditor, within the previous four years may serve.

### 4.b Duty

When the office of Auditor-General is vacant, or will be vacant within one year, the Panel shall publicly invite applications, interview candidates in public, and propose to Congress at least **three** qualified candidates, with the reasons for each. The Panel ends its work when an Auditor-General is appointed.

### 4.c Qualifications

A candidate shall have at least fifteen years of experience in auditing, accounting, or public finance, and shall meet the requirements of Article IV, Section IV.10.g.

### 4.d Appointment

Each House shall vote on the candidates within ninety days after they are proposed. A candidate approved by three-fifths of all members of each House is appointed. If no candidate is approved within that period, the Panel shall propose a new list, which may include earlier candidates.

## Section 5 — Deputy and Continuity

The Auditor-General shall appoint a Deputy Auditor-General from the office's career staff. When the office of Auditor-General is vacant, the Deputy acts as Auditor-General, with every power of the office, until an Auditor-General is appointed.

The staff of the office shall be appointed on merit under the Civil Service Act, and shall be independent of every body the office audits.

## Section 6 — Audits

### 6.a Annual Audit of Accounts

The Auditor-General shall audit the accounts of the federal government each year, and publish an opinion on whether they are complete and accurate, within six months after the end of the fiscal year.

### 6.b Performance Audits

The Auditor-General shall audit whether federal programs achieve their purposes at reasonable cost, choosing the programs to audit independently. Either House of Congress may ask for an audit, but may not direct its conclusions.

### 6.c Bodies Audited

The Auditor-General shall audit every body listed in Article IV, Section IV.10.g, including the central bank's accounts and operations (Central Bank Act, Section 9.c), the National Wealth Fund (Pensions and National Wealth Fund Act, Section 8), the Emergency Reserve Fund after each emergency (Emergency Powers Act, Section 8), and every organization receiving federal funds under a contract or grant, as to those funds.

## Section 7 — Reports and Follow-Up

Every audit report shall be made public under Article IV, Section IV.10.g. The audited body shall publish a written response within **sixty days**, stating which recommendations it accepts, and how and when it will carry them out.

The Auditor-General shall report to Congress each year on whether earlier recommendations have been carried out.

## Section 8 — Obstruction

A person who knowingly withholds, falsifies, or destroys a record the Auditor-General is entitled to examine, or retaliates against a person for providing one, commits an offense punishable by imprisonment for no more than **two years**. The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

Nothing in this Act permits the Auditor-General to examine the personal records of a natural person because the person receives a salary, pension, or public benefit (Article IV, Section IV.10.g), or to direct or reverse a monetary policy decision (Article IV, Section IV.10.f).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment. The first Selection Panel shall be drawn within ninety days after enactment.
