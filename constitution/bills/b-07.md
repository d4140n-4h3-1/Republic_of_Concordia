# BILL — DEFENSIVE USE OF UNLAWFUL WEAPONS

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Defensive Use of Unlawful Weapons Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to make clear that a person who lawfully defends themselves or another person does not lose the right of self-defense under Article I, Section I.3.a because the weapon they used was unlawful to possess.

The defensive use and the unlawful possession are separate. The defensive use is not a crime. The unlawful possession remains an offense and may be prosecuted.

Where a defender is held in custody, this Act protects them from the person they defended against.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every prosecution, and to every place of custody, under the law of the federal government or of any state, territory, or local government.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.3.a and Article II, Sections II.5 and II.10, against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c).

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Unlawful Weapon

**Unlawful weapon** means a weapon that the person who used it was not lawfully permitted to possess or carry at the time and place of its use, whether because of the type of weapon, the manner or place of carrying, a lack of license or registration, or a law prohibiting that person from possessing it.

### 4.b Defensive Use

**Defensive use** means a use of force that is self-defense or defense of others under Article I, Section I.3.a.

*(Self-defense, defense of others, imminent danger, and reasonable force are defined in Article I, Section I.3.a.)*

### 4.c Defender

**Defender** means a person who made a defensive use of force with an unlawful weapon.

### 4.d Aggressor

**Aggressor** means a person whose imminent unlawful threat of bodily harm prompted a defensive use, and any person the custodial authority knows was acting with that person in making the threat.

### 4.e Possession Offense

**Possession offense** means an offense whose elements are met by possessing, carrying, acquiring, or transporting an unlawful weapon.

## Section 5 — Self-Defense Preserved

### 5.a No Forfeiture

The unlawfulness of a weapon shall not, by itself, defeat a claim of self-defense or defense of others.

Whether force was a defensive use shall be decided as if the weapon had been lawfully possessed. The unlawfulness of the weapon shall not be treated as evidence that the defender:

1. provoked the threat;
2. was the initial aggressor;
3. used unreasonable force; or
4. was engaged in unlawful activity that removes the right of self-defense.

The force used must still be reasonable force. The type of weapon may be considered only in deciding whether the force was proportionate to the threat, in the same way it would be considered for a lawful weapon of the same type.

### 5.b No Offense From the Defensive Use

A defensive use is not a criminal offense. No person shall be convicted of, or receive an increased punishment for, any offense because of a defensive use, including an offense or enhancement for using, discharging, or brandishing a weapon.

### 5.c Burden of Proof

Where there is evidence that force was a defensive use, the government bears the burden of proving beyond a reasonable doubt that it was not (Article II, Section II.4.f).

### 5.d Pretrial Determination

A person charged with an offense based on a use of force may move for a pretrial determination that the force was a defensive use. If the court finds by a preponderance of the evidence that it was, the charges based on that use of force shall be dismissed. A denial does not prevent the person from claiming self-defense at trial.

### 5.e Initial Aggressors

Nothing in this Act changes the law that applies when a person was the initial aggressor or provoked the threat, except that the unlawfulness of the weapon is not evidence of either.

## Section 6 — The Possession Offense

### 6.a Prosecution Permitted

A defender may be prosecuted for a possession offense. A defensive use does not excuse the possession.

### 6.b No Increase for Defensive Use

The punishment for a possession offense shall not be increased because the weapon was used, carried for, or available for a defensive use.

### 6.c Mitigation

In sentencing a defender for a possession offense, the court shall consider the defensive use, and the circumstances that led the defender to possess the weapon, as mitigating circumstances.

### 6.d Protection of Testimony

Testimony a defender gives in support of a pretrial determination under Section 5.d shall not be used against them, at trial, to prove a possession offense, except to impeach testimony they give at trial that contradicts it. *(See Article II, Section II.4.b.)*

### 6.e The Weapon

The unlawful weapon may be seized as evidence and, after the case ends, may be forfeited or destroyed under the law that makes it unlawful.

## Section 7 — Custody of a Defender

### 7.a Pretrial Detention

A defensive use shall not be treated as evidence that the defender is dangerous for purposes of pretrial detention. A defender charged only with a possession offense may be detained before trial only on a ground permitted by Article II, Section II.3.d, established without reliance on the defensive use.

### 7.b Separate Facility

A defender held in any custody, whether arrest custody, pretrial custody, or punitive custody for a possession offense, shall not be held:

1. in the same place of custody as an aggressor;
2. in the same vehicle, holding cell, or courthouse holding area as an aggressor, at the same time; or
3. in any place where the custodial authority knows that a person acting on an aggressor's behalf is held, if that person is a threat to the defender.

### 7.c Nearest Facility

A defender shall be held in the place of custody nearest to the defender's residence that complies with Section 7.b and is suitable for the defender's custodial category. If the defender has no residence within the jurisdiction, the place of custody shall be the nearest to the place of the defensive use.

A defender may request a different place of custody. The request shall be granted if the requested place complies with Section 7.b.

### 7.d The Aggressor Moves, Not the Defender

If an aggressor is later brought into custody where a defender is held, the aggressor shall be placed elsewhere. A defender shall not be moved to achieve separation unless no other placement for the aggressor is lawfully available.

### 7.e No Worse Conditions

Separation under this Section is a protective measure, not punishment (Article II, Section II.5.c). It shall not:

1. result in solitary confinement (Article II, Section II.5.e);
2. reduce the defender's access to counsel, visits, communication, medical care, education, rehabilitative programs, or other privileges; or
3. delay the defender's release, hearing, or trial.

### 7.f No Contact

An aggressor in custody shall not be permitted to contact a defender through any communication system controlled by a custodial authority, except through counsel for purposes of a pending proceeding.

### 7.g Records

Before placing a defender, the custodial authority shall determine whether any aggressor is in custody within its system, and shall record that determination and the reason for the placement chosen. Each placement under this Section is subject to the inspections and reporting of the Custodial Oversight Act.

## Section 8 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. restrict the right of self-defense or defense of others under Article I, Section I.3.a;
2. authorize any use of force that is not reasonable force;
3. authorize the possession of an unlawful weapon;
4. authorize any custodial restriction beyond what Article II, Section II.10.d permits; or
5. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 9 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 10 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 5 applies to every prosecution pending on or after that date. Section 7 applies to every person in custody on or after that date.
