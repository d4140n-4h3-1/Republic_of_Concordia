# BILL — FIREARM DEFINITION

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Firearm Definition Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to establish a single definition of a firearm, so that every law of Concordia, and every law carrying out Article X, uses the same meaning.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every law of Concordia that uses the word "firearm," and to every law of a state, territory, or local government that carries out Article X, Section 2.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article X against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c), and under IV.1.b(17).

No law shall define "firearm" more broadly than this Act, or treat as a firearm anything this Act excludes.

## Section 4 — Definition of Firearm

### 4.a Firearm

**Firearm** means any projectile weapon designed to expel a projectile designed to cause death, by the use of a combustible substance.

A firearm remains a firearm regardless of its age, its design, or its method of loading, including an antique, muzzle-loading, or black-powder firearm.

### 4.b Terms Used in the Definition

* **Projectile weapon** means a weapon that launches an object through the air toward a target.
* **Designed to** means made for that purpose by its manufacturer or maker, as shown by its structure and function.
* **Projectile designed to cause death** means a bullet, ball, slug, shot, or other projectile that is made to be capable of killing a person or an animal when expelled as the weapon is designed, whatever its material. It does not include a projectile designed to incapacitate or cause pain without a substantial risk of death, such as a rubber, foam, beanbag, or marking projectile.
* **Combustible substance** means gunpowder, smokeless powder, or any other substance that burns rapidly to produce the gas that propels the projectile.

## Section 5 — Parts and Conversions

### 5.a Frame or Receiver

The frame or receiver of a firearm is treated as a firearm. **Frame or receiver** means the part of a firearm that houses the firing mechanism and to which the barrel is attached.

No other part, accessory, or ammunition is a firearm.

### 5.b Readily Converted

A weapon that is not a firearm is treated as a firearm if it can be converted into one in less than **eight hours** of work by a person of ordinary skill, using ordinary hand tools.

## Section 6 — Exclusions

None of the following is a firearm:

1. a weapon that expels a projectile by compressed air, gas, or a spring, without a combustible substance;
2. a tool that uses a combustible charge to drive nails, fasteners, or other objects for construction or industry, and that is not designed for use as a weapon;
3. a flare gun or other signaling device, unless it is designed or modified to expel a projectile designed to cause death;
4. a line-throwing device used for rescue or for maritime or industrial work;
5. a launcher designed to expel only projectiles that are not designed to cause death, such as a less-lethal launcher (Use of Force Act, Section 4.d);
6. a device that fires only blank cartridges and cannot expel a projectile, such as a starter pistol; and
7. a weapon that has been permanently made incapable of firing.

## Section 7 — Relation to Article X

Every firearm that an individual can carry and use is arms under Article X, Section X.1.a, except a firearm designed to fire more than one shot with a single function of the trigger, which X.1.a excludes.

Nothing in this Act narrows the meaning of arms in Article X, or limits any right in that Article.

## Section 8 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

Where it is uncertain whether an object is a firearm, the reading that preserves the greater protection of a constitutional right shall control (Article VI, Section VI.3.d).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 9 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 10 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
