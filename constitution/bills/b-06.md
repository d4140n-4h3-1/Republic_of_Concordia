# BILL — BIAS-MOTIVATED CRIMES

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Bias-Motivated Crimes Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to increase the punishment for a crime committed because of a victim's protected status, by a fixed formula, so that the increase is predictable, proportionate, and applied equally to every person.

A crime that selects its victim because of protected status harms the victim and threatens every person who shares that status. This greater harm and culpability is a lawful ground for greater punishment under Article II, Section II.7.b.

This Act punishes the selection of a victim. It does not punish any belief, opinion, or expression protected by Article I, Section I.1.a.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every covered offense (Section 4.b) prosecuted under federal law.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce the equal protection guaranteed by Article I, Section I.4.a, and under IV.1.b(17).

A state or territory may adopt this Act's formula for offenses under its own law. A state or territory that enhances punishment for bias by any other method shall give the accused every protection in Sections 6, 7, and 10.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Protected Status

*(Protected status is defined in Article I, Section I.4.a. It includes race, color, ethnicity, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, age, religion, belief, non-belief, and socioeconomic status.)*

Protected status includes a status the offender believed the victim had, whether or not the belief was correct, and a status of a person or group with whom the victim was associated.

### 4.b Covered Offense

**Covered offense** means a criminal offense that has, as an element, the use, attempted use, or threatened use of force against a person, or damage to or destruction of property.

A covered offense does not include an offense whose only conduct is speech, expression, or peaceful assembly.

### 4.c Bias Selection

**Bias selection** means that the offender selected the victim, or the property, because of protected status, and would not have selected that victim or that property but for the protected status.

### 4.d Base Sentence

**Base sentence** (B) means the sentence the court would impose for the covered offense without this Act, stated in months of imprisonment, or as a fine amount, or both.

### 4.e Enhancement

**Enhancement multiplier** (m) means the sum of the factor weights proven under Section 6.

**Added time** (A) means the additional imprisonment imposed under this Act, in months.

## Section 5 — The Formula

### 5.a Imprisonment

The total sentence of imprisonment (S) for a covered offense committed with bias selection is:

> **S = B + A**, where **A = min( B × (m − r), C )**

and:

* **B** is the base sentence (Section 4.d);
* **m** is the enhancement multiplier (Section 6), no greater than **0.50**;
* **r** is the restorative credit (Section 8), no greater than **m ÷ 2**; and
* **C** is the cap on added time: **120 months** for an adult offender, or **36 months** for an offense committed while the offender was a minor (Article II, Section II.7.d).

Added time is rounded down to the nearest whole day.

### 5.b Fines

Where the base sentence includes a fine (F), the total fine is:

> **F × (1 + m − r)**

The total fine shall satisfy Article II, Section II.7.f, and shall be reduced to the extent the offender cannot pay it without hardship.

### 5.c Minors

For an offense committed while the offender was a minor, every factor weight in Section 6 is halved before m is calculated. Nothing in this Act permits a sentence that exceeds the limits of Article II, Section II.7.d.

### 5.d Life Imprisonment

Where the base sentence is life imprisonment, this Act does not change the life sentence. It instead delays the earliest date of parole eligibility (P, in months) by:

> **min( P × (m − r), C )**

For an offense committed while the offender was a minor, the delay is instead:

> **min( P × (m − r), C, max(0, 180 − P) )**

so that the total period before parole eligibility never exceeds the fifteen years (180 months) allowed by Article II, Section II.7.d.

Where the base sentence is life imprisonment without parole, or death, this Act adds nothing.

## Section 6 — Factors

### 6.a Factor Table

The enhancement multiplier is the sum of the weights of every factor proven under Section 7:

| Factor | Description | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **Bias selection** (Section 4.c). Required: if F1 is not proven, this Act does not apply and m = 0. | 0.20 |
| **F2** | **Intimidation of a group:** the offender intended the offense to intimidate persons other than the victim who share the protected status, such as by public display, recording, or broadcast of the offense. | 0.10 |
| **F3** | **Serious bodily injury:** the offense caused serious bodily injury or death, and the base offense does not already require serious bodily injury or death as an element. | 0.10 |
| **F4** | **Place of community:** the offense was committed at or against a place of worship, cemetery, school, community center, or other place identified with persons of the protected status. | 0.05 |
| **F5** | **Group offense:** the offender acted with two or more other persons who also engaged in bias selection. | 0.05 |

The maximum possible multiplier is **m = 0.50**.

### 6.b No Double Counting

A factor shall not be counted if the same fact is already an element of the covered offense or was already used to increase the base sentence.

Where the base offense itself requires proof of bias selection, F1 shall not be counted and this Act does not apply.

### 6.c One Enhancement per Episode

Where the offender is convicted of more than one covered offense arising from the same criminal episode, this Act applies only once for the episode. Added time is calculated using the largest single base sentence among those offenses, and is not added to any other sentence for the episode.

## Section 7 — Proof

### 7.a Elements for the Jury

Bias selection (F1) and each other factor in Section 6.a are elements of an enhanced offense. Each shall be charged in the indictment or information, and proven to the trier of fact beyond a reasonable doubt (Article II, Sections II.4.c, II.4.f, and II.4.j).

Where the base offense is tried to a jury, the jury shall find each factor unanimously, by a separate verdict for each factor.

### 7.b Evidence of Bias

Bias selection may be proven by the offender's statements, conduct, and choice of victim, but only to the extent the evidence is connected to the selection of the victim or property in the offense charged.

The following shall not, by itself, be admitted to prove bias selection:

1. the offender's membership in or association with any lawful organization;
2. the offender's beliefs, opinions, or expression not connected to the offense; or
3. the offender's reading, viewing, or possession of lawful material.

The victim's protected status, by itself, does not prove bias selection.

### 7.c Mixed Motive

Where the offender had more than one motive, bias selection is proven only if the trier of fact finds beyond a reasonable doubt that the offender would not have selected the victim or property but for protected status.

## Section 8 — Restorative Credit

A sentencing court shall grant a restorative credit (r) of up to **0.05** for each of the following, but the total credit shall not exceed **m ÷ 2**:

1. the offender has completed, or agrees as a condition of parole to complete, a restorative justice program in which the victim, or a representative of the affected community chosen by the victim, has freely chosen to take part; or
2. the offender has completed an educational program concerning the harm of the offense.

No offender shall be compelled to take part in any program under this Section. A refusal to take part shall not increase the sentence. No victim shall be required to take part in a restorative justice program.

## Section 9 — Worked Examples

The following examples illustrate Section 5. They are not part of the formula, and if an example conflicts with Section 5, Section 5 controls.

| Case | B | Factors | m | r | A | S |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adult assaults a stranger because of the stranger's religion | 24 months | F1 | 0.20 | 0 | 24 × 0.20 = 4.8 months | 28.8 months |
| Same, recorded and posted to intimidate others of that religion | 24 months | F1, F2 | 0.30 | 0 | 24 × 0.30 = 7.2 months | 31.2 months |
| Same as above, offender completes a restorative program | 24 months | F1, F2 | 0.30 | 0.05 | 24 × 0.25 = 6 months | 30 months |
| Adult and three others burn a place of worship, injuring a caretaker | 180 months | F1, F2, F3, F4, F5 | 0.50 | 0 | min(90, 120) = 90 months | 270 months |
| Adult commits a homicide with every factor proven | 360 months | F1–F5 | 0.50 | 0 | min(180, 120) = 120 months | 480 months |
| Minor, age 16, assaults a person because of the person's disability | 12 months | F1 (halved to 0.10) | 0.10 | 0 | 12 × 0.10 = 1.2 months | 13.2 months |

## Section 10 — Custody and Treatment

A conviction under this Act shall not, by itself, change where or how a sentence is served.

Any restriction on a person in custody because of the nature of the offense, including separation from persons the offender may endanger, shall be based on an individualized determination and shall satisfy Article II, Section II.10.d.

No person shall be placed in a separate facility, unit, or custodial classification solely because of a conviction under this Act, and no person shall be placed in solitary confinement for that reason (Article II, Section II.5.e).

## Section 11 — Equal Application

This Act applies equally to every protected status and to every person, regardless of the protected status of the offender or the victim.

No prosecutor shall decline to seek, or choose to seek, an enhancement under this Act because of the protected status of the offender or the victim. A pattern of such decisions is a violation of Article I, Section I.4.a, and may be challenged under Article II, Section II.9.c.

## Section 12 — Capital Punishment

Nothing in this Act makes any offense punishable by death or adds to the aggravating circumstances in Article II, Section II.7.e.

## Section 13 — Reporting

The head of the executive department responsible for federal prosecutions shall publish, each year, the number of prosecutions, convictions, and acquittals under this Act, by protected status of the victim, by factor proven, and by the average added time imposed. No published record shall identify any victim.

## Section 14 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. punish any belief, opinion, or expression protected by Article I, Section I.1.a;
2. permit a factor to be found by any standard lower than proof beyond a reasonable doubt;
3. permit a sentence that is grossly disproportionate under Article II, Section II.7.b;
4. permit a sentence for a minor offender that exceeds Article II, Section II.7.d; or
5. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 15 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 16 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, and applies only to offenses committed on or after that date (Article II, Section II.7.a).
