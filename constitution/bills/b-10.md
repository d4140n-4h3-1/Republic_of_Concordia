# BILL — JUDICIAL ENFORCEMENT SERVICE

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Judicial Enforcement Service Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article VI, Section VI.3.e establishes a Judicial Enforcement Service to serve process, enforce judicial orders, and protect the courts, and sets the consequences when a governmental officer defies an enforceable order. The purpose of this Act is to organize the Service, set the procedure for a finding of defiance, and fix the sanctions, so that every court order can be carried out without depending on the officer who is defying it.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to the Service, to every court of Concordia, and to every governmental officer or body subject to an enforceable order.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(17), to carry into execution Article VI, Section VI.3.e.

*(Enforceable order and finding of defiance are defined in Article VI, Section VI.3.e.)*

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Service

**Service** means the Judicial Enforcement Service.

### 4.b Deputy

**Deputy** means a member of the Service who is authorized to carry out its duties.

### 4.c Special Officer

**Special officer** means a person appointed by a court under VI.3.e(1) to carry out an enforceable order in place of a defiant officer or body.

## Section 5 — Organization

### 5.a Director

The Director of the Service is appointed by the Supreme Court and may be removed only by the Supreme Court (VI.3.e). The Director serves a term of six years and may be reappointed once.

No person may be appointed Director who, within the previous five years, held elected office, held office in a political party, or was employed by the executive branch in law enforcement or the armed forces.

### 5.b Deputies

The Director shall appoint deputies by open competition on merit. A deputy shall not be a member of the armed forces or employed by any executive law enforcement agency while serving.

The Service shall maintain an office in every judicial circuit (VI.2.j) and enough deputies to serve every court.

### 5.c Funding

The Service is funded as part of the courts under Article VI, Section VI.4.a. Its funding shall not be reduced or conditioned to prevent or delay the enforcement of any order.

## Section 6 — Duties and Powers

### 6.a Duties

The Service shall:

1. serve process and court papers;
2. protect judges, jurors, witnesses, court staff, and courthouses;
3. carry out enforceable orders when a court directs it; and
4. assist special officers.

### 6.b Powers When Enforcing an Order

When carrying out an enforceable order at a court's direction, a deputy may:

1. enter any governmental building or property, and inspect and copy any governmental record, needed to carry out the order;
2. take custody of property or records that the order directs be produced or delivered; and
3. arrest a person only under a warrant or contempt order issued by the court, or to prevent imminent physical harm to a person protected under Section 6.a(2).

### 6.c Arms

Deputies may carry firearms and less-lethal weapons for the protection of persons and courts and for their own defense. The Service shall not possess any weapon designed to fire more than one shot with a single function of the trigger, any explosive weapon, or any armored or military vehicle.

### 6.d Limits on Force

The Service is subject to the Use of Force Act, including its Office of Independent Investigation. No deputy shall use force to carry out an order against a person who is complying, or use deadly force except as that Act permits.

### 6.e Assistance

Every law enforcement agency shall assist the Service when a court requests it in writing. No officer shall be disciplined for giving that assistance (VI.3.e).

## Section 7 — Finding of Defiance

### 7.a Motion

Any party to a case, or the court on its own motion, may seek a finding of defiance when an enforceable order has not been complied with within the time it allowed.

### 7.b Hearing

The court shall give the officer or body written notice and shall hold a hearing within fourteen days. The officer may be represented by counsel and may present evidence of compliance or of a lawful excuse.

### 7.c Standard

A finding of defiance requires clear and convincing evidence that the order was enforceable, that the officer or body knew of it, that it was not complied with within the time allowed, and that there was no lawful excuse.

**Lawful excuse** means that compliance was impossible despite good-faith effort, or that a court had stayed or reversed the order. Disagreement with the order is not a lawful excuse.

### 7.d President and Vice President

A finding of defiance against the President or Vice President may be made only by the Supreme Court (VI.3.e), which shall hold the hearing within seven days.

## Section 8 — Sanctions

### 8.a Special Officer

The court shall appoint a special officer whenever the order can be carried out by someone other than the defiant officer. A special officer shall be a person with the experience the task requires, shall not be a political appointee of the defiant officer, and shall report to the court every fourteen days until the order is carried out.

### 8.b Coercive Fines

The court may impose a coercive fine on the defiant officer personally, for each day of continued defiance after the finding, of no more than **one-thirtieth of the officer's annual public salary**, rising by that amount for each further thirty days of defiance.

The fine shall be paid from the officer's own funds and never from public funds (VI.3.e). The fine ends on the day of compliance. The court may return part or all of the fine after compliance.

### 8.c Coercive Confinement

If an officer remains in defiance thirty days after the finding, and a special officer cannot carry out the order, the court may order the officer confined until the officer complies. Confinement shall be reviewed by the court every seven days, shall end immediately on compliance, and shall not exceed ninety days.

Coercive confinement shall not be ordered against the President or the Vice President. Their defiance is addressed by impeachment under Article IV, Section 9.

### 8.d Certification to the House

The court shall certify each finding of defiance against an impeachable officer to the House of Representatives within two days, for the vote required by VI.3.e(5).

### 8.e No Pardon

A pardon, commutation, or reprieve does not apply to any sanction under this Section (Article V, Section V.5.b).

## Section 9 — Review and Records

A finding of defiance and any sanction may be appealed on an expedited basis, and the appeal shall be decided within fourteen days. A sanction remains in effect during the appeal unless the appellate court stays it.

Every finding of defiance, sanction, and special-officer report shall be public, except where a court seals a part of it to protect a person's safety.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. permit the Service to enforce any order that is not an enforceable order;
2. permit any sanction against a person who is complying;
3. permit any use of force beyond the Use of Force Act; or
4. place the Service under the direction of the executive branch.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 11 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 12 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment. The Supreme Court shall appoint the first Director within ninety days after enactment.
