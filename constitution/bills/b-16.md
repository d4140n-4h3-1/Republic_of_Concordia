# BILL — SAFE DRIVING

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Safe Driving Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to keep impaired drivers off the road and to treat alcohol and every other substance by the same rules: substances in a vehicle stay in a closed container, no one consumes them in a moving vehicle, and no one drives while impaired.

This Act carries out Article I, Section I.3.b, which permits laws against impaired conduct and laws governing substances in vehicles, and respects the limits on searches in Section I.3.b and Article II, Section II.6.d.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

Sections 4 through 7 apply on every road owned or maintained by the federal government, and to every commercial vehicle in interstate or foreign commerce. A state, territory, or local government may adopt them for other roads. No such law may impose a greater penalty than Section 8, or create an offense based on the presence of a substance alone.

Section 9 applies to every government in Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(6), as to interstate and foreign commerce; under IV.1.b(17); and under IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.3.b and Article II, Section II.6 against every level of government.

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Impaired is defined in Article I, Section I.3.b.)*

### 4.a Substance

**Substance** means alcohol, or any controlled substance as defined in the Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act, Section 3.a.

### 4.b Closed Container

**Closed container** means a container that is sealed as packaged, or that has been closed so that its contents cannot be consumed without opening it.

### 4.c Passenger Area

**Passenger area** means the part of a vehicle designed to seat the driver and passengers, including any area readily accessible to the driver or a passenger while seated. It does not include a trunk, a locked compartment, or the area behind the last upright seat of a vehicle without a trunk.

### 4.d Vehicle

**Vehicle** means a motor vehicle on a public road.

## Section 5 — Closed Containers

No person shall keep a substance in the passenger area of a vehicle unless it is in a closed container.

An opened container of a substance shall be kept outside the passenger area.

This Section does not apply to:

1. a substance prescribed to an occupant, kept in its prescription container;
2. the living area of a motor home or camper, while it is parked; or
3. a substance in the passenger compartment of a bus, taxi, or other vehicle for hire, if that compartment is separated from the driver.

## Section 6 — Consumption in a Moving Vehicle

No person shall consume a substance in a vehicle while it is in motion. A passenger in a compartment of a vehicle for hire that is fully separated from the driver may consume a substance that is not smoked or vaporized.

## Section 7 — Impaired Driving

### 7.a Offense

A person commits the offense of **impaired driving** if the person drives a vehicle while impaired.

### 7.b Proof of Impairment

Impairment shall be proven by evidence of the person's present condition, such as the manner of driving, the person's speech, coordination, and behavior, standardized field sobriety tests, an evaluation by a trained examiner, and the results of a lawfully obtained sample.

The presence of a substance or its byproducts in the person's body is not, by itself, proof of impairment (Article I, Section I.3.b). A concentration of a substance may be treated as proof of impairment only where reliable scientific evidence shows that the concentration impairs nearly every person. For alcohol, that concentration is **0.08 grams per 100 milliliters of blood**, or the equivalent in breath.

## Section 8 — Penalties

| Offense | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Open container (Section 5) | A civil fine; no arrest |
| Consumption in a moving vehicle (Section 6) | A civil fine; no arrest |
| Impaired driving (Section 7) | Imprisonment for **six months**, and suspension of the license to drive for up to **one year** |
| Impaired driving that causes bodily harm to another person | Imprisonment for **five years**, and suspension for up to **five years** |
| Impaired driving that causes the death of another person | Imprisonment for **fifteen years**, and suspension for up to **ten years** |

The aggravating facts in the last two rows shall be charged and proven beyond a reasonable doubt (Article II, Section II.4.f).

A court may order treatment for a substance-use disorder, with the person's consent under Article I, Section I.3.a, in place of or in addition to any penalty.

No law shall require a minimum sentence for an offense under this Act. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.f.

## Section 9 — Limits on Enforcement

### 9.a Stops and Searches

A vehicle may be stopped only for a lawful reason. The odor or sight of a substance in an amount consistent with personal use shall not justify a search, seizure, or arrest without a warrant (Article I, Section I.3.b).

An officer who lawfully sees an open container in the passenger area may issue a citation under Section 5, and may require the occupants to close it or move it outside the passenger area. The officer shall not search the vehicle or seize the substance on that basis.

### 9.b Samples

A sample of blood, urine, or other bodily fluid or tissue may be taken only with a warrant or with consent. A breath test may be taken without a warrant only after a lawful arrest for impaired driving that rests on evidence of impairment other than the odor or sight of a substance (Article II, Section II.6.d).

Every government that enforces this Act shall make electronic warrants available from an impartial tribunal at any hour (Article II, Section II.6.d).

### 9.c Refusal

Refusing a search or sample that requires a warrant or consent is not an offense and is not evidence of impairment.

### 9.d No Forfeiture

No vehicle or other property shall be seized or forfeited because of an offense under this Act (Article I, Section I.3.b; Article II, Section II.1.c), except as evidence under a warrant.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. treat the presence of a substance alone as proof of impairment;
2. permit any search, seizure, or sample beyond what Article I, Section I.3.b and Article II, Section II.6.d allow;
3. treat alcohol and other substances differently under Sections 5 and 6; or
4. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 11 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 12 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
