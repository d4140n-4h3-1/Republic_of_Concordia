# BILL — PROHIBITION OF CONVERSION PRACTICES

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Conversion Practices Prohibition Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose and Authority

The purpose of this Act is to protect persons from practices that seek to change, suppress, or eliminate a person's sexual orientation or gender identity through coercion, deception, physical or aversive methods, or the exploitation of persons who cannot give informed consent.

This Act protects the physical integrity and informed consent guaranteed by Article I, Section I.3.a, and the life and health of minors. It does not restrict the expression of any opinion or belief about sexual orientation or gender identity, and it applies equally whatever the direction of the change a practice seeks.

**Authority:** Congress enacts this Act:

1. under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Sections I.3.a and I.4.a against every level of government, as to Sections 8 and 9 and any conversion practice by or on behalf of a government; and
2. under IV.1.b(6), to regulate interstate and foreign commerce, as to every other conversion practice that is offered or provided for compensation, advertised, arranged, or provided across the boundary of a constituent jurisdiction or of Concordia, or by telephone, internet, or other interstate means of communication.

Where the interstate connection in item 2 is required, it is an element of the offense, to be charged and proven beyond a reasonable doubt (Article II, Section II.4.f).

**State laws:** A state or territory may adopt this Act, or a law consistent with it, for conversion practices within its own borders.

## Section 3 — Definitions

### 3.a Conversion Practice

**Conversion practice** means any treatment, therapy, program, intervention, or course of conduct that:

1. is directed at a particular person; and
2. seeks to change, suppress, or eliminate that person's sexual orientation or gender identity, or to bring it into conformity with a predetermined outcome.

*(Sexual orientation and gender identity are defined in Article I, Section I.4.a.)*

A conversion practice may be carried out through talk, counseling, behavioral techniques, medication, physical methods, confinement, or any combination of these.

### 3.b Excluded Practices

The following are not conversion practices:

1. acceptance, support, or understanding of a person's sexual orientation or gender identity;
2. exploration of a person's sexual orientation or gender identity that does not seek a predetermined outcome;
3. assistance to a person in coping with, or living consistently with, their own sexual orientation or gender identity, including medical care chosen under Article I, Section I.3.a;
4. treatment intended to prevent unlawful sexual conduct or sexual conduct with a person who cannot consent, provided that the treatment does not seek to change the person's sexual orientation toward or away from adults of any sex; and
5. the expression of an opinion, belief, or religious teaching that is not part of a conversion practice directed at a particular person.

### 3.c Practitioner

**Practitioner** means a person or organization that provides, offers, or arranges a conversion practice:

1. in exchange for compensation;
2. under a professional license, certification, or title; or
3. as part of a program, facility, or residential setting operated by an organization.

A religious organization is a practitioner when it meets any of these conditions.

### 3.d Compensation

**Compensation** means money, property, services, or another material benefit, whether paid by the person subjected to the practice or by another person.

### 3.e Aversive or Physical Method

**Aversive or physical method** means any of the following when used as part of a conversion practice:

1. inducing pain, nausea, vomiting, electric shock, or other physical discomfort;
2. administering medication or any other substance;
3. physical restraint, confinement, or isolation;
4. deprivation of food, water, sleep, or medical care; or
5. touching or other physical contact.

### 3.f Coercion

**Coercion** means causing a person to undergo or continue a conversion practice through force, threat of harm, confinement, withholding of the person's identity documents or property, threat of withdrawal of housing or financial support from a minor, abuse of legal process, or the exploitation of a custodial, supervisory, or dependent relationship.

### 3.g Younger Minor

**Younger minor** means a person below sixteen years of age.

*(A minor who has reached sixteen years of age makes decisions concerning their own body and medical care as a competent person under Article I, Section I.3.a.)*

### 3.h Informed Consent and Person in Custody

*(Informed consent, competent person, and person in custody are defined in Article I, Section I.3.a.)*

### 3.i Religious Counselor

**Religious counselor** means a person who, whether or not for compensation, provides a conversion practice while acting as:

1. a clergy member, minister, pastor, priest, imam, rabbi, elder, or other religious leader;
2. a religious teacher, youth leader, or spiritual mentor; or
3. a member, employee, or volunteer of a religious organization acting on its behalf.

A person is not a religious counselor solely because they are a parent, legal guardian, other family member, or friend of the minor.

## Section 4 — Coercive Conversion Practices

A person commits the offense of **coercive conversion practice** if the person knowingly subjects another person to a conversion practice:

1. through coercion; or
2. through an aversive or physical method, without that person's informed consent given under Section 6.

This Section applies to every person, whether or not a practitioner, and to every setting, including religious and family settings.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 5 — Younger Minors

A practitioner or religious counselor shall not provide, offer, or arrange a conversion practice for a younger minor, whether or not for compensation.

The consent of a parent or legal guardian does not authorize a conversion practice for a younger minor.

A parent or legal guardian shall not send or deliver a younger minor to a practitioner or religious counselor, inside or outside Concordia, for the purpose of a conversion practice.

**Constitutional basis:** A parent or legal guardian decides matters concerning a younger minor's body and care in the minor's best interests under Article I, Section I.3.a. A conversion practice exposes a younger minor to serious risk to life and health, including risk of suicide, and cannot be in the minor's best interests. That risk does not depend on whether the practice is paid or on whether it is religious. This Section protects the younger minor's life, health, and physical integrity for purposes of Article I, Section I.4.c.

**Limits:** This Section restricts only a conversion practice directed at a particular younger minor, as defined in Section 3.a. It does not restrict religious teaching, preaching, or worship addressed to a congregation, class, or the public, or the expression of an opinion or belief that is not part of such a practice (Section 10). It applies equally to religious and non-religious persons and to every religion, belief, and non-belief.

## Section 6 — Informed Consent of Competent Persons

A practitioner may provide a conversion practice to a competent person who has reached sixteen years of age only with that person's informed consent.

Consent is not informed unless, before the practice begins, the practitioner gives the person a written statement that:

1. there is no reliable scientific evidence that a person's sexual orientation or gender identity can be changed by a conversion practice;
2. conversion practices are associated with depression, anxiety, self-harm, and suicide;
3. the person may stop the practice at any time without penalty, and any fee for sessions not yet provided shall be refunded; and
4. identifies every aversive or physical method the practice may use.

Consent may be withdrawn at any time. A practice continued after consent is withdrawn is a practice without informed consent.

Consent given while the person is subject to coercion is not informed consent.

## Section 7 — Deceptive Claims

A practitioner shall not, in offering or providing a conversion practice, represent that the practice can or will change a person's sexual orientation or gender identity, or represent that a sexual orientation or gender identity is a disease, disorder, or defect.

This Section applies only to representations made in offering or providing a conversion practice for compensation. It does not restrict any other expression of opinion or belief.

## Section 8 — Persons in Custody

No person shall subject a person in custody to a conversion practice.

Because custody prevents voluntary participation, the consent of a person in custody does not authorize a conversion practice. Custody shall not be conditioned on, extended for, or made more restrictive because of a person's participation or refusal to participate in a conversion practice.

## Section 9 — Government

The government shall not:

1. provide, fund, or arrange a conversion practice;
2. pay public funds, including through insurance, grants, or contracts, to a practitioner for a conversion practice; or
3. condition any right, benefit, service, license, or custody decision on a person's participation in a conversion practice.

*(Government is defined in Article I, Section I.0.a.)*

## Section 10 — Protected Expression and Religion

Nothing in this Act restricts:

1. the expression of any opinion or belief about sexual orientation or gender identity, including in public speech, publications, advocacy, or teaching;
2. religious teaching, preaching, prayer, or worship, including prayer for or with a particular person, that does not involve coercion or an aversive or physical method and, for a younger minor, is not part of a conversion practice by a practitioner or religious counselor (Section 5);
3. a private conversation between a person and a family member or friend, or between a person who has reached sixteen years of age and a religious leader, that is not provided by a practitioner; or
4. a person's own choice, as a competent person, to seek out or engage in a religious or spiritual practice concerning their sexual orientation or gender identity, subject to Sections 6 and 7 where a practitioner provides it.

## Section 11 — Professional Discipline

A violation of Sections 5, 6, 7, or 8 by a person who holds a professional license or certification is professional misconduct.

The body that issued the license or certification may suspend, revoke, or condition it after notice and a hearing consistent with Article II, Section II.1.a.

## Section 12 — Civil Remedy

A person subjected to a conversion practice in violation of this Act may bring a civil action against the person or practitioner responsible.

The court may award actual damages, damages for emotional distress, the return of any compensation paid, injunctive relief, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

An action shall be brought within six years after the last conversion practice, or within six years after the person reaches the age of majority, whichever is later.

## Section 13 — Penalty

1. **Coercive conversion practice (Section 4):** imprisonment for no more than **three years**. Where the government charges and proves beyond a reasonable doubt that the practice was committed against a minor, or used an aversive or physical method that caused bodily harm, the maximum is **six years** (Article II, Section II.4.f).
2. **Younger minors (Section 5):** for a practitioner or religious counselor, imprisonment for no more than **one year**, in addition to professional discipline under Section 11. For a parent or legal guardian, a fine of no more than the compensation paid for the practice, and no imprisonment.
3. **Persons in custody (Section 8):** imprisonment for no more than **three years**.

Violations of Sections 6 and 7 are subject only to professional discipline (Section 11) and the civil remedy (Section 12).

No law shall require a minimum sentence for an offense under this Act. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.f.

## Section 14 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. restrict speech or expression on the basis of viewpoint, contrary to Article I, Section I.1.a;
2. restrict religious belief, teaching, or practice beyond what Article I, Section I.4.c permits;
3. restrict any medical decision that a person or their parent or legal guardian may make under Article I, Section I.3.a, other than a conversion practice for a younger minor;
4. treat any sexual orientation or gender identity more or less favorably than another; or
5. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 15 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 16 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
