# BILL — EMERGENCY POWERS

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Emergency Powers Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article V, Section V.6.c permits the President, during a declared emergency, to exercise only emergency powers that Congress authorized by law before the emergency. The purpose of this Act is to authorize those powers in advance, so that the government can respond to a real emergency, and to state their limits, so that no emergency becomes a source of unchecked power.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every emergency declared under Article V, Section 6.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(17), to carry into execution Article V, Section 6, and under IV.1.b(11) and IV.1.b(13).

No power that this Act does not list may be exercised as an emergency power. No other law grants emergency powers unless it expressly amends this Act.

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Emergency and emergency power are defined in Article V, Section V.6.a.)*

### 4.a Declaration

**Declaration** means a declaration of emergency made under Article V, Section V.6.a.

### 4.b Affected Area

**Affected area** means the geographic area stated in a declaration.

### 4.c Essential Goods and Services

**Essential goods and services** means food, water, medicine, medical supplies and services, fuel, shelter, sanitation, and the repair of housing.

## Section 5 — Declaring an Emergency

### 5.a Form

A declaration shall be published immediately and shall state, as V.6.a requires, the facts constituting the emergency, the affected area, and each power in Section 6 that is invoked, by its paragraph number.

A power not invoked by number in the declaration may not be exercised. The President may add a power by a published amendment to the declaration, which does not extend its duration.

### 5.b Notice to Congress

The President shall deliver the declaration to both Houses of Congress within twenty-four hours. If Congress is not in session, it shall convene within seventy-two hours of the declaration to consider it.

### 5.c Reports

While a declaration is in effect, the President shall report to Congress in writing at least every seven days, stating each action taken under each power invoked, its cost, and why it remains necessary.

## Section 6 — Authorized Emergency Powers

During a declaration, and only within the affected area, the President may invoke any of the following powers, subject to Section 7:

1. **Federal response.** Direct federal agencies, personnel, equipment, and supplies to rescue, relief, medical care, shelter, repair, and recovery.
2. **Emergency Reserve Fund.** Spend from the Emergency Reserve Fund established by Section 8, for the purposes in paragraph 1.
3. **Armed forces in support.** Use the armed forces to support civilian authorities, as V.7.a permits, only for transport, logistics, engineering, medical care, communications, and search and rescue. The armed forces shall not make arrests, conduct searches, control crowds, or otherwise enforce civilian law.
4. **Evacuation.** Order the evacuation of a specific place where remaining presents an imminent threat to life. No person shall be arrested, fined, or punished for refusing to evacuate. A person may be moved only under emergency custody (Article II, Section II.2.f) where the person's presence prevents the rescue of others.
5. **Use of property.** Take temporary possession of private vehicles, buildings, equipment, or supplies that are needed for rescue or relief and not otherwise available. The owner shall be paid fair market compensation for the use and for any damage, within ninety days after the property is returned. No home in which a person lives shall be taken, and no arms shall be taken (Article X, Section X.3.a).
6. **Prices.** Prohibit the sale of essential goods and services at a price more than twenty-five percent above the average price in the affected area during the thirty days before the declaration, except to the extent the seller's own costs have risen.
7. **Licenses and permits.** Suspend federal licensing and permit requirements that would prevent qualified persons from providing medical care, shelter, or repair, such as by permitting medical professionals licensed in any constituent jurisdiction to practice in the affected area.
8. **Isolation and quarantine.** Order the isolation or quarantine of a specific person who has, or has been exposed to, a communicable disease that presents imminent, serious harm to others, under Article I, Section I.3.a. Each order shall be individual, shall be the least restrictive measure that protects others, and shall be reviewed by a court within the period set for prompt review in Article II, Section II.3.c.
9. **Assistance.** Require brief and reasonable assistance under Article I, Section I.1.d(5), such as helping to evacuate others nearby. No person shall be required to assist for more than twenty-four hours in any declaration, or in a way that exposes them to serious danger. Every person who assists shall be paid at a reasonable rate.

## Section 7 — Limits on Every Emergency Power

No emergency power shall be used to:

1. suspend or narrow any right beyond what Article I, Section I.4.c would permit without an emergency (V.6.c);
2. postpone, cancel, or alter an election, or extend a term of office;
3. adjourn, dissolve, or obstruct Congress or any court;
4. spend money except from the Emergency Reserve Fund or another appropriation;
5. create a criminal offense or increase a penalty;
6. restrict the press, speech, peaceful assembly, or religious worship;
7. shut down, slow, or censor telephone, internet, broadcast, or other communications;
8. seize or require the surrender of lawfully held arms;
9. impose a curfew or a general restriction on movement, other than an evacuation order under Section 6(4); or
10. take any action outside the affected area, or after the declaration ends.

Every action taken under an emergency power ends when the declaration ends. Any contract made under an emergency power shall end no later than ninety days after the declaration ends, unless Congress approves it by law.

## Section 8 — Emergency Reserve Fund

There is established in the Treasury an Emergency Reserve Fund. Congress shall appropriate money to the Fund in advance, by law, in the ordinary budget. Spending from the Fund is spending under an appropriation for purposes of Article IV, Section IV.10.a.

The Fund may be spent only for the purposes in Section 6(1), only during a declaration, and only within the affected area. Every expenditure shall be published within seven days.

## Section 9 — Judicial Review

Any person affected by a declaration or by an action under an emergency power may challenge it in court under Article V, Section V.6.d.

The court shall hear the challenge on an expedited basis and shall decide it within seven days after it is filed.

The government bears the burden of showing that the declaration satisfies V.6.a and that the action is within this Act and V.6.c.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. grant any power beyond those listed in Section 6;
2. permit any act that Article V, Section V.6.c prohibits;
3. permit the armed forces to enforce civilian law; or
4. extend any declaration beyond what Article V, Section V.6.b permits.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 11 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 12 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment.
