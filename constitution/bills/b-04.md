# BILL — CUSTODIAL OVERSIGHT

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Custodial Oversight Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to carry out Article II, Section II.5.h by establishing independent inspection of every place of custody, a safe means for persons in custody to report abuse, neglect, and unlawful conditions, and protection from retaliation for those who report.

This Act protects the rights of persons in custody under Article II, Sections 5 and 10, and their bodily rights under Article I, Section I.3.a.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every place of custody operated by, or on behalf of, the federal government or any state, territory, or local government, including places operated by contractors.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article II, Sections II.5 and II.10 against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c).

A state, territory, or local government may establish additional oversight. No such oversight may limit the powers of the Inspectorate under this Act.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Place of Custody

**Place of custody** means any place where a person is held in custody under any custodial category in Article II, Section 2, including prisons, jails, police lockups, courthouse holding cells, juvenile facilities, immigration and other status-based custody facilities, facilities for civil protective custody, psychiatric facilities holding persons in custody, and vehicles used to transport persons in custody.

### 4.b Custodial Authority

*(Custody, custodial category, and custodial authority are defined in Article II, Section II.2.a. Custodial person is defined in Article II, Section II.5.a.)*

For purposes of this Act, a contractor operating a place of custody, or providing services within one, is bound by every duty of the custodial authority.

### 4.c Inspectorate

**Inspectorate** means the Custodial Inspectorate established by Section 5.

### 4.d Retaliation

**Retaliation** means any adverse action taken against a person because they reported, complained, cooperated with an inspection, or testified, including discipline, transfer, loss of privileges, reclassification, solitary confinement, denial of visits, threats, or the use of force.

### 4.e Serious Incident

**Serious incident** means a death in custody, a suicide attempt, serious bodily harm, a use of deadly force, an allegation of sexual abuse under Article II, Section II.5.f, an escape, or a placement in solitary confinement beyond the limits of Article II, Section II.5.e.

## Section 5 — The Custodial Inspectorate

### 5.a Establishment

The **Custodial Inspectorate** is established as an independent office of the federal government. It is not part of, and shall not share staff, facilities, or budgets with, any custodial authority or any agency that employs officers.

### 5.b Chief Inspector

The Inspectorate shall be headed by a Chief Inspector, nominated by the President and appointed with the confirmation of a majority of the Senate under Article V, Section V.4.a.

The Chief Inspector serves a single term of seven years and may not be reappointed.

No person may be appointed Chief Inspector who has been employed by a custodial authority within the five years before the appointment.

The President may remove the Chief Inspector only for cause, meaning neglect of duty, misconduct in office, or incapacity, after written notice stating the cause. A removed Chief Inspector may seek judicial review of the removal. *(See Article V, Section V.4.c.)*

No official may direct the Inspectorate to inspect, or not to inspect, a particular place, or to change a finding.

### 5.c Staff

The Inspectorate shall employ inspectors with expertise in custodial operations, medicine, mental health, the care of minors, disability, and human rights.

No inspector shall inspect a place of custody operated by a custodial authority that employed them within the previous three years.

### 5.d Regional Offices

The Inspectorate shall maintain regional offices sufficient to inspect every place of custody under Section 6 and to respond to a serious incident within twenty-four hours.

### 5.e Funding

Congress shall appropriate funds sufficient for the Inspectorate to carry out its duties. Funding shall not be reduced or conditioned in order to influence a particular inspection or finding.

## Section 6 — Inspections

### 6.a Frequency

The Inspectorate shall inspect:

1. every place of custody that holds persons for more than seventy-two hours, at least once each year;
2. every place of custody that holds minors, or that is used for solitary confinement, at least twice each year; and
3. every other place of custody, at least once every three years.

The Inspectorate may inspect any place of custody at any other time.

### 6.b Unannounced Access

Inspections may be unannounced. Inspectors shall be admitted to any place of custody immediately, at any hour, without prior notice or permission.

Inspectors shall have unrestricted access to:

1. every part of the place of custody, including cells, solitary confinement units, medical units, and transport vehicles;
2. every record, including custody records, medical records, use-of-force reports, recordings, grievance records, and records of solitary confinement; and
3. every person in custody and every member of staff.

A custodial authority may delay an inspector's access to a particular area for no more than one hour, and only because of an ongoing emergency that would endanger the inspector. The delay and its reason shall be recorded.

### 6.c Confidential Interviews

Inspectors may interview any person in custody or member of staff privately, out of the sight and hearing of other staff. No person shall be required to be interviewed.

Medical records may be examined by inspectors with medical expertise, and shall not be published in a manner that identifies any person.

### 6.d Standards

Inspections shall assess compliance with the Constitution, this Act, and applicable law, including:

1. humane treatment, safety, and protection from torture and cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment (Article II, Section II.5.a);
2. medical and mental health care (Article II, Section II.5.b);
3. separation of custody and punishment (Article II, Section II.5.c);
4. use of force (Article II, Section II.5.d);
5. solitary confinement (Article II, Section II.5.e);
6. protection from sexual abuse (Article II, Section II.5.f);
7. labor (Article II, Section II.5.g);
8. voting access (Article III, Section III.1.b);
9. education and the special protections for minors (Article II, Section II.2.k); and
10. whether each person is held under a lawful, recorded custodial category, and only for as long as that category permits (Article II, Sections II.2.a and II.2.o).

## Section 7 — Findings and Remedies

### 7.a Reports

The Inspectorate shall publish a report of each inspection within sixty days, stating its findings and recommendations. Reports shall not identify any person in custody without their consent.

### 7.b Corrective Plan

Within sixty days after a report finding a violation, the custodial authority shall publish a corrective plan stating how and by when each violation will be corrected. The Inspectorate shall monitor the plan and report on compliance.

### 7.c Compliance Orders

Where the Inspectorate finds a violation of the Constitution or this Act, it may issue a compliance order requiring the custodial authority to correct the violation within a reasonable time stated in the order.

Where a violation creates an imminent danger to the life, health, or safety of a person in custody, the Inspectorate may order immediate correction, including the transfer of that person to medical care or out of solitary confinement.

A custodial authority may seek judicial review of a compliance order. The order remains in effect during review unless a court stays it.

### 7.d Court Enforcement

The Inspectorate may bring an action in court to enforce a compliance order, or to remedy a pattern of violations. Where a custodial authority has repeatedly failed to correct serious violations, the court may appoint a receiver to operate the place of custody until the violations are corrected.

### 7.e Release Recommendations

Where the Inspectorate finds that a person is held without a lawful, recorded custodial category, or beyond the period that category permits, it shall immediately notify the person, their counsel, and the court with jurisdiction, so that the person may obtain review under Article II, Section II.9.b.

## Section 8 — Serious Incidents and Deaths

A custodial authority shall report every serious incident to the Inspectorate within twenty-four hours.

A death in custody shall also be reported within twenty-four hours to the Office of Independent Investigation, and the person's next of kin shall be notified as soon as reasonably possible and in no event later than twenty-four hours. The body shall not be released, and no autopsy shall be performed, until the Office of Independent Investigation has been notified. The next of kin may obtain an independent autopsy.

The Inspectorate shall review every death in custody and publish its findings concerning the conditions of custody. Any suspected crime shall be referred to the Office of Independent Investigation.

## Section 9 — Complaints

### 9.a Confidential Channels

Every person in custody shall have free and confidential means to complain to the Inspectorate, including:

1. sealed written correspondence, which no custodial staff shall open, read, delay, or destroy;
2. a free telephone line that is not recorded or monitored; and
3. private meetings with inspectors during inspections.

Every place of custody shall post notice of these means in each language commonly spoken by persons held there, and in accessible formats.

### 9.b Response

The Inspectorate shall acknowledge each complaint within seven days and, where it concerns an imminent danger to life, health, or safety, act on it within twenty-four hours.

The Inspectorate shall tell the complainant the outcome of each complaint.

### 9.c Access to Courts

A complaint under this Act is not required before, and does not limit, any action in court, including a challenge to custody under Article II, Section II.9.b or an action for a remedy under Article II, Section II.9.c.

No custodial authority shall require a person to use an internal grievance procedure before seeking a judicial remedy.

## Section 10 — Protection From Retaliation

No person shall take retaliation against a person in custody, a member of staff, a visitor, a family member, or any other person for complaining under this Act, cooperating with an inspection, or testifying.

Where a person in custody suffers an adverse action within ninety days after complaining or cooperating with an inspection, the custodial authority bears the burden of proving that the action was not retaliation.

The Inspectorate may order any retaliation reversed under Section 7.c.

## Section 11 — Other Access

### 11.a Counsel

Counsel for a person in custody shall have confidential access to that person at reasonable hours, without unreasonable delay.

### 11.b Members of Legislatures

A member of Congress may visit any place of custody operated by or on behalf of the federal government, and a member of a state or territorial legislature may visit any place of custody within that jurisdiction, without prior notice. A member may be accompanied by staff.

### 11.c Journalists

Journalists may visit places of custody and interview persons in custody who consent, subject to reasonable rules on time and safety that are neutral as to viewpoint and published in advance. No person shall be denied an interview because of what they are expected to say.

## Section 12 — Public Data

Each custodial authority shall publish quarterly, for each place of custody:

1. the number of persons held, by custodial category, age group, and protected status where known;
2. the number of serious incidents, by type;
3. the number of uses of force, by type;
4. the number and duration of placements in solitary confinement;
5. the number of complaints received and their outcomes; and
6. the number of deaths and their causes.

Published data shall not identify any person in custody.

The Inspectorate shall publish an annual national report to Congress.

## Section 13 — Offenses

A person commits an offense if the person knowingly:

1. obstructs, delays, or denies an inspector's access in violation of Section 6.b;
2. opens, reads, delays, or destroys correspondence protected by Section 9.a, or monitors the line protected by Section 9.a;
3. takes retaliation prohibited by Section 10;
4. falsifies, conceals, or destroys a record that the Inspectorate may examine; or
5. fails to report a death in custody as Section 8 requires.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 14 — Penalty

### 14.a Imprisonment

A person convicted of an offense under Section 13 may be sentenced to imprisonment for no more than:

1. **one year**, for an offense under Section 13(1), 13(2), or 13(5);
2. **three years**, for an offense under Section 13(4);
3. **three years**, for an offense under Section 13(3); or
4. **ten years**, for an offense under Section 13(3) where the retaliation involved the use of force, a placement in solitary confinement, or serious bodily harm.

The sentencing court shall consider whether the offense concealed or contributed to a death, serious bodily harm, or sexual abuse. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.c.

### 14.b Loss of Custodial Employment

A person convicted of retaliation under Section 13(3) that involved the use of force, a placement in solitary confinement, or serious bodily harm (the offense punished under Section 14.a(4)) shall not afterward be employed in, or provide services within, any place of custody.

**Independent basis:** This restriction is a qualification for authority over persons in custody, not continuing punishment. It protects the safety and physical integrity of persons in custody (Article II, Section II.5.a) and has an independent lawful basis under Article II, Section II.8.c.

The person shall be listed in the registry maintained under the Use of Force Act, and no government or contractor shall employ a person so listed in a place of custody.

### 14.c Civil Penalties for Non-Compliance

Where a custodial authority or contractor fails to comply with a compliance order by the date it states, the Inspectorate may impose a civil penalty for each day of non-compliance of no more than:

1. **$10,000** per day, for a custodial authority; and
2. **$25,000** per day, for a contractor.

A civil penalty may be imposed only after notice and a hearing consistent with Article II, Section II.1.a, and is subject to judicial review. In setting the amount, the Inspectorate shall consider the seriousness of the violation, the danger to persons in custody, the authority's or contractor's good-faith efforts, and its ability to pay. No penalty shall be excessive under Article II, Section II.7.f.

Penalties stop accruing on the day the order is complied with, or while a court stays the order.

### 14.d Use of Penalties Paid by a Government

A civil penalty paid by a custodial authority shall be paid into a corrective fund for the place of custody concerned, held by the Inspectorate, and spent only on correcting the violation and improving conditions of custody there.

No custodial authority shall pay a civil penalty by reducing spending on food, medical care, mental health care, education, staffing necessary for safety, or any other necessity of persons in custody.

### 14.e Contractor Debarment

A contractor that fails to comply with three or more compliance orders within any five-year period, or that fails to comply with an order concerning an imminent danger under Section 7.c, shall be barred from any contract to operate a place of custody, or to provide services within one, for **five years**. Debarment applies to any affiliate or successor of the contractor.

Debarment may be imposed only after notice and a hearing, and is subject to judicial review.

No contract shall allow a contractor to recover a civil penalty from the government.

## Section 15 — Contractors

Every contract to operate a place of custody, or to provide services within one, shall require the contractor to comply with this Act. A contractor's failure to comply is grounds for ending the contract.

No contract shall pay a contractor according to the number of persons held, the length of custody, or the occupancy of a place of custody.

## Section 16 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. limit any right of a person in custody under Articles I, II, or III;
2. limit access to courts or any remedy under Article II, Section 9;
3. authorize any restriction on a person in custody beyond what Article II, Section II.10.d permits;
4. require any person to be interviewed, or to disclose medical information, against their will; or
5. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 17 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 18 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, except that:

1. the Inspectorate shall complete its first inspection of every place of custody described in Section 6.a(1) and 6.a(2) within two years after enactment; and
2. Section 15 applies to every contract entered into or renewed after enactment, and to every existing contract no later than three years after enactment.
