# BILL — UNLAWFUL POSSESSION WITH INTENT TO PROFIT

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to prohibit possession of a controlled substance when the possessor specifically intends to obtain a material benefit through its unlawful transfer to another person.

This Act does not criminalize possession solely because a person intends to personally consume or use the substance, including as an exercise of bodily autonomy under Article I, Section I.3.a, except where another provision of law is independently applicable.

## Section 3 — Definitions

### 3.a Controlled Substance

**Controlled substance** means a substance designated by law as subject to lawful restrictions on manufacture, possession, transfer, or distribution.

### 3.b Possession

**Possession** means knowingly having actual physical possession of a controlled substance or knowingly exercising dominion or control over it.

Mere proximity to a controlled substance does not constitute possession without sufficient evidence of knowing dominion or control.

### 3.c Transfer

**Transfer** means knowingly providing, delivering, distributing, selling, exchanging, or otherwise conveying possession of a controlled substance to another person.

### 3.d Intent to Profit

**Intent to profit** means a specific intent to obtain money, property, services, or another material benefit through the transfer or attempted transfer of a controlled substance to another person.

Intent to profit shall not be established solely by:

1. possession of a controlled substance;
2. personal use or intended personal use;
3. the quantity of the substance possessed;
4. the possession of money or property unrelated to a demonstrated intended transfer; or
5. the status, occupation, associations, or personal characteristics of the accused.

### 3.e Material Benefit

**Material benefit** means money, property, services, debt reduction, or another objectively identifiable economic or material advantage.

## Section 4 — Offense

A person commits the offense of **unlawful possession with intent to profit** if the person:

1. knowingly possesses a controlled substance;
2. possesses the substance unlawfully under applicable law; and
3. specifically intends to obtain a material benefit through its transfer or attempted transfer to another person.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 5 — No Presumption From Possession

Possession of a controlled substance shall not, by itself, create a legal presumption that the person intended to transfer the substance to another person or intended to profit from such transfer.

The government bears the burden of proving intent to profit beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 6 — Bodily Autonomy

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to establish an exception to the right of bodily autonomy beyond an exception otherwise authorized by the Constitution.

The offense created by this Act concerns possession undertaken for the purpose of obtaining a material benefit through transfer to another person. It does not, by itself, criminalize a person's private bodily decision to possess or use a substance for personal purposes.

Any application of this Act that substantially restricts a constitutionally protected right remains subject to the constitutional requirements governing restrictions upon fundamental rights.

## Section 7 — Attempt

A person who knowingly takes a substantial step toward unlawful possession with intent to profit may be prosecuted for attempt, provided that the government proves the required specific intent beyond a reasonable doubt.

Preparation alone shall not constitute a substantial step.

## Section 8 — Conspiracy

Two or more persons may be prosecuted for conspiracy to commit an offense under this Act when they knowingly agree to commit the offense and at least one conspirator takes an overt act toward its commission.

Mere association, communication, or knowledge of another person's unlawful conduct shall not constitute conspiracy without the required agreement and overt act.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. create a broader governmental power than the Constitution grants;
2. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of the offense;
3. convert lawful private possession into unlawful commercial conduct solely through legislative definition;
4. permit punishment for conduct that the Constitution expressly protects; or
5. circumvent any constitutional requirement concerning bodily autonomy, due process, equal protection, or limitations on custodial authority.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 11 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 12 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
