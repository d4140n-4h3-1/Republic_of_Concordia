# BILL — UNLAWFUL POSSESSION WITH INTENT TO PROFIT

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose and Authority

The purpose of this Act is to prohibit possession of a controlled substance when the possessor specifically intends to obtain a material benefit through its unlawful transfer to another person.

This Act does not criminalize possession solely because a person intends to personally consume or use the substance, including as an exercise of bodily autonomy under Article I, Sections I.3.a and I.3.b.

**Authority:** Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(6), to regulate interstate and foreign commerce in controlled substances, and under IV.1.b(17).

**Interstate element:** The offenses in Sections 4 and 9, and attempt and conspiracy under Sections 7 and 8, apply only where the transfer intended would cross the boundary of a constituent jurisdiction or of Concordia, or the substance was brought across such a boundary for transfer. This is an element of each offense, to be charged and proven beyond a reasonable doubt (Article II, Section II.4.f).

**State laws:** A state or territory may make transfers within its own borders an offense, by a law that requires proof of intent to profit, creates no presumption from possession, and imposes no greater penalty than Section 10. A state or territory may also make a transfer to a minor within its own borders an offense, without proof of intent to profit, by a law that imposes no greater penalty than Section 9. No such law may punish possession for personal use (Article I, Section I.3.b), or a gift between adults under Section 9.a.

## Section 3 — Definitions

### 3.a Controlled Substance

**Controlled substance** means a substance designated by law as subject to lawful restrictions on manufacture, transfer, or distribution.

### 3.b Possession

**Possession** means knowingly having actual physical possession of a controlled substance or knowingly exercising dominion or control over it.

Mere proximity to a controlled substance does not constitute possession without sufficient evidence of knowing dominion or control.

### 3.c Transfer

**Transfer** means knowingly providing, delivering, distributing, selling, exchanging, or otherwise conveying possession of a controlled substance to another person.

### 3.d Intent to Profit

**Intent to profit** means a specific intent to obtain money, property, services, or another material benefit through the transfer or attempted transfer of a controlled substance to another person.

Intent to profit shall not be established solely by:

1. possession of a controlled substance;
2. personal use or intended personal use;
3. the quantity of the substance possessed;
4. the possession of money or property unrelated to a demonstrated intended transfer; or
5. the status, occupation, associations, or personal characteristics of the accused.

### 3.e Material Benefit

**Material benefit** means money, property, services, debt reduction, or another objectively identifiable economic or material advantage.

### 3.f Lawful Authority to Transfer

**Lawful authority to transfer** means a license, permit, or other authorization granted under law to manufacture, distribute, or dispense a controlled substance.

Possession for personal use remains protected by I.3.b. The absence of lawful authority to transfer does not, by itself, make such possession unlawful.

## Section 4 — Offense

A person commits the offense of **unlawful possession with intent to profit** if the person:

1. knowingly possesses a controlled substance;
2. has no lawful authority to transfer the substance (3.f); and
3. specifically intends to obtain a material benefit through its transfer or attempted transfer to another person.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 5 — No Presumption From Possession

Possession of a controlled substance shall not, by itself, create a legal presumption that the person intended to transfer the substance to another person or intended to profit from such transfer.

The government bears the burden of proving intent to profit beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 6 — Bodily Autonomy

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to establish an exception to the right of bodily autonomy beyond an exception otherwise authorized by the Constitution.

The offense created by this Act concerns possession undertaken for the purpose of obtaining a material benefit through transfer to another person. It does not, by itself, criminalize a person's private bodily decision to possess or use a substance for personal purposes.

Any application of this Act that substantially restricts a constitutionally protected right remains subject to the constitutional requirements governing restrictions upon fundamental rights.

## Section 7 — Attempt

A person who knowingly takes a substantial step toward unlawful possession with intent to profit may be prosecuted for attempt, provided that the government proves the required specific intent beyond a reasonable doubt.

Preparation alone shall not constitute a substantial step.

## Section 8 — Conspiracy

Two or more persons may be prosecuted for conspiracy to commit an offense under this Act when they knowingly agree to commit the offense and at least one conspirator takes an overt act toward its commission.

Mere association, communication, or knowledge of another person's unlawful conduct shall not constitute conspiracy without the required agreement and overt act.

A person whose only role is to acquire or receive a controlled substance for their own personal use (Article I, Section I.3.b) shall not be prosecuted for conspiracy to commit, or as an accomplice to, an offense under this Act committed by the person who transfers it. Requesting, arranging to receive, or paying for the substance does not, by itself, make that person a conspirator or accomplice.

## Section 9 — Gifts and Transfers to Minors

### 9.a Gifts Between Adults

A transfer of a controlled substance from one adult to another, without a material benefit, for the recipient's personal use, is not an offense under this Act.

### 9.b Transfer to a Minor

A person who has reached the age of majority commits the offense of **transfer to a minor** if the person knowingly gives, sells, or otherwise transfers a controlled substance to a minor, knowing that the recipient is a minor or acting in reckless disregard of that fact, whether or not for a material benefit.

This offense does not apply to:

1. a substance prescribed to the minor, or given under the direction of a licensed health professional; or
2. a parent or legal guardian administering a substance to the minor as medical care under Article I, Section I.3.a.

The minor who receives the substance commits no offense (Article I, Section I.3.b).

### 9.c Penalty

A person convicted of transfer to a minor may be sentenced to imprisonment for no more than **three years**. Where the government charges and proves beyond a reasonable doubt that the minor was below sixteen years of age, the maximum is **five years** (Article II, Section II.4.f).

Where the transfer was also for a material benefit, Section 10 applies instead, and the person shall not be punished under both (Article II, Section II.4.g).

## Section 10 — Penalty

A person convicted of unlawful possession with intent to profit may be sentenced to imprisonment for no more than **five years**, a fine of no more than **twice the material benefit sought or obtained**, or both.

Where the government charges and proves beyond a reasonable doubt that the person intended to transfer the substance to a minor, the maximum imprisonment is **ten years** (Article II, Section II.4.f).

The maximum penalty for attempt (Section 7) or conspiracy (Section 8) is **one-half** of the maximum for the completed offense.

No law shall require a minimum sentence for an offense under this Act, and a court may impose a sentence without imprisonment. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.f.

## Section 11 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. create a broader governmental power than the Constitution grants;
2. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of the offense;
3. convert lawful private possession into unlawful commercial conduct solely through legislative definition;
4. permit punishment for conduct that the Constitution expressly protects; or
5. circumvent any constitutional requirement concerning bodily autonomy, due process, equal protection, or limitations on custodial authority.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 12 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 13 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
