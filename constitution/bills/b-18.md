# BILL — SINGLE SUBJECT

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Single Subject Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to stop policies from being hidden inside unrelated bills. A bill may address more than one matter only if every matter relates to the bill's subject. A provision that does not relate, in a new law or in one already on the books, is unenforceable until it is enacted on its own, in a bill whose subject it does relate to, where it can be seen, debated, and voted on for what it is.

This Act carries out Article IV, Section IV.5.c, which requires each bill to address a reasonably identifiable legislative subject and forbids combining unrelated legislation to conceal its substance, and the public notice required by IV.5.b. It applies IV.12.a, under which a law enacted in violation of the Constitution has no legal effect to the extent of the violation.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every bill passed by Congress, including every appropriations bill and every bill enacted over a veto, whether it was enacted before or after this Act takes effect. Section 11 sets how it applies to laws enacted before that date.

A state, territory, or local government may adopt the rules of this Act for its own legislation.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(17), to carry into execution IV.5.b, IV.5.c, and IV.12.a. Section 8 is enacted as an exercise of the rulemaking power of each House under IV.4.a, and each House may change its own rules under that Section at any time, so long as the rules continue to meet Section 8.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Subject

**Subject** means the single matter of law or policy that a bill addresses, as identified under Section 5.

### 4.b Provision

**Provision** means any section, subsection, item, or sentence of a bill, including an amendment to or repeal of another law and any condition attached to an appropriation.

### 4.c Related Provision

**Related provision** means a provision that relates to the bill's subject under Section 6.

### 4.d Unrelated Provision

**Unrelated provision** means a provision that is not a related provision.

## Section 5 — Stating the Subject

### 5.a Title States the Subject

Every bill shall state its subject in its title, in plain words that a member of the public could understand without reading the bill.

The subject of a law enacted before this Act took effect is identified from its title in the same way. Where its title does not state a subject in plain words, the subject is identified as Section 5.c provides.

### 5.b More Than One Subject Named

If a title names more than one matter, and those matters do not relate to each other under Section 6, the bill's subject is the matter named first. Every provision that relates only to a matter named later is an unrelated provision.

### 5.c Subject Too Broad

A title that does not identify a reasonably identifiable legislative subject (IV.5.c), such as "government operations," "public welfare," "various matters," or "miscellaneous provisions," does not state a subject. For such a bill, the subject is the narrowest matter that fairly describes the bill's principal provisions, as shown by its title, its purpose section, and the provisions that make up most of the bill.

## Section 6 — When Provisions Relate

### 6.a Test

A provision relates to a bill's subject if a reasonable member of the public, reading the title, would expect to find it in the bill, and it:

1. carries out, defines, administers, funds, or enforces the subject;
2. sets the scope, purpose, authority, effective date, severability, or interpretation of the bill; or
3. amends or repeals another law only so far as necessary for the rest of the bill to work, including a conforming amendment that changes a cross-reference, a name, or a number.

### 6.b What Does Not Make a Provision Related

A provision does not relate to a bill's subject merely because:

1. it is administered by the same agency, or paid for from the same fund, as the subject;
2. it concerns the same group of persons, the same industry, or the same place;
3. both it and the subject serve a general aim such as public safety, the economy, or national security;
4. it was needed to win votes for the bill; or
5. the bill's title or text says that it relates.

### 6.c Appropriations Bills

The subject of an appropriations bill is the funding of the functions of government it names. An appropriations bill may set the amount, period, and purpose of each appropriation, and the terms on which the money is spent for that purpose.

A provision of an appropriations bill is an unrelated provision if it:

1. amends, repeals, or suspends any other law;
2. creates, ends, or changes a right, duty, penalty, or power that exists apart from the money appropriated; or
3. forbids the use of funds to carry out or enforce a law, where its purpose or effect is to suspend that law rather than to set the level of funding for the function that carries it out.

A provision that continues existing functions at existing levels under IV.10.e is a related provision.

### 6.d No Proof of Intent Needed

Whether a provision relates to a bill's subject depends only on the provision and the bill. No person challenging a provision need prove that it was hidden on purpose. Proof that unrelated provisions were combined in order to conceal them, to evade review, or to prevent consideration is a separate violation of IV.5.c, and may be shown in addition.

## Section 7 — Effect of an Unrelated Provision

### 7.a Unenforceable

An unrelated provision has no legal effect. No government, court, or official shall enforce, apply, or rely on it, and no person shall be bound by it, gain a right under it, or be penalized for disregarding it.

A law that an unrelated provision would have amended, repealed, or suspended continues in force as it stood before.

### 7.b Rest of the Bill

Every related provision of the bill remains in full force. An unrelated provision is not a reason to hold any related provision invalid.

### 7.c Enactment in a Proper Bill

An unrelated provision may become law only if Congress enacts it again in a separate bill whose subject it relates to, with all the steps of Article IV, Section 5 and Section 7. It then takes effect on the date that bill sets, and never earlier than that bill's enactment.

Referring to the unrelated provision, or declaring it valid, in a later bill whose subject it does not relate to does not enact it.

### 7.d Good Faith Reliance

No person shall be penalized for any act or omission taken in good faith reliance on an unrelated provision before a court found it unrelated.

## Section 8 — Procedure in Congress

### 8.a Subject Analysis

Before a final vote on a bill in either House, the nonpartisan legislative counsel of that House shall publish, with the bill (IV.5.b), a subject analysis that:

1. states the bill's subject under Section 5;
2. lists every provision of the bill, and states, in plain words, how each relates to the subject; and
3. identifies every provision that the counsel finds to be, or may be, an unrelated provision.

The analysis shall be published at least **three days** before the final vote, excluding days on which that House is not in session. Any amendment adopted after it is published shall be added to it before the final vote.

### 8.b Point of Order

Any member may raise a point of order that a provision of a pending bill is an unrelated provision. The presiding officer shall rule on it, with the advice of the legislative counsel, before the final vote, and the ruling and every vote on it shall be recorded in the journal (IV.4.c).

A ruling or vote in Congress that a provision is related does not bind any court.

## Section 9 — Judicial Review

### 9.a Who May Challenge

Any of the following may ask a court to declare that a provision is an unrelated provision:

1. any person against whom the provision is, or may be, enforced or applied;
2. any person whose right under another law the provision would amend, repeal, or suspend; and
3. any member of Congress.

Any person may also raise it as a defense in any proceeding in which the provision is applied against them.

### 9.b Standard

The court shall decide the question itself, under Sections 5 and 6, without deference to the title, to any statement in the bill, or to any ruling or vote in Congress. The government bears the burden of showing that a challenged provision is related.

### 9.c Timing

A court shall decide a challenge brought by a member of Congress, or a challenge to a provision whose enforcement would impose a penalty or end a right, within **sixty days** of its filing.

### 9.d Remedy

A court that finds a provision is an unrelated provision shall declare it unenforceable under Section 7, and may order any government not to enforce or apply it. The court may award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to a prevailing challenger.

## Section 10 — No Exemption by Implication

No later law shall be read to exempt a bill or provision from this Act unless that law names this Act and states the exemption expressly. A provision that exempts a bill from this Act relates only to a bill whose subject is this Act.

## Section 11 — Laws Enacted Before This Act

### 11.a Review of Existing Law

Within **one year** after this Act takes effect, the nonpartisan legislative counsel of each House shall jointly review every federal law then in force, and publish a list of every provision that the counsel find to be, or may be, an unrelated provision, with the reasons. The list shall be updated whenever a court finds a provision unrelated.

### 11.b Period to Re-enact

An unrelated provision of a law enacted before this Act took effect keeps its effect until **two years** after this Act takes effect, so that Congress may enact it again in a proper bill under Section 7.c. At the end of that period it has no legal effect under Section 7.a, whether or not it appears on the list, and whether or not a court has yet found it unrelated.

A court may hear a challenge to such a provision under Section 9 at any time, and its finding takes effect at the end of that period, or on the date of the finding if later.

### 11.c Restored Law

Where an unrelated provision had amended, repealed, or suspended another law, that law is restored as it stood before, subject to these limits:

1. a restored law that makes conduct an offense, or increases a penalty, applies only to conduct after it is restored, and no person shall be punished for conduct that was not an offense under the law in force when it was committed (II.7.a);
2. a restored law does not undo any final judgment, completed transaction, or payment made before it is restored; and
3. a restored right, protection, or remedy applies from the date it is restored, and to any proceeding still pending on that date.

The list under Section 11.a shall state each law that is restored, and the date it is restored.

### 11.d Earlier Changes

Where a provision of a law enacted before this Act took effect has since been enacted again in a bill whose subject it relates to, it has effect under that later enactment, and Section 11.b does not apply to it.

## Section 12 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution, and in favor of open and separate consideration of each subject of legislation.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. narrow the protection of IV.5.c or IV.5.b, or the power of a court to find any law unconstitutional under IV.12;
2. require proof of intent to find a provision unrelated;
3. permit any person to be penalized under an unrelated provision; or
4. limit the power of Congress to enact any policy in a bill whose subject it relates to.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional provision, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 13 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 14 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect ninety days after its enactment. It applies to every bill enacted on or after that date, and to every law enacted before that date as Section 11 provides. Section 8 applies only to bills still pending in Congress on that date.
