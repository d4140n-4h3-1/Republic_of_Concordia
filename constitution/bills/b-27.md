# BILL — PUBLIC PETITIONS

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Public Petitions Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to carry out Article I, Section I.1.i, so that a petition supported by enough voters receives a public hearing and a public answer from Congress, and to make signing one simple and private.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to public petitions addressed to either House of Congress.

Congress enacts this Act under Article I, Section I.1.i and Article IV, Section IV.1.b(18), and under IV.1.b(17). Sections 6 and 7 are also enacted as an exercise of the rulemaking power of each House under Article IV, Section IV.4.a.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Public Petition

**Public petition** means a written request to a House of Congress, on any subject, that states what it asks for in no more than five hundred words, names at least one voter as its sponsor, and is filed under this Act.

### 4.b Threshold

The **threshold** is the number of signatures that requires a hearing and a response: **one-quarter of one percent** of the voters registered at the most recent general election.

## Section 5 — Filing and Signatures

### 5.a Platform

The Election Authority shall run a free public platform on which any voter may file a public petition and any voter may sign one, online or on paper at any public library or election office.

### 5.b Signatures

Each voter may sign each petition once. A signature shall be verified against the voter registration records (Article III, Section III.3.c). Signatures may be gathered for **one year** after the petition is filed.

### 5.c Privacy

The names of signers shall not be published or given to any person, including Congress and the sponsors, without each signer's consent. Only the number of verified signatures, and their distribution by constituent jurisdiction, shall be published. Signature records shall be used only to verify signatures, and shall be deleted one year after the petition closes.

### 5.d No Rejection for Content

No petition shall be refused, delayed, or removed because of its subject or viewpoint (Article I, Section I.1.a). The Election Authority may refuse only a petition that contains a true threat, discloses personal information about a private person, or repeats a petition on the same request filed by the same sponsor within the previous year.

## Section 6 — Hearing

When a petition reaches the threshold, the Election Authority shall certify it to the House to which it is addressed. A petition is filed with a House, for purposes of Article I, Section I.1.i, on the day it is certified. The presiding officer shall refer it to a committee within seven days.

The committee shall hold a public hearing at which the sponsors may present the petition, in person or remotely, and answer questions. The hearing shall be held within **sixty days** after certification.

## Section 7 — Response

The House shall publish a written response within **ninety days** after the petition is filed with it (Article I, Section I.1.i). The response shall state whether the House will act on the request, what it will do, and its reasons.

A response is not a decision on the merits of any law, and no petition binds Congress to enact anything.

## Section 8 — Protection

No person shall be punished, disadvantaged, or retaliated against for filing, signing, or gathering signatures for a petition (Article I, Section I.1.i). No employer, landlord, or public body shall ask whether a person signed a petition.

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. Nothing in this Act limits the right of any person to petition any government in any other way (Article I, Section I.1.i).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment. The Election Authority shall open the platform within one year after enactment.
