# BILL — RIGHTS OF VICTIMS OF CRIME

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Victims of Crime Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to carry out Article II, Section II.1.f: to keep victims of crime informed and heard, to protect them, to make sure restitution is actually paid, and to compensate victims of violent crime for harm that restitution does not cover.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every offense prosecuted under federal law, and Section 8 applies to every victim of violent crime in Concordia as provided there.

Congress enacts this Act under Article II, Section II.1.f, and under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(3) and IV.1.b(17).

Every state and territory shall provide the rights in Article II, Section II.1.f for offenses under its own law, and the compensation that II.1.f requires, by a law it enacts. It may adopt this Act for that purpose.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Victim

**Victim** means a person who suffered physical, psychological, or financial harm as a direct result of an offense. Where the victim has died, is a minor, or cannot act, a family member (Article V, Section V.5.a) or legal guardian may exercise the victim's rights, but not a person accused of the offense.

### 4.b Violent Crime

**Violent crime** means an offense that has, as an element, the use, attempted use, or threatened use of force against a person, and any sexual offense.

## Section 5 — Information and Participation

### 5.a Notice

The prosecuting authority shall tell each victim, in plain words and in a language the victim understands (Article I, Section I.1.f), of the rights in Article II, Section II.1.f and this Act, and of how to ask for notice under item 1 of that subsection.

A victim who asks shall be notified, by the means they choose, of every public proceeding, every release or escape, and the outcome of the case.

### 5.b Being Heard

A victim may make a statement, in person or in writing, at any proceeding concerning release, a plea agreement, sentencing, or parole. The court shall consider it, together with every other matter the law requires, but the victim's views do not decide the outcome.

### 5.c Conferring With the Prosecution

The prosecution shall consult the victim before agreeing to a plea agreement or dismissing a charge, where the victim has asked to be consulted, and shall record that it did so.

## Section 6 — Protection

A court may order the accused, or any other person, not to contact or approach a victim, where needed to protect the victim from intimidation or harm. Such an order shall satisfy Article I, Section I.4.c, and an order that restricts the liberty of the accused before trial is subject to Article II, Section II.3.d.

Where the offense is a sexual offense, no government shall publish the victim's name, address, or image, or any information that would identify them, without the victim's consent.

## Section 7 — Restitution

### 7.a Order

A court sentencing a person for an offense that caused a victim financial loss, or the cost of medical care, counseling, lost income, or funeral expenses, shall order restitution for the full amount of that loss.

### 7.b Collection

The government shall collect restitution and pay it to the victim. Collection shall be set according to the person's means and shall never deprive them of the means of subsistence (Article II, Section II.1.f; II.7.f). No person shall be held in custody because they cannot pay restitution.

### 7.c Advance Payment

Where restitution for a violent crime remains unpaid ninety days after it is ordered, the Victims Compensation Fund shall pay the victim the unpaid amount, up to the limits of Section 8, and shall then collect it from the person ordered to pay.

## Section 8 — Victims Compensation Fund

### 8.a Establishment

There is established in the Treasury a **Victims Compensation Fund**, funded by appropriations made by law.

### 8.b Eligibility

Any victim of a violent crime committed in Concordia, or of a violent crime against a citizen anywhere, may apply for compensation, whether or not anyone has been charged or convicted, and whatever the victim's citizenship or immigration status.

Compensation shall not be denied because the victim did not take part in a prosecution, or because of the victim's criminal record. It may be reduced where the victim's own wrongful conduct caused the harm.

### 8.c What Is Paid

The Fund shall pay the reasonable cost of medical and mental health care, lost income, funeral expenses, and the cost of moving to safety, to the extent not paid by restitution, insurance, or another public program, up to a total for each victim set by law.

### 8.d Procedure

An application may be made within **three years** after the crime, or after the victim reaches the age of majority, whichever is later. The Fund shall decide within ninety days. A denial shall state its reasons and is subject to review in court.

### 8.e States and Territories

The federal government shall pay at least one-half of the cost of every state's and territory's compensation program that meets the standards of this Section.

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. limit any right of an accused person or of a person in custody;
2. make a victim a party to a criminal case, or give a victim control of a prosecution; or
3. permit any punishment that Article II, Section 7 forbids.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 8 applies to every violent crime committed on or after that date.
