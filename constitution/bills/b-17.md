# BILL — FREEDOM OF INFORMATION

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Freedom of Information Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to secure two freedoms: the freedom of every person to see the records of their government, and the freedom of every person to seek and receive information, including through digital networks, without blocking, filtering, or surveillance, except for illegal content.

This Act carries out the freedoms of speech and of the press in Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.1.e, which include the freedom to gather and receive information, and the privacy of personal information in Article II, Section II.6.e.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

Part One applies to every government in Concordia. Part Two applies to every government, and to every provider of internet access in Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.1.e and Article II, Section II.6.e against every level of government; under IV.1.b(6), as to internet access in interstate and foreign commerce; and under IV.1.b(17).

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Record

**Record** means any information held by a government in any form, including documents, data, email, messages, recordings, and images.

### 4.b Lawful Content

**Lawful content** means any information, in any form, other than illegal content.

### 4.c Illegal Content

**Illegal content** means only:

1. images or recordings of the sexual abuse of a minor;
2. images or recordings of a person's intimate parts or sexual activity, shared without that person's consent;
3. a true threat of violence against a specific person or group;
4. material whose distribution a court has found, after a hearing, to infringe a copyright or to be defamatory under Article I, Section I.1.e; and
5. any other content whose distribution is a crime under a law that satisfies Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.4.c.

Content is not illegal because it is false, offensive, sexual, political, or critical of any government or official.

### 4.d Internet Access Provider

**Internet access provider** means a business that provides the public with access to the internet.

**Part One — Access to Government Records**

## Section 5 — Right of Access

Every person may inspect and copy any record of any government, without stating a reason, except as Section 7 permits.

## Section 6 — Requests

### 6.a Response

A government shall provide a requested record, or a written denial stating the exemption relied on and the reasons, within **twenty working days**. It may extend that period once, by twenty working days, for a large or complex request, with written notice.

A government that does not respond in time may not withhold the record under any exemption other than Section 7(1) or 7(2).

### 6.b Fees

A government may charge no more than the reasonable cost of copying. It shall not charge for search or review time, and shall waive every fee for a requester for whom paying it would be a hardship, and for any request made in the public interest by the press (Article I, Section I.1.e).

### 6.c Partial Release

Where only part of a record is exempt, the government shall release the rest, marking what was withheld and the exemption relied on.

## Section 7 — Exemptions

A government may withhold a record, or part of it, only where its release would:

1. disclose personal information about a private person, protected by Article II, Section II.6.e, unless that person consents or the public interest in release clearly outweighs the privacy interest;
2. endanger the life or physical safety of a specific person;
3. reveal military operations, plans, or weapons designs, or intelligence sources, where release would cause serious and specific harm to the defense of Concordia;
4. interfere with a pending criminal investigation or proceeding, for no longer than the investigation or proceeding lasts; or
5. disclose a confidential source of the press, or unpublished material, protected by Article I, Section I.1.e.

No record shall be withheld because it would embarrass a government or official, reveal wrongdoing, inefficiency, or error, or reveal the use of public funds.

Every exemption under item 3 expires after **twenty-five years**, unless a court finds that release would still cause serious and specific harm.

## Section 8 — Information Commissioner

### 8.a Establishment

The **Information Commissioner** is established as an independent office of the federal government. The Commissioner shall be nominated by the President and confirmed by three-fifths of the Senate, serves a single term of seven years, and may be removed only for cause, after notice and a hearing.

### 8.b Appeals

A requester may appeal any denial, delay, or fee to the Commissioner, who shall review the withheld record itself and decide within thirty days. The Commissioner may order release. A requester or government may seek review of the Commissioner's decision in court, which shall review the record itself and decide the question without deference to the government.

## Section 9 — Records Published Without Request

Every government shall publish online, free of charge and in a searchable form:

1. every law, regulation, and binding policy;
2. every budget, appropriation, and expenditure over an amount set by law;
3. every public contract and grant, with its amount and recipient;
4. every court decision, with personal information about private persons removed; and
5. the records of its decisions and votes.

## Section 10 — Destruction of Records

No person shall destroy, alter, or conceal a record to prevent its release. A record that has been requested shall be preserved until the request and every appeal are finally resolved.

**Part Two — Freedom to Seek and Receive Information**

## Section 11 — Right to Receive Information

Every person may seek, receive, and share lawful content by any medium, including the internet, without the permission of any government.

## Section 12 — Limits on Government

No government shall:

1. block, filter, or slow access to lawful content;
2. shut down, slow, or restrict internet or telephone service in any area, including during an emergency (Emergency Powers Act, Section 7);
3. require any person to identify themselves in order to access lawful content;
4. require an internet access provider or online service to monitor what its users access, or to keep records of it; or
5. obtain a record of what a person has accessed, except under a particularized warrant (Article II, Section II.6.c).

## Section 13 — Illegal Content

### 13.a Court Order Required

A government may require the removal of, or the blocking of access to, illegal content only by a court order that identifies the specific content and the specific location where it is found. The order shall be issued after notice to the person who published the content, where that person can be identified, and an opportunity to be heard.

In an emergency involving content described in Section 4.c(1), (2), or (3), a court may issue a temporary order without notice, but a hearing shall be held within seven days.

### 13.b Limits on Orders

No order shall block an entire website, service, or network because some of its content is illegal, unless the court finds that the website, service, or network exists mainly to distribute content described in Section 4.c(1) or (2).

### 13.c Public Register

Every government shall publish each order to remove or block content, with the court and the legal basis, but without reproducing the content, in a register open to the public.

## Section 14 — Internet Access Providers

An internet access provider shall not block, slow, or give priority to lawful content because of its source, its destination, its content, or the viewpoint it expresses. A provider may manage its network to address congestion or security, if it does so equally for all lawful content of the same type and discloses how.

This Section does not prevent a provider from complying with a court order under Section 13.

## Section 15 — Minors

A public school may filter the content available on its own devices and networks to protect minors. A public library may offer filtered access for minors, but shall provide unfiltered access to lawful content for any adult on request, without asking the reason.

A parent or legal guardian may use any filtering tool they choose for a minor in their care.

## Section 16 — Remedies

A person denied access to a record, or whose access to lawful content is unlawfully blocked, filtered, slowed, or monitored, may bring a civil action. The court may order release or restoration of access, award damages, and award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to a prevailing requester or user.

An official who knowingly destroys a record to prevent its release, or who knowingly orders lawful content blocked without a court order, commits an offense punishable by imprisonment for no more than **two years**.

## Section 17 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution, and broadly, in favor of access.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. treat content as illegal because it is false, offensive, or critical of any government;
2. permit a government to withhold a record for a reason not listed in Section 7;
3. permit any search of what a person has accessed without a particularized warrant; or
4. limit any right under Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.1.e, or Article II, Section II.6.e.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 18 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 19 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 9 applies to records created after that date, and to older records within three years.
