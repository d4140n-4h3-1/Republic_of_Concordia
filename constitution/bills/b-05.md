# BILL — PERSONAL CULTIVATION LICENSING

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Personal Cultivation Licensing Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to protect the cultivation of controlled plants and fungi for personal use by establishing a license that any adult may obtain.

Article I, Section I.3.a protects the decision to possess or consume a substance for personal use. This Act protects a lawful means of obtaining such a substance without buying it, and ensures that licensing serves only to set safe and uniform limits, not to deny the right.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to cultivation for personal use anywhere within the jurisdiction of the federal government or any state, territory, or local government.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.3.a against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c).

A state, territory, or local government may give greater protection to personal cultivation than this Act gives. No such government may require a license other than the license established by this Act, or impose a limit on licensed cultivation stricter than those in Section 8.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Controlled Substance

*(Controlled substance is defined in the Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act, Section 3.a.)*

### 4.b Controlled Plant or Fungus

**Controlled plant or fungus** means a living plant or fungus that contains, or is cultivated to produce, a controlled substance.

### 4.c Cultivation

**Cultivation** means planting, growing, tending, harvesting, drying, curing, and storing a controlled plant or fungus, and the simple processing of its harvest by physical means such as trimming, grinding, or pressing.

Cultivation does not include chemical extraction using a flammable solvent, chemical synthesis, or any other process that creates a substantial risk of fire, explosion, or toxic exposure to other persons.

### 4.d Personal Use

**Personal use** means possession or consumption by the licensee, and not transfer to another person for a material benefit. *(Material benefit is defined in the Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act, Section 3.e.)*

### 4.e Licensee

**Licensee** means a person who holds a valid personal cultivation license under this Act.

### 4.f Licensing Office

**Licensing Office** means the office designated by law under Section 5.

## Section 5 — Licensing Office

Congress shall by law designate an office within an executive department to issue personal cultivation licenses under this Act. The Licensing Office shall not be part of, and shall not share records with, any agency whose principal duty is criminal investigation or prosecution, except as Section 11 permits.

The Licensing Office shall accept applications in person, by mail, and electronically, and shall make application forms available in the languages required by Article II, Section II.4.i for court proceedings.

## Section 6 — Eligibility

Every person who has reached the age of majority (Article I, Section I.4.a) shall be eligible for a personal cultivation license.

No license shall be denied because of:

1. a criminal record, including a prior conviction for a drug offense;
2. protected status (Article I, Section I.4.a);
3. immigration or residency status, if the person lawfully resides at the place of cultivation;
4. the type of controlled plant or fungus the person intends to cultivate; or
5. any reason not stated in this Section.

A license may be denied only if the applicant has not reached the age of majority, or if the applicant's license was revoked under Section 12 within the previous two years.

## Section 7 — Application, Issuance, and Fees

### 7.a Application

An application shall require only:

1. the applicant's name and date of birth;
2. the address of the place of cultivation; and
3. a statement that the applicant has read the limits in Section 8.

No application shall require the applicant to state which plants or fungi they intend to cultivate, why they intend to cultivate them, or how they intend to use them.

### 7.b Issuance

The Licensing Office shall issue the license within thirty days after receiving a complete application. If it does not issue or deny the license within that period, the license is issued by operation of law, and the applicant's copy of the application serves as the license until a license is delivered.

A denial shall be in writing and shall state the reason. An applicant may seek judicial review of a denial.

### 7.c Term and Renewal

A license is valid for five years and may be renewed by the same procedure. A licensee who moves shall notify the Licensing Office of the new place of cultivation within sixty days.

### 7.d Fees

The fee for a license or renewal shall not exceed the reasonable cost of processing it. The fee shall be waived for any applicant who states that paying it would be a hardship. *(Socioeconomic status is a protected status under Article I, Section I.4.a.)*

## Section 8 — Limits on Licensed Cultivation

### 8.a Quantity

`[DECIDE: the maximum number of plants and quantity of fungi a licensee may cultivate at one time, and the maximum quantity of harvested product a licensee may store. Consider a single household limit where more than one licensee shares a residence. Every limit must satisfy Article I, Section I.4.c.]`

### 8.b Place

Licensed cultivation shall take place at the licensee's residence, or on other land the licensee owns or lawfully controls with the owner's consent.

### 8.c Safety

A licensee shall:

1. keep controlled plants, fungi, and harvested product reasonably secured from access by minors;
2. keep controlled plants out of plain view from a public place, unless local law permits otherwise; and
3. comply with building, fire, electrical, and environmental codes that apply equally to other indoor and outdoor growing.

No safety rule adopted under this Section shall be stricter than necessary to prevent harm to other persons.

### 8.d Transfer

A license under this Act is not lawful authority to transfer a controlled substance (Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act, Section 3.f). A licensee who transfers any part of a harvest to another person for a material benefit remains subject to that Act.

## Section 9 — Protection of Licensed Cultivation

Licensed cultivation within the limits of Section 8, and possession of its harvest for personal use, shall not be a criminal offense or a civil violation under any law of the federal government or of any state, territory, or local government.

No government shall, because a person holds a license or engages in licensed cultivation:

1. seize or forfeit any property, other than controlled plants, fungi, or harvested product that exceed the limits of Section 8;
2. deny, end, or reduce public housing, public benefits, or public employment;
3. deny or revoke a professional or occupational license;
4. treat it as evidence of unfitness in any proceeding concerning custody of, or visitation with, a child, unless the cultivation is shown to have endangered the child; or
5. treat it as a factor in any immigration or citizenship decision.

## Section 10 — Searches and Probable Cause

Holding a license, cultivating within the limits of Section 8, or the odor or sight of controlled plants, fungi, or harvested product at a licensed place of cultivation shall not, alone or together, establish probable cause or reasonable grounds for a search, seizure, or arrest.

A search of a licensed place of cultivation to determine whether cultivation exceeds the limits of Section 8 requires a particularized warrant under Article II, Section II.6.c.

## Section 11 — Privacy of Records

License records are stored personal information protected by Article II, Section II.6.e.

The Licensing Office shall not disclose a license record, or confirm that a person holds a license, except:

1. to the licensee;
2. under a particularized warrant issued for the investigation of an offense under the Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act or of cultivation exceeding the limits of Section 8; or
3. to a law enforcement officer who is investigating cultivation at a particular place and who has been shown a license by a person at that place, to confirm that the license is valid.

The Licensing Office shall delete every record of an expired or surrendered license within one year after it expires or is surrendered, unless the record is subject to a pending warrant or proceeding.

## Section 12 — Unlicensed Cultivation and Revocation

### 12.a Unlicensed Cultivation

Cultivation for personal use by an adult without a license, within the limits of Section 8, is a civil infraction and not a criminal offense.

`[DECIDE: the maximum civil fine for unlicensed cultivation. Any fine must satisfy Article II, Section II.7.f.]`

No fine shall be imposed if the person obtains a license within sixty days after being notified of the infraction. Unlicensed cultivation shall not be grounds for arrest, custody, or forfeiture of property other than controlled plants, fungi, or harvested product.

### 12.b Revocation

The Licensing Office may revoke a license only if:

1. the licensee has been convicted of an offense under the Unlawful Possession With Intent to Profit Act involving the harvest of licensed cultivation; or
2. a court has found, on two separate occasions within three years, that the licensee cultivated beyond the limits of Section 8.

A revocation requires written notice of the reason and an opportunity for a hearing before an impartial decision-maker, and is subject to judicial review. A revoked license may not be renewed for two years.

## Section 13 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. limit the right to possess or consume a substance for personal use under Article I, Section I.3.a;
2. make a license a condition of that right;
3. authorize any search, seizure, or disclosure of records beyond what Article II, Section 6 permits;
4. authorize any restriction on cultivation that does not satisfy Article I, Section I.4.c; or
5. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 14 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 15 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, except that the Licensing Office shall begin accepting applications no later than one year after enactment. Until it does, Section 12.a shall not apply.
