# BILL — EQUAL TREATMENT

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Equal Treatment Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article I, Section I.4.a protects every person from discrimination by the government because of protected status. Article IV, Section IV.1.b(16) permits Congress to prohibit such discrimination by private actors in employment, housing, education, credit, and public accommodations. The purpose of this Act is to exercise that power, so that every person has an equal opportunity to work, live, learn, borrow, and be served, while respecting the freedoms of Article I, Section 1.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to private actors in Concordia in the areas listed in Sections 6 through 10. Discrimination by a government remains governed by Article I, Section I.4.a.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(16).

A state, territory, or local government may give greater protection than this Act gives, but not less.

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Protected status is defined in Article I, Section I.4.a. It includes race, color, ethnicity, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, age, religion, belief, non-belief, and socioeconomic status.)*

### 4.a Discriminate

To **discriminate** means to deny, limit, or worsen a person's access to an opportunity, a benefit, or terms or conditions, where the person's protected status, or the protected status of a person they are associated with, was a motivating factor, even if other factors also motivated the decision (Article I, Section I.4.a).

### 4.b Disparate Impact

A practice has a **disparate impact** when it is neutral on its face but falls substantially more heavily on persons of a protected status than on others.

### 4.c Reasonable Accommodation

**Reasonable accommodation** means a change in a rule, practice, schedule, or physical environment that allows a person with a disability, or a person with a religious practice, to take part equally, and that does not impose an undue hardship.

### 4.d Undue Hardship

**Undue hardship** means significant difficulty or expense, judged by the size, resources, and nature of the employer or provider.

## Section 5 — Prohibited Practices

In each area covered by Sections 6 through 10, no covered person shall:

1. discriminate against any person;
2. use a practice that has a disparate impact, unless the practice is necessary to a legitimate purpose and no less discriminatory practice would serve that purpose equally well;
3. refuse a reasonable accommodation to a person with a disability, or to a person's religious practice or religious leave;
4. permit harassment because of protected status that is severe or pervasive enough to deny a person equal access, where the covered person knows of it and fails to act; or
5. retaliate against any person for opposing a practice this Act prohibits, filing a complaint, or taking part in an investigation.

## Section 6 — Employment

This Act applies to every employer with **fifteen or more** employees, and to every employment agency and labor organization, in hiring, pay, promotion, assignment, discipline, discharge, and every other term of employment.

## Section 7 — Housing

This Act applies to the sale, rental, financing, advertising, and terms of housing, except the rental of rooms or units in a building with **four or fewer** units in which the owner lives. No advertisement for any housing may state a preference based on protected status.

## Section 8 — Education

This Act applies to every private school, college, university, and training program that is open to the public or receives public funds, in admission, discipline, financial aid, and access to programs.

## Section 9 — Credit

This Act applies to every lender and every person who arranges or decides on credit, insurance, or other financial services offered to the public.

## Section 10 — Public Accommodations

This Act applies to every business, facility, or service that offers goods, services, or access to the general public.

## Section 11 — Lawful Distinctions

None of the following is prohibited by this Act:

1. **Need and ability to pay.** Considering a person's income, assets, or credit history to determine ability to pay, in credit, insurance, or housing, if applied equally to all applicants. Offering discounts, aid, or programs to persons in need, consistent with Article I, Section I.4.a.
2. **Age.** Housing reserved for older persons; age requirements set by law, including the age of majority; and age-based programs that address a need under Article I, Section I.4.a.
3. **Genuine job requirements.** A requirement of sex, religion, age, or disability that is genuinely necessary to perform the job, such as an actor portraying a character or a counselor for survivors of sexual violence. Race, color, ethnicity, and national origin are never genuine job requirements.
4. **Language.** Language requirements permitted by Article I, Section I.1.f.
5. **Remedies.** Measures that remedy specific, identified discrimination under Article I, Section I.4.a.

This Act does not decide whether a facility may be separated by sex. That question is governed by Article I, Section I.4.c.

## Section 12 — Religious Organizations and Expression

A religious organization may prefer members of its own religion for positions that lead worship, teach the faith, or carry out its religious mission, and may conduct its worship and religious teaching according to its beliefs.

An expressive association may make decisions about its leaders and message according to its viewpoint.

Nothing in this Act requires any person to express a message they do not hold. *(See Article I, Section 1.)*

## Section 13 — Equal Treatment Commission

### 13.a Establishment

The **Equal Treatment Commission** is established as an independent office of the federal government. It shall investigate complaints, attempt to resolve them by agreement, issue guidance, and bring civil actions to enforce this Act.

### 13.b Members

The Commission consists of five members serving staggered five-year terms, nominated by the President and confirmed by a majority of the Senate. No more than three members may belong to the same political party. A member may be removed only for cause, after notice and a hearing.

### 13.c Reports

The Commission shall publish an annual report of complaints received, their outcomes, and patterns of discrimination, with no information that identifies any complainant.

## Section 14 — Remedies

### 14.a Complaint

A person who believes they have been subjected to a practice this Act prohibits may file a complaint with the Commission within **two years**, or bring a civil action directly within **three years**.

### 14.b Civil Action

A court may award hiring, reinstatement, or promotion; back pay; damages for actual loss and emotional distress; an order to stop the practice; and reasonable attorney's fees and costs to a prevailing complainant.

Where discrimination was intentional and willful, the court may award additional damages of up to **twice** the actual damages.

### 14.c No Criminal Penalty

A violation of this Act is not a criminal offense.

## Section 15 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. restrict any freedom of Article I, Section 1, including speech, press, religion, and language;
2. require any person to express a message they do not hold;
3. permit discrimination that Article I, Section I.4.a prohibits to a government; or
4. create a presumption against any party.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 16 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 17 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
