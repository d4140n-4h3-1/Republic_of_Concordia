# BILL — PUBLIC INTEGRITY

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Public Integrity Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to let the public see whether officials have a financial stake in the decisions they make, and to keep officials from deciding matters in which they have one.

This Act carries out the rules on conflicts of interest and financial disclosure in Article IV, Section IV.8.c, Article V, Section V.8.a, and Article VI, Section VI.4.c, and extends them to every public official.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every public official of the federal government.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(17), and, as to members and officers of Congress, under IV.8.c.

A state, territory, or local government may adopt this Act for its own officials.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Public Official

**Public official** means every elected federal official, every federal judge, every official confirmed by the Senate, and every head of an executive department or independent office.

### 4.b Personal Financial Interest

**Personal financial interest** means an interest of the official, or of a family member (Article V, Section V.5.a), that a decision could reasonably be expected to increase or decrease in value, other than an interest the official shares equally with the general public.

## Section 5 — Disclosure

Every public official shall publicly disclose their income, assets, debts, and other financial interests each year, in a free, searchable public register. Each disclosure shall remain public for **five years** after the official leaves office.

The register shall not publish an official's home address, account numbers, or other information whose publication would endanger the official's safety (Article II, Section II.6.a).

## Section 6 — Recusal

No public official shall take part in a decision in which they have a personal financial interest. An official who is required to decide a matter in which they have such an interest, because no one else may lawfully decide it, shall disclose the interest publicly before deciding.

Recusal of judges remains governed by Article VI, Section VI.4.b.

## Section 7 — Enforcement

A knowing failure to disclose, or a knowing false disclosure, is subject to a civil fine, imposed after notice and a hearing under Article II, Section II.1.a. Every fine shall satisfy Article II, Section II.7.f.

A decision taken in violation of Section 6 may be challenged in court by any person it affects, and a court may set it aside.

## Section 8 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 9 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 10 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
