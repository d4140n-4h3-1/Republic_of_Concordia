# BILL — MILITARY JUSTICE

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Military Justice Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to establish the military courts permitted by Article V, Section V.7.c, so that discipline in the armed forces is kept by fair trials before independent judges, free from the influence of commanders, with every right of an accused person.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every member of the armed forces on active duty.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(13) and IV.1.b(17), and under Article V, Section V.7.c.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Military Offense

**Military offense** means an offense defined in Section 6.

### 4.b Commander

**Commander** means any member of the armed forces with authority over other members, and any civilian official in the chain of command.

## Section 5 — Jurisdiction

### 5.a Military Courts

Military courts may try a member of the armed forces on active duty only for:

1. a military offense; and
2. another offense committed in the course of military duty, other than one listed in Section 5.b.

### 5.b Civilian Courts

The following belong to the civilian courts, and no military court shall try them:

1. any offense by or against a civilian (Article V, Section V.7.a);
2. any offense against a civilian within Concordia not connected with military duty, and any offense against a civilian while supporting civilian authorities (Article V, Section V.7.c);
3. any offense by a member of the reserve forces not on active duty; and
4. any sexual offense, wherever and against whomever committed.

### 5.c Choice of Court

Where an offense may be tried in either a military or a civilian court, it shall be tried in a civilian court if the civilian prosecutor brings charges within ninety days after learning of it. No person shall be tried in both for the same act (Article II, Section II.4.g).

## Section 6 — Military Offenses

A member of the armed forces on active duty commits a military offense who knowingly:

1. deserts, or is absent without leave for more than thirty days;
2. disobeys a lawful order, as defined in Section 10;
3. mutinies, by refusing in concert with others to obey lawful orders, or by using force against a superior;
4. is unfit for duty through impairment while on duty (Article I, Section I.3.b);
5. abandons a post, or endangers others by neglect of a safety duty; or
6. strikes or threatens a superior or a subordinate.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt. Maximum penalties shall be set by law, shall satisfy Article II, Section 7, and shall not include death (Article V, Section V.7.c).

## Section 7 — Military Judges

### 7.a Appointment

Military judges are appointed from members of the armed forces who are licensed to practice law and have at least eight years of legal experience, by the Chief Military Judge, after open competition.

The **Chief Military Judge** is nominated by the President and confirmed by a majority of the Senate (Article V, Section V.4.a), for a single term of seven years.

### 7.b Independence

Every military judge serves a fixed term of six years (Article V, Section V.7.c), may be reappointed once, and may be removed during the term only for cause, after a hearing before three other military judges, subject to review by a civilian court.

No commander shall rate a military judge, decide their promotion or assignment, or reduce their pay. A military judge's career is managed only by the Chief Military Judge.

## Section 8 — Prosecution

The **Office of the Military Prosecutor** is established outside the chain of command. It alone decides whether to charge a military offense or another offense within Section 5.a. No commander may order, prevent, or withdraw a charge.

The Chief Military Prosecutor is nominated by the President and confirmed by a majority of the Senate, for a single term of seven years, and may be removed only for cause stated in this Section, after written notice, subject to judicial review (Article V, Section V.4.c).

## Section 9 — Trial

### 9.a Rights of the Accused

The accused has every right of an accused person in Article II, including the right to counsel at public expense from a defense service independent of the chain of command (II.4.a), to remain silent (II.4.b), and to appeal (II.4.h).

### 9.b The Panel

Guilt shall be decided by a panel of **eight** members of the armed forces, chosen at random from members who are not in the accused's chain of command and have no connection to the case. Where the accused asks, at least four members shall be of the accused's rank or below. A conviction requires the agreement of every member (Article V, Section V.7.c).

### 9.c Pretrial Custody

A member may be held before trial only under Article II, Sections II.2.d and II.3.d, with judicial review by a military judge within forty-eight hours (II.3.c).

## Section 10 — Lawful Orders

A **lawful order** is an order that is within the authority of the person who gives it and does not require the person who receives it to violate the Constitution or a law.

No member of the armed forces shall be punished for refusing an order that is not lawful, including an order to use force against a peaceful assembly, to influence an election, or to enforce civilian law outside Article V, Section V.7.a. Obedience to an order is no defense to an offense where the person knew, or must have known, that the order was unlawful.

## Section 11 — Appeals

Every conviction may be appealed to a civilian court of appeals designated by law, and then to the Supreme Court (Article V, Section V.7.c; Article VI, Section VI.2.k).

## Section 12 — Command Influence

No commander shall direct or attempt to influence the decision of a military judge, prosecutor, defense counsel, panel member, or witness, or retaliate against any of them for their part in a case. A person who does so knowingly commits an offense punishable by imprisonment for no more than **three years**, which shall be tried in a civilian court.

## Section 13 — Discipline Without Trial

A commander may impose minor discipline for a breach of duty that is not a military offense, limited to a written reprimand, extra duties of no more than fourteen days, or a loss of no more than seven days' pay. Minor discipline shall not include custody of any kind. A member may refuse minor discipline and ask to be tried instead.

## Section 14 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to permit the trial of a civilian by a military court, a sentence of death, or any restriction on a right of members of the armed forces beyond what military duty requires and Article I, Section I.4.c permits (Article V, Section V.7.c).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 15 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 16 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, and applies only to offenses committed on or after that date (Article II, Section II.7.a).
