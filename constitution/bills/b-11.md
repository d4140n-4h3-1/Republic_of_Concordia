# BILL — FIREARM LICENSING

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Firearm Licensing Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article X, Section X.2 permits a law to require a license to purchase arms, background checks, safe storage, and limits on bearing arms in certain places. The purpose of this Act is to carry out those conditions once, uniformly, and no further than Article X allows, while keeping the protections of X.3 against registries and selective enforcement.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies throughout Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article X against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c), and under IV.1.b(17).

This Act is the only license to purchase in Concordia. No state, territory, or local government shall require any other license, permit, or registration to acquire, keep, or bear arms.

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Arms, keep, and bear are defined in Article X, Section X.1.a. Firearm, and frame or receiver, are defined in the Firearm Definition Act, Sections 4 and 5.)*

### 4.a License

**License** means a license to purchase issued under this Act.

### 4.b Licensing Office

**Licensing Office** means the office designated by law under Section 5.

### 4.c Prohibited Persons Index

**Prohibited Persons Index** means the list of prohibited persons kept under Section 7.

### 4.d Transfer

**Transfer** means a sale, gift, loan, trade, or other transfer of possession of a firearm, including its frame or receiver.

## Section 5 — Licensing Office

Congress shall by law designate an office within an executive department to issue licenses. The Licensing Office shall not be part of, or share records with, any agency whose principal duty is criminal investigation or prosecution, except under a particularized warrant (Article X, Section X.3.b).

The Licensing Office shall accept applications in person, by mail, and electronically, and shall provide forms and interpretation in the languages required by Article I, Section I.1.f.

## Section 6 — The License

### 6.a When a License Is Required

No person shall acquire a firearm by transfer without a valid license, except by a transfer exempt under Section 9.b.

No license is required to keep or bear a firearm that a person lawfully holds, or to acquire ammunition or parts other than a frame or receiver (Article X, Section X.2.a).

### 6.b Application

An application shall require only:

1. the applicant's name, date of birth, and address;
2. proof that the applicant has reached the age of majority and lawfully resides in Concordia; and
3. a certificate of completion of the course under Section 8.

No application shall ask why the applicant wants a license, what arms the applicant owns or intends to acquire, or for references from other persons.

### 6.c Issuance

The Licensing Office shall check the applicant against the Prohibited Persons Index and shall issue the license unless the applicant is listed in it. No license shall be denied for any other reason (Article X, Section X.2.a).

The license shall be issued or denied within thirty days. A license not acted on within that time is issued by operation of law, and the applicant's copy of the application serves as the license until a license is delivered.

A denial shall be in writing and shall state the reason. The applicant may seek judicial review, which the court shall decide within thirty days.

### 6.d Fees

The fee shall not exceed the reasonable cost of processing, and shall be waived for any applicant who states that paying it would be a hardship.

### 6.e Term and Renewal

A license is valid for five years. It may be renewed by a new check against the Prohibited Persons Index. The course need not be repeated.

### 6.f Suspension

A license is suspended only while the holder is a prohibited person under Article X, Section X.2.b. The Licensing Office shall give the holder written notice of the suspension and the reason, and shall restore the license as soon as the prohibition ends.

## Section 7 — Prohibited Persons Index

### 7.a Contents

The Prohibited Persons Index lists only persons who are prohibited under Article X, Section X.2.b, and for each the court, the reason, and the date the prohibition ends, if known. It shall not list any arms.

### 7.b Entries and Removal

A person may be entered only by the court that issued the conviction, sentence, protective order, or finding that makes the person prohibited. The entry shall be removed automatically when the prohibition ends, including when a sentence ends, an order expires, or a right is restored under X.2.c.

### 7.c Access and Correction

Every person may learn whether they are listed, and why. A person may ask the listing court to correct an error, and the court shall decide within fourteen days.

### 7.d Privacy

The Index is stored personal information under Article II, Section II.6.e. It may be used only to carry out this Act, and shall not be disclosed except under a particularized warrant.

## Section 8 — Safety Course

The course shall teach the safe handling and storage of firearms and the law of self-defense under Article I, Section I.3.a, including the Defensive Use of Unlawful Weapons Act.

The course shall be offered free of charge, at reasonable times and places, in person and, where practicable, remotely, and shall not exceed eight hours (Article X, Section X.2.a). The Licensing Office may certify instructors to teach it.

Completion shall be shown by a certificate, which does not expire.

## Section 9 — Transfers

### 9.a Confirming a License

Before transferring a firearm, the transferor shall confirm that the recipient's license is valid, through a confirmation service run by the Licensing Office.

The service shall answer only whether the license is valid. It shall not ask for or receive any information about the firearm, and shall not keep any record of the request (Article X, Section X.2.f).

### 9.b Exempt Transfers

No license or confirmation is required for:

1. a transfer between family members;
2. a temporary transfer for immediate self-defense, or for hunting or sport under the transferor's direct supervision; or
3. a transfer to or from a licensed dealer for repair, storage, or return.

**Family member** means a spouse, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, or grandchild, including by marriage or adoption.

**Licensed dealer** means a business licensed by law to sell or repair firearms.

## Section 10 — Places

Bearing a firearm is prohibited, other than by a law enforcement officer on duty, in the places listed in Article X, Section X.2.d(1) through (6), when the place is clearly marked.

A state, territory, or local government may prohibit bearing arms at a public assembly or demonstration only by a law that applies equally to every assembly, regardless of viewpoint (X.2.d).

No other place shall be closed to the bearing of arms except by a law that satisfies Article I, Section I.4.c.

## Section 11 — Storage

A person who keeps a firearm where a minor or a prohibited person lives, or is likely to have access to it, shall store it securely by a lock or locked container whenever it is not in the person's immediate control. This Section does not prevent a person from keeping a firearm immediately available for self-defense (Article X, Section X.2.e).

## Section 12 — Offenses and Penalties

### 12.a Offenses

1. **Unlicensed acquisition:** knowingly acquiring a firearm by a non-exempt transfer without a valid license.
2. **Unconfirmed transfer:** knowingly transferring a firearm by a non-exempt transfer without confirming the recipient's license.
3. **Transfer to a prohibited person:** knowingly transferring a firearm to a person the transferor knows is prohibited under Article X, Section X.2.b.
4. **Possession while prohibited:** keeping or bearing a firearm while knowing that one is prohibited under X.2.b.
5. **False application:** knowingly making a false statement in an application.
6. **Prohibited place:** knowingly bearing a firearm in a place prohibited under Section 10.
7. **Unsafe storage:** knowingly or recklessly failing to store a firearm as Section 11 requires.

### 12.b Penalties

| Offense | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Unlicensed acquisition, unconfirmed transfer, false application | Imprisonment for one year |
| Transfer to a prohibited person | Imprisonment for five years |
| Possession while prohibited | Imprisonment for three years |
| Prohibited place | A civil fine for a first violation; imprisonment for six months for a later violation |
| Unsafe storage | A civil fine; imprisonment for two years if a minor or prohibited person obtained the firearm because of the failure and used it to injure a person |

No law shall require a minimum sentence for an offense under this Act. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.f.

### 12.c Seizure

A firearm may be seized only as Article X, Section X.3.a permits. A court that finds a person prohibited shall allow the person to transfer their firearms to a family member who is not prohibited, to another person who holds a valid license, or to store them with a licensed dealer, until the prohibition ends.

## Section 13 — Equal Application

No condition of this Act shall be applied selectively because of protected status, viewpoint, political belief or affiliation, or the lawful exercise of any other constitutional right (Article X, Section X.3.c).

## Section 14 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. require a license to keep or bear lawfully held arms;
2. create any record of the arms a person owns or acquires;
3. permit the denial of a license for a reason not stated in Article X, Section X.2.a; or
4. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 15 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 16 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, except that Sections 6.a and 12.a(1) and (2) take effect one year later, so that the Licensing Office and the course are available before a license is required.
