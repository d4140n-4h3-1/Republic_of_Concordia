# BILL — CENSUS AND APPORTIONMENT

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Census and Apportionment Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to carry out Article VII, Section VII.2.b: to fix the number of Representatives by a rule that grows with the population, and to set how the census counts people and how seats are shared among the constituent jurisdictions.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

Congress enacts this Act under Article VII, Section VII.2.b and Article IV, Section IV.1.b(18), and under IV.1.b(17).

## Section 4 — Number of Representatives

The number of Representatives is the cube root of the population of Concordia counted at the most recent census, rounded to the nearest whole number, but never fewer than the number of constituent jurisdictions.

Under Article VII, Section VII.2.b, the number takes effect only at the apportionment that follows each census.

## Section 5 — The Census

### 5.a Census Day

The census counts every person residing in each constituent jurisdiction on census day, the first day of the census year, regardless of citizenship or immigration status (Article VII, Section VII.2.b).

### 5.b Where a Person Is Counted

A person is counted at their usual residence, except that:

1. a person in custody is counted at their last residence before custody, so that no district gains population from a prison or detention facility;
2. a member of the armed forces stationed outside Concordia is counted at their last residence in Concordia;
3. a student is counted where they live while studying; and
4. a person without a home is counted where they are found on census day.

### 5.c Questions

The census shall ask only what is needed to count the population and to produce statistics that a law requires. Every question, and the reason for it, shall be published at least two years before census day. No census shall ask a person's political opinion, party affiliation, or how they voted.

### 5.d Confidentiality

Information about a person collected by the census shall be used only to produce statistics, shall not be given to any other part of any government, and shall not be used against any person (Article VII, Section VII.2.b). Published statistics shall not identify any person. A census record that identifies a person shall be destroyed, or sealed for **seventy-five** years, as law provides.

A person who knowingly discloses or uses census information about a person in violation of this Section commits an offense punishable by imprisonment for no more than **two years**.

### 5.e Answering

No person shall be punished for declining to answer a census question, other than a civil fine set by law for refusing to state the number of persons in a household.

## Section 6 — Apportionment

### 6.a Method of Equal Proportions

The Election Authority shall apportion the seats under Article VII, Section VII.2.b by the method of equal proportions:

1. each constituent jurisdiction first receives one seat;
2. each remaining seat is given, one at a time, to the jurisdiction with the highest priority value, calculated as its population divided by the square root of n × (n + 1), where n is the number of seats it already holds; and
3. this continues until every seat is given.

### 6.b Publication

The Election Authority shall publish the population of each jurisdiction, every priority value, and the resulting apportionment, so that any person can check the calculation.

### 6.c Review

Any voter may challenge the count or the apportionment in court within ninety days after publication. The court shall decide on an expedited basis.

## Section 7 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 8 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 9 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment. Section 4 first applies at the apportionment that follows the first census held after enactment (Article VII, Section VII.2.b).
