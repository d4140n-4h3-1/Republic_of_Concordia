# BILL — PUBLIC PARTICIPATION PROTECTION

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Public Participation Protection Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article I, Section I.1.e requires Congress to provide by law for the early dismissal of any civil action brought to punish or deter the lawful exercise of the freedom of the press. The purpose of this Act is to provide that dismissal, and to extend the same protection to speech, peaceful assembly, and petitions to the government on public matters, so that no person can be silenced by the cost of defending a lawsuit that cannot succeed.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every civil action in every court of Concordia, and of every state, territory, and local government.

Congress enacts this Act under Article I, Section I.1.e and Article IV, Section IV.1.b(18), and under IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.1.e against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c).

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Protected Activity

**Protected activity** means:

1. gathering, publishing, broadcasting, or distributing news, information, or opinion under Article I, Section I.1.e;
2. speech or expression on a matter of public concern under Article I, Section I.1.a;
3. peaceful assembly; and
4. a statement made to, or in, a legislature, court, executive body, or other proceeding of any government, or concerning an issue under its consideration.

### 4.b Matter of Public Concern

**Matter of public concern** means a matter of political, social, economic, scientific, or community interest, including the conduct of a public official or public figure, public health or safety, and the conduct of a business toward the public.

### 4.c Claim

**Claim** means any cause of action, including a counterclaim or cross-claim, for damages or for an order restricting future activity.

## Section 5 — Motion to Dismiss

### 5.a Filing

A person against whom a claim is brought may file a motion to dismiss it under this Act within sixty days after being served, or later if the court permits.

### 5.b Stay

Filing the motion stops all discovery, and all other proceedings on the claim, until the motion is decided. The court may allow limited discovery that is essential to deciding the motion.

### 5.c Hearing and Decision

The court shall hear the motion within thirty days after it is filed, and shall decide it within thirty days after the hearing.

### 5.d Standard

The court shall dismiss the claim if:

1. the moving party shows that the claim is based on protected activity; and
2. the party bringing the claim does not then establish, by admissible evidence, a probability that it will prevail on each element of the claim, including, where Article I, Section I.1.e requires it, that a statement was false and was made with knowledge of its falsity or reckless disregard for whether it was false.

The court shall also dismiss the claim if the moving party establishes a defense that defeats it as a matter of law.

## Section 6 — Costs and Sanctions

### 6.a Fees for a Successful Motion

A person whose motion is granted, in whole or in part, shall recover reasonable attorney's fees and costs from the party who brought the dismissed claim.

### 6.b Frivolous Motions

If the court finds that a motion was frivolous or filed only to cause delay, it shall award the responding party reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

### 6.c Repeated Claims

A court that dismisses claims under this Act against the same party bringing them three times within five years may require that party to obtain the court's permission before filing any new claim based on protected activity.

## Section 7 — Appeal

A party may appeal the grant or denial of a motion immediately. The appeal stops proceedings on the claim, and the appellate court shall decide it within sixty days.

## Section 8 — Exclusions

This Act does not apply to:

1. a claim brought by the government to enforce a law, other than a claim the Constitution prohibits;
2. a claim concerning a statement made to sell or lease the speaker's own goods or services, unless the statement concerns a matter of public concern beyond the transaction; or
3. a claim for bodily injury, or for a threat of violence.

No government shall bring a claim for defamation (Article I, Section I.1.e).

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution, and broadly, to protect the rights in Article I, Section 1.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to limit any other defense, or any right under Article I.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment and applies to every claim pending on or after that date.
