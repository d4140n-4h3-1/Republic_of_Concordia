# BILL — DATA PROTECTION

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Data Protection Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to carry out Article II, Section II.6.f, so that every person controls the personal information that businesses collect about them: what is collected, how it is used, who receives it, and when it is deleted.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every organization, and every natural person acting in the course of a business, that collects, uses, keeps, or shares personal information about any person in Concordia. Each is a **data holder**.

This Act does not govern access by a government to information held by a data holder, which remains governed by Article II, Sections II.6.b through II.6.e.

Congress enacts this Act under Article II, Section II.6.f and Article IV, Section IV.1.b(18), and under IV.1.b(6) and IV.1.b(17).

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Personal information is defined in Article II, Section II.6.a. Organization is defined in Article I, Section I.0.a.)*

### 4.a Consent

**Consent** means a clear, specific, and informed statement or act by which a person agrees to a particular use of their personal information. Silence, a pre-ticked box, continued use of a service, or agreement to general terms of service is not consent.

### 4.b Sale

**Sale** means giving personal information to another person in exchange for money or any other benefit, including access to advertising or other data.

### 4.c Breach

**Breach** means any loss of, or access to, personal information that the data holder did not authorize.

## Section 5 — Lawful Uses

### 5.a The Constitutional Rule

A data holder may collect, use, keep, or share personal information only as Article II, Section II.6.f permits: with consent, as needed to provide a good or service the person asked for or to carry out a transaction with them, or as this Section permits.

### 5.b Uses Permitted by This Act

For purposes of Article II, Section II.6.f(3), a data holder may also use personal information, only as far as necessary:

1. to comply with a duty imposed by law, such as keeping tax or employment records;
2. to protect the life or physical safety of any person in an emergency; and
3. to protect the security of its own systems, and to prevent fraud against the person or itself, in the service the person asked for.

Information used under item 3 shall not be used for any other purpose.

### 5.c Limits on Every Use

A data holder shall collect no more personal information than the purpose requires, keep it no longer than the purpose requires, and use it for no other purpose without new consent.

### 5.d Consent Not a Condition

A data holder shall not refuse a good or service, or charge more for it, because a person declines consent to a use that is not needed to provide it.

### 5.e Sale

No personal information shall be sold without the person's express consent to that sale, given separately from any other consent.

## Section 6 — Rights of Persons

Every person may, free of charge, and with an answer within **thirty days**:

1. learn what personal information a data holder holds about them, where it came from, how it is used, and to whom it has been given;
2. have inaccurate information corrected;
3. have information deleted, unless a law requires that it be kept; and
4. receive a copy of information they provided, in a common electronic format, so that they may take it elsewhere.

No person shall be denied a good or service, or treated worse, for exercising a right under this Section.

## Section 7 — Security and Breaches

A data holder shall protect personal information with security measures suited to its sensitivity.

A data holder that learns of a breach shall notify the Information Commissioner within **seventy-two hours**, and every person whose information was affected without unreasonable delay, stating what was lost and what the person can do.

## Section 8 — Enforcement

### 8.a Authority

The Information Commissioner (Freedom of Information Act, Section 8) is the independent authority required by Article II, Section II.6.f. The Commissioner shall receive complaints, investigate, issue guidance, and enforce this Act.

### 8.b Orders and Penalties

After notice and a hearing under Article II, Section II.1.a, the Commissioner may order a data holder to stop a use, delete information, or comply with a right under Section 6, and may impose a civil penalty of no more than **four percent** of the data holder's annual revenue, or an amount set by law for a data holder with little or no revenue. In setting a penalty, the Commissioner shall consider the harm, the number of persons affected, whether the violation was intentional, and the data holder's size. Every penalty shall satisfy Article II, Section II.7.f, and is subject to judicial review.

### 8.c Civil Action

A person whose rights under this Act are violated may bring a civil action for damages, including for emotional distress, an order to comply, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs. A violation is not a criminal offense.

## Section 9 — Limits

Nothing in this Act restricts news activity, the speech or publication of any person (Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.1.e), or research for academic, historical, or statistical purposes that keeps personal information secure and does not publish it in a form that identifies any person.

A natural person's purely personal or household use of personal information, such as keeping contacts or family photographs, is not governed by this Act.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution, and in favor of the person whose information is concerned.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 11 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 12 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect one year after its enactment, so that data holders may prepare.
