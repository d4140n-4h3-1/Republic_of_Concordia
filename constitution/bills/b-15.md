# BILL — FAIR COMPETITION

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Fair Competition Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to ensure that every person can compete in sport according to their ability, to permit divisions by sex where they serve fairness or safety, and to forbid any physical inspection of an athlete's body to decide which division they may enter.

This Act protects the physical integrity and privacy guaranteed by Article I, Section I.3.a and Article II, Section II.6.a, and the equal protection guaranteed by Article I, Section I.4.a.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every organized athletic competition held or sanctioned by:

1. any government, including public schools and universities;
2. any private school, college, university, or training program covered by the Equal Treatment Act, Section 8; and
3. any league, association, or event that is open to the public or crosses the boundary of a constituent jurisdiction or of Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Sections I.3.a and I.4.a against every level of government; under IV.1.b(16), as to education and public accommodations; and under IV.1.b(6), as to competition in interstate and foreign commerce.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Competition

**Competition** means an organized athletic contest, league, season, or event to which this Act applies.

### 4.b Open Division

**Open division** means a division that any person may enter, in which athletes are grouped only by ability.

### 4.c Ability

**Ability** means measured athletic performance, such as times, distances, scores, results, or rankings, and, where a sport uses them, weight, age, or disability classifications.

### 4.d Sex Division

**Sex division** means a division limited to athletes of one sex.

*(Sex is defined in Article I, Section I.4.a as biological sex characteristics.)*

### 4.e Physical Inspection

**Physical inspection** means any visual or physical examination of a person's genitals or body, any genetic or chromosomal test, any hormone test, or any other medical procedure, used to decide or verify a person's sex.

## Section 5 — Ban on Physical Inspection

No person shall require, request, perform, or arrange a physical inspection of any athlete, or of any person seeking to compete, as a condition of entering, remaining in, or winning a competition.

No athlete shall be required to disclose medical records, or to answer questions about their body, to establish eligibility for a division.

This Section applies to every athlete, of every age, in every division.

## Section 6 — Open Divisions

Every competition that has a sex division shall also have an open division in the same sport, at the same level, and at the same event.

The open division shall receive at least the same standing as any sex division at that event, including scheduling, facilities, coaching, officiating, publicity, titles, and prize money in proportion to the number of participants.

No person shall be excluded from an open division for any reason other than ability, or a rule of conduct that applies equally to all.

## Section 7 — Sex Divisions

### 7.a When Permitted

A competition may have sex divisions where they serve fairness or safety in that sport. Each sex division is a distinction based on sex that shall satisfy Article I, Section I.4.c.

### 7.b Eligibility

Eligibility for a sex division shall be shown only by the sex recorded in an official identity document or birth record, or by the athlete's written statement where no such document exists.

A challenge to an athlete's eligibility shall be decided only on documents, and never by a physical inspection. The person who challenges bears the burden of proof. A challenge brought to harass an athlete, or brought without a reasonable basis, shall be dismissed, and the athlete shall recover reasonable costs.

### 7.c Privacy

An athlete's eligibility, any challenge, and its outcome shall be kept confidential, except as needed to decide the challenge.

## Section 8 — Ability Classification

A competition may group athletes by ability in any division, including by performance tiers, weight classes, age groups, and disability classifications, if the classification applies equally to all athletes in that division.

## Section 9 — Harassment and Retaliation

No person shall harass an athlete because of their sex, gender identity, or appearance, or retaliate against any person for refusing a physical inspection or reporting a violation of this Act.

## Section 10 — Remedies

A person harmed by a violation of this Act may file a complaint with the Equal Treatment Commission (Equal Treatment Act, Section 13), or bring a civil action. The court may award damages, including for emotional distress, an order to stop the violation, restoration of results, titles, or eligibility, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

A government body, school, or organization that requires a physical inspection shall be ineligible for any public funding for athletics until it ends the practice.

A violation of this Act is not a criminal offense.

## Section 11 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. permit any physical inspection of any person;
2. permit any sex division that does not satisfy Article I, Section I.4.c;
3. exclude any person from an open division because of sex or gender identity; or
4. limit any right under Article I, Sections I.3.a and I.4.a.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 12 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 13 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 5 takes effect on enactment.
