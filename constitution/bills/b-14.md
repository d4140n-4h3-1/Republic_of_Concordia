# BILL — FREEDOM FROM SERVITUDE

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Freedom from Servitude Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article I, Sections I.1.c and I.1.d prohibit slavery, serfdom, and involuntary servitude everywhere in Concordia, bind private actors as well as the government, and require Congress to enact laws enforcing them, including remedies for those held in servitude. The purpose of this Act is to make those prohibitions enforceable, to protect and compensate survivors, and to keep forced labor out of custody and out of public contracts.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every person, and to every government, in Concordia, and to any citizen or business of Concordia acting outside it.

Congress enacts this Act under Article I, Section I.1.d and Article IV, Section IV.1.b(18), and under IV.1.b(6) and IV.1.b(15).

## Section 4 — Definitions

*(Involuntary servitude and debt bondage are defined in Article I, Section I.1.d. Slavery and serfdom are prohibited by Article I, Section I.1.c.)*

### 4.a Coercion

**Coercion** means force; a threat of physical, financial, reputational, or legal harm to a person or their family; a threat to report a person to any authority because of their immigration status; or any scheme intended to make a person believe that they or another person would suffer serious harm if they stopped working.

### 4.b Identity Document

**Identity document** means a passport, visa, identity card, birth certificate, work permit, or other government-issued document that establishes a person's identity or legal status.

### 4.c Survivor

**Survivor** means a person who has been held in slavery, serfdom, involuntary servitude, or debt bondage, or subjected to an offense under Section 5.

## Section 5 — Offenses

1. **Slavery or serfdom:** holding, buying, selling, transferring, or treating a person as property, or holding a person in serfdom (I.1.c).
2. **Involuntary servitude:** obtaining a person's labor or services by coercion, abuse of legal process, or debt bondage (I.1.d).
3. **Trafficking:** recruiting, transporting, harboring, or receiving a person, knowing that the person will be subjected to an offense under items 1 or 2.
4. **Document withholding:** taking, hiding, or destroying a person's identity document to keep the person working or to prevent them from leaving.
5. **Benefiting:** knowingly receiving money or anything of value from an offense under items 1 through 3.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Section 6 — Penalties

| Offense | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Slavery or serfdom | Imprisonment for twenty-five years |
| Involuntary servitude, or trafficking | Imprisonment for twenty years |
| Any offense under items 1 through 3 committed against a minor, or involving sexual exploitation, where charged and proven beyond a reasonable doubt (Article II, Section II.4.f) | Imprisonment for twenty-five years |
| Document withholding | Imprisonment for five years |
| Benefiting | Imprisonment for ten years |

A person convicted under this Act shall also pay restitution under Section 8.

No law shall require a minimum sentence for an offense under this Act. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, II.7.c, and II.7.f.

## Section 7 — Protection of Survivors

### 7.a No Punishment for Compelled Acts

No survivor shall be prosecuted or punished for an act they were compelled to commit as a direct result of an offense under this Act, including an immigration, labor, or document offense.

### 7.b Immigration Protection

A survivor who is not a citizen shall not be placed in status-based custody (Article II, Section II.2.l) or removed from Concordia because of their immigration status while a criminal or civil case under this Act is pending, and may apply for lawful residence. Cooperation with an investigation shall not be required as a condition of that protection.

### 7.c Services

The government shall offer every survivor safe shelter, medical and mental health care, legal assistance, and interpretation (Article I, Section I.1.f), whether or not the survivor takes part in a prosecution.

### 7.d Privacy

The name and location of a survivor shall not be made public without the survivor's consent.

## Section 8 — Restitution and Civil Remedy

### 8.a Restitution

A court sentencing a person under this Act shall order restitution to each survivor of at least the greater of:

1. the value of the survivor's labor or services at the minimum wage established by law, or at the prevailing wage for that work if higher; or
2. the gross income or value the offender gained from the survivor's labor or services;

together with the survivor's costs of medical care, housing, transportation, and lost income.

### 8.b Civil Action

A survivor may bring a civil action against any person who committed or knowingly benefited from an offense under this Act, and may recover actual damages, damages for emotional distress, up to **three times** actual damages where the conduct was willful, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

An action may be brought within ten years after the survivor is freed, or after the survivor reaches the age of majority, whichever is later.

### 8.c Unenforceable Debts

No debt arising from an offense under this Act, including a debt in debt bondage, may be enforced against a survivor.

## Section 9 — Labor in Custody

### 9.a Conditions

Labor may be required of a person in custody only as part of a criminal sentence imposed by the sentencing court, and only under every condition of Article I, Section I.1.d: safe work consistent with Article II, Sections II.5 and II.7.c; pay of at least the minimum wage for comparable work, with no part withheld for the cost of custody; work only for a government body, never leased or performed for a private business; and an end no later than the sentence.

No labor shall be required of a person for an offense committed while a minor.

### 9.b Offense

A custodial official who knowingly compels a person in custody to work outside these conditions, or as discipline, retaliation, or exploitation (Article II, Section II.5.g), commits the offense of involuntary servitude under Section 5.

### 9.c Oversight

The Custodial Inspectorate under the Custodial Oversight Act shall inspect every custodial work program for compliance with this Section.

## Section 10 — Public Contracts

Every contractor with any government in Concordia shall certify that no part of its goods or services, including those of its suppliers, was produced by slavery, serfdom, involuntary servitude, or debt bondage. A contractor that knowingly makes a false certification shall lose the contract and may be barred from future public contracts for up to five years, after notice and a hearing.

## Section 11 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. treat any permitted obligation in Article I, Section I.1.d as involuntary servitude;
2. require a survivor to cooperate with any investigation as a condition of protection or services; or
3. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 12 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 13 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on its enactment, and applies only to offenses committed on or after that date (Article II, Section II.7.a). Sections 7 and 8.c apply to every survivor from that date.
