# BILL — USE OF FORCE BY GOVERNMENT OFFICERS

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Use of Force Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to establish uniform rules for the use of force by government officers, so that force is used only when necessary, only to the degree necessary, and never as punishment.

This Act carries out the right to physical integrity (Article I, Section I.3.a), the requirement that seizures be reasonable (Article II, Section II.6.b), and the limits on custodial force (Article II, Section II.5.d). It applies equally to every person, regardless of protected status (Article I, Section I.4.a).

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every officer, employee, agent, or contractor of the federal government, or of any state, territory, or local government, who is authorized to use force against a person, including members of the armed forces acting in support of civilian authorities under Article V, Section V.7.a.

This Act does not govern the use of armed force in armed conflict under Article V, Section V.7.a.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.3.a and Article II, Sections II.5.d and II.6.b against every level of government (Article VII, Section VII.3.c).

A state, territory, or local government may adopt rules that restrict the use of force further than this Act does. No such rule may permit force that this Act prohibits.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Officer

**Officer** means a person to whom this Act applies under Section 3.

### 4.b Force

**Force** means physical contact, a weapon, a restraint, a device, a chemical agent, an animal, or another physical means used to compel a person's compliance, to restrain a person, or to cause physical harm.

The following are not force, unless used to cause pain or injury:

1. guiding or escorting a person who is not resisting; and
2. handcuffing a person who is not resisting, in a manner that causes no injury.

### 4.c Deadly Force

**Deadly force** means force that creates a substantial risk of death or serious bodily harm, including:

1. discharging a firearm at or in the direction of a person;
2. any neck restraint, chokehold, or other method that restricts breathing or the flow of blood to the brain;
3. striking a person's head, neck, or spine with a hard object;
4. placing body weight on the back, neck, or chest of a person who is lying face down, or otherwise restraining a person in a position that restricts breathing; and
5. driving a vehicle into a person or into a vehicle a person occupies.

### 4.d Less-Lethal Weapon

**Less-lethal weapon** means a weapon designed to incapacitate or cause pain without a substantial risk of death when used as designed, including electronic control devices, chemical agents, impact munitions, and batons. Use of a less-lethal weapon in a manner that creates a substantial risk of death or serious bodily harm is deadly force.

### 4.e Serious Bodily Harm

**Serious bodily harm** means bodily injury that creates a substantial risk of death, or causes serious permanent disfigurement, or causes long-term loss or impairment of the function of any part of the body or of any organ.

### 4.f Imminent

**Imminent** means occurring, or reasonably appearing likely to occur, so soon that there is no reasonable opportunity to prevent it by a less forceful measure.

*(Imminent danger and reasonable force are defined in Article I, Section I.3.a.)*

### 4.g De-escalation

**De-escalation** means action taken to reduce the intensity of an encounter and the need for force, including verbal communication, warnings, time, distance, cover, calling for additional or specialized help, and allowing a person to comply.

### 4.h Restrained Person

**Restrained person** means a person whose movement is substantially limited by handcuffs, other restraints, or physical control, so that the person cannot pose an imminent threat by their own movement.

### 4.i Person in Crisis

**Person in crisis** means a person whose behavior reasonably appears to result from a mental health condition, intellectual or developmental disability, substance intoxication, or a medical emergency.

## Section 5 — General Standard

An officer may use force only when all of the following are true:

1. **Lawful purpose:** the force is used to make a lawful arrest or seizure, to prevent escape from lawful custody, to carry out a lawful custodial purpose under Article II, Section II.5.d, or to protect the officer or another person from imminent bodily harm;
2. **Necessity:** no reasonably available alternative, including de-escalation, would accomplish that purpose;
3. **Proportionality:** the type and degree of force are proportionate to the threat the person poses and to the seriousness of the suspected offense; and
4. **Duration:** the force ends as soon as the purpose is accomplished or the person stops resisting or posing a threat.

Whether force was reasonable shall be judged from the perspective of a reasonable officer in the same circumstances, considering the totality of the circumstances, including the officer's own conduct before the use of force and whether that conduct unnecessarily created the need for force.

Force shall not be used for punishment, retaliation, humiliation, or coercion, or because of a person's speech, protected status, or exercise of a constitutional right.

## Section 6 — De-escalation and Warnings

Before using force, an officer shall use de-escalation whenever it is feasible to do so without increasing the danger to the officer or another person.

Before using force, an officer shall, when feasible, identify themselves as an officer, state what the person must do, warn that force may be used, and give the person a reasonable opportunity to comply.

An officer shall consider whether a person's failure to comply results from a disability, a language barrier, a medical condition, intoxication, fear, or an inability to hear or understand, and shall adjust their response accordingly.

## Section 7 — Deadly Force

An officer may use deadly force only when necessary to protect the officer or another person from an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm, and only when no less forceful measure would prevent it.

An officer shall not use deadly force:

1. against a person who poses a threat only to themselves;
2. to protect property alone;
3. against a person fleeing, unless the person poses an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm to another person;
4. against a person suspected only of an offense that did not involve the threat or use of serious bodily harm, unless that person poses an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm;
5. by firing at or from a moving vehicle, unless a person in the vehicle is using or imminently threatening deadly force by means other than the vehicle, or the vehicle is being used to attack people and no other means would stop it; or
6. by firing a warning shot.

An officer shall, when feasible, give a verbal warning before using deadly force.

When using deadly force, an officer shall take reasonable care to avoid harming bystanders.

## Section 8 — Restrictions on Particular Methods

### 8.a Neck and Breathing Restraints

The methods described in Section 4.c(2) and 4.c(4) are deadly force and may be used only where Section 7 permits deadly force. An officer shall move a restrained person out of a face-down position as soon as the person is restrained.

### 8.b Less-Lethal Weapons

An officer shall not use a less-lethal weapon:

1. against a restrained person, unless necessary to prevent imminent serious bodily harm;
2. against a person who is passively resisting, meaning not complying but not posing a threat;
3. repeatedly or for an extended duration, unless each use is separately justified;
4. against a person the officer knows or reasonably believes is pregnant, elderly, or a minor, or who is in water, at a height, or operating a vehicle, unless Section 7 would permit deadly force; or
5. against a person in crisis, unless necessary to prevent imminent serious bodily harm.

### 8.c Restrained Persons

An officer shall not use force against a restrained person, except the minimum force necessary to prevent imminent bodily harm or escape.

### 8.d Animals

An officer shall not use a police animal to bite or hold a person, unless the person poses an imminent threat of bodily harm and no less forceful measure would prevent it.

## Section 9 — Peaceful Assembly

An officer shall not use force against a peaceful assembly protected by Article I, Section I.1.a, or against any person because of the person's lawful speech or assembly.

An officer may order an assembly to disperse only when the assembly is no longer peaceful or creates an imminent danger to persons, and only after:

1. announcing the order clearly and repeatedly, by means audible to the persons present;
2. identifying a safe route to leave; and
3. giving a reasonable time to leave.

Force used to disperse an assembly shall be directed only at persons engaged in violence or who refuse a lawful dispersal order, and not at the assembly as a whole.

An officer shall not use chemical agents, impact munitions, or other area weapons indiscriminately against a crowd, or use them against persons who are peaceful, including journalists, legal observers, and medical volunteers.

## Section 10 — Persons in Crisis

### 10.a Crisis Response Services

Each state and territory shall ensure that a civilian crisis response service is available at all hours throughout its territory and can be reached through the general emergency telephone number.

A civilian crisis response service shall be staffed by mental health professionals, medical professionals, and trained peer counselors. Its responders are not officers, shall not carry weapons, and shall not use force except in self-defense or defense of others under Article I, Section I.3.a.

### 10.b Dispatch

A call concerning a person in crisis shall be answered by civilian crisis responders, and not by officers, unless the call indicates that the person:

1. has a weapon; or
2. is using or imminently threatening violence against another person.

Officers may join a civilian crisis response only when the responders request it, or when the person begins using or imminently threatening violence against another person.

### 10.c Officers Responding to a Person in Crisis

When an officer responds to a person in crisis, the officer shall give priority to de-escalation, time, and distance, and shall request civilian crisis responders to join or take over the response as soon as it is safe to do so.

### 10.d Custody

A civilian crisis responder shall not take a person into custody. A person in crisis may be taken into custody only under the custodial categories of Article II, Section 2 and their requirements, including emergency custody (Article II, Section II.2.f) and civil protective custody (Article II, Section II.2.g).

### 10.e Funding

The federal government shall pay at least one-half of the cost of each state's and territory's civilian crisis response service, through appropriations sufficient for that purpose. Federal funding shall be distributed by a neutral formula based on population and need, and shall not be conditioned on any matter unrelated to this Section.

### 10.f Standards

The Department responsible for health shall establish minimum standards for the training, staffing, and response times of civilian crisis response services, for the purpose of ensuring that every person in crisis receives a timely and safe response. The standards shall not authorize responders to use force or take custody.

## Section 11 — Duties After Force

### 11.a Medical Aid

After using force, an officer shall promptly provide or request medical aid for any person who is injured, complains of injury, or was subjected to deadly force or a less-lethal weapon. This duty carries out Article II, Section II.5.b for persons in custody.

### 11.b Duty to Intervene

An officer who observes another officer using force that the observing officer knows or reasonably should know violates this Act shall intervene to stop it, when they have a reasonable opportunity to do so, and shall report it under Section 12.

### 11.c Retaliation Prohibited

No officer shall be dismissed, disciplined, threatened, or otherwise penalized for intervening, for reporting a violation of this Act, or for testifying about one.

## Section 12 — Reporting and Records

### 12.a Use-of-Force Reports

Every officer who uses force, other than the actions excluded from force in Section 4.b, shall file a written report before the end of their shift, describing the circumstances, the force used, any injury, and the de-escalation attempted.

### 12.b Recording

Every officer who carries out arrests, seizures, or crowd control shall wear an activated body camera during every enforcement encounter, unless doing so is not feasible because of an emergency that does not allow time to activate it.

An officer's failure to record an encounter, without a lawful reason, permits a court or tribunal to infer that the recording would have been unfavorable to the officer.

Recordings shall be preserved for a period established by law, and for the duration of any investigation or proceeding concerning the encounter.

A person subjected to force, or their counsel or next of kin, shall have the right to view the recording of the encounter. A recording of the use of deadly force, or of force causing serious bodily harm, shall be made public within forty-five days, except as necessary to protect the privacy of persons other than the officers involved (Article II, Section II.6.a).

### 12.c Public Data

Each government agency employing officers shall publish annually the number of uses of force by type, injuries, deaths, complaints, and their outcomes, together with the protected status of the persons subjected to force where known. The published data shall not identify any person subjected to force.

## Section 13 — Independent Investigation

### 13.a Establishment

The **Office of Independent Investigation** is established as an independent office of the federal government. It is not part of, and shall not share staff, facilities, or budgets with, any agency that employs officers.

### 13.b Jurisdiction

The Office shall investigate, and where warranted prosecute:

1. every use of force by an officer that causes death or serious bodily harm;
2. every death of a person in custody; and
3. any other suspected offense under Section 15.c that is referred to it, or that it chooses to investigate.

Every agency employing officers shall notify the Office within twenty-four hours after any event described in item 1 or 2, and shall preserve all evidence, including recordings, until the Office takes custody of it.

The Office shall have authority to investigate and prosecute offenses under this Act, and any related offense under federal law, in the courts of Concordia. No other prosecuting authority shall prosecute an event within the Office's jurisdiction while the Office's investigation is pending, without the Office's consent.

### 13.c Director

The Office shall be headed by a Director, nominated by the President and appointed with the confirmation of a majority of the Senate under Article V, Section V.4.a.

The Director serves a single term of seven years and may not be reappointed.

No person may be appointed Director who has served as an officer, or as a prosecutor in the jurisdiction of an agency employing officers, within the five years before the appointment.

The President may remove the Director only for cause, meaning neglect of duty, misconduct in office, or incapacity, after written notice stating the cause. A removed Director may seek judicial review of the removal. *(See Article V, Section V.4.c.)*

No official may direct the Office to open, close, expand, or limit a particular investigation or prosecution.

### 13.d Regional Offices and Staff

The Office shall maintain regional offices sufficient to reach the scene of any event described in Section 13.b within twelve hours. Its investigators shall not include any person who served as an officer in the same region within the previous three years.

### 13.e Reports

The Office shall publish each investigation's outcome, with the reasons for any decision not to prosecute, and an annual report to Congress. Publication shall not identify a person who has not been charged, other than the officer whose use of force is under investigation.

### 13.f Funding

Congress shall appropriate funds sufficient for the Office to carry out its duties. Funding shall not be reduced or conditioned in order to influence a particular investigation or prosecution.

## Section 14 — Training

Every officer shall be trained, before being authorized to use force and at regular intervals afterward, in the requirements of this Act, de-escalation, crisis intervention, the duty to intervene, and medical aid.

## Section 15 — Accountability

### 15.a Discipline

A violation of this Act is grounds for discipline, including dismissal, after a procedure providing due process.

### 15.b Civil Remedy

A person subjected to force in violation of this Act, or their next of kin, may bring a civil action against the officer and the agency employing them for damages, injunctive relief, and reasonable attorney's fees and costs, as Article II, Section II.9.c requires.

It is not a defense to an action under this Act that the right violated had not previously been established by a court in a case with similar facts.

The employing agency shall pay any judgment against an officer for conduct within the scope of their employment, except that the agency may recover from an officer who acted with intent to harm or with knowledge that the conduct violated this Act.

### 15.c Criminal Offense

An officer commits an offense if the officer knowingly or recklessly uses force in violation of this Act and causes bodily harm.

The prosecution must prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt.

### 15.d Self-Defense

Force that violates this Act is unlawful for purposes of a person's right of self-defense under Article I, Section I.3.a. Nothing in this Act authorizes force against an officer who is using force lawfully.

## Section 16 — Penalty

### 16.a Imprisonment

A person convicted of an offense under Section 15.c may be sentenced to imprisonment for no more than:

1. **three years**, where the force caused bodily harm;
2. **ten years**, where the force caused serious bodily harm; or
3. **twenty-five years**, where the force caused death.

The sentencing court shall consider whether the officer used deadly force, whether the person was restrained, and whether the officer acted with intent to harm. Every sentence shall satisfy Article II, Sections II.7.a, II.7.b, and II.7.c.

### 16.b Loss of Authority to Use Force

A person convicted of an offense under Section 15.c shall not afterward be employed or authorized as an officer by any government.

**Independent basis:** This restriction is a qualification for the authority to use force against other persons, not continuing punishment. It protects the physical integrity of other persons (Article I, Section I.3.a) and has an independent lawful basis under Article II, Section II.8.c.

### 16.c Decertification Registry

The Office of Independent Investigation shall maintain a national registry of every person who is barred under Section 16.b, or who was dismissed by any government for a use of force that violated this Act. No government shall employ or authorize as an officer a person listed in the registry because of a conviction. A government that employs a person listed because of dismissal shall first review the dismissal and state in writing why the person is fit to use force.

A person listed in the registry shall receive notice and may challenge the listing before a court.

## Section 17 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. authorize force that Article I, Section I.3.a or Article II, Section II.5.d would not permit;
2. restrict a person's right of self-defense or defense of others under Article I, Section I.3.a beyond what the Constitution permits;
3. restrict peaceful speech or assembly protected by Article I, Section I.1.a;
4. limit any remedy available under Article II, Section II.9.c; or
5. create a presumption against the accused concerning any element of an offense.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 18 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 19 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment, except that:

1. Sections 12.b and 14 take effect one year later, and two years later for state, territorial, and local governments, to allow equipment to be acquired and officers to be trained;
2. Sections 10.a and 10.b take effect three years later, to allow civilian crisis response services to be established; and
3. the Office of Independent Investigation shall begin investigations within one year after enactment. Until then, Section 13 shall be carried out by a prosecuting authority from a jurisdiction other than that of the officer's agency.
