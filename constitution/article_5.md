# ARTICLE V — THE EXECUTIVE POWER

> **Draft note:** Age, term, term-limit, and oath rules for the President and Vice President are not repeated here; they come from III.5, III.6, and III.6.c.

## Section 1 — Vesting of Executive Power

### V.1.a — The President

The executive power of the United States is vested in a President.

The President shall exercise only executive powers granted by this Constitution or by law consistent with it.

### V.1.b — Executive Duties

The President shall:

1. take care that the laws be faithfully executed;
2. direct the executive departments and administrative agencies established by law (IV.1.b);
3. command the armed forces, subject to civilian constitutional control and to the powers of Congress;
4. conduct the foreign relations of the United States, subject to Section 7; and
5. report to Congress at least once each calendar year on the state of the nation and recommend measures the President considers necessary.

### V.1.c — Limits on Executive Power

The President shall not exercise legislative or judicial power.

No executive order, directive, or regulation may create a criminal offense, a tax, an appropriation, or an exception to a constitutional right.

Every executive order shall identify the constitutional or statutory authority on which it relies and shall be made publicly available. Publication may be delayed only when necessary for national security, and only for a limited time and subject to lawful oversight by Congress.

The President shall spend appropriated funds as Congress directs, and shall not withhold or delay them for a purpose not authorized by the appropriating law.

## Section 2 — Election and Qualifications

### V.2.a — Election of the President and Vice President

The President and Vice President shall be elected together, as a joint candidacy, by a national popular vote administered by the Election Authority (III.3).

All votes for President shall be counted as a single national total, and each vote shall carry equal weight (III.1.c).

### V.2.b — Ranked-Choice Counting

**Definitions:**
* **Ranked ballot** means a ballot on which a voter ranks joint candidacies in order of preference.
* **Continuing candidacy** means a joint candidacy that has not been eliminated.
* **Continuing ballot** means a ballot that ranks at least one continuing candidacy.

A voter may rank as many or as few candidacies as the voter chooses.

Each continuing ballot counts as one vote for its highest-ranked continuing candidacy.

If a candidacy receives a majority of the votes on continuing ballots, it is elected. Otherwise, the candidacy with the fewest votes is eliminated and the count is repeated.

Where two or more candidacies are tied for elimination, the candidacy with the fewest first-choice votes is eliminated. A tie that remains shall be resolved by lot.

### V.2.c — Qualifications

The President and Vice President shall each:

1. be a citizen of the United States; and
2. satisfy the age eligibility and other qualifications established by III.6.

For a person who becomes President or Vice President other than by election, the age eligibility of III.6.a is measured on the day the person takes office.

No qualification shall depend on whether a person's citizenship was acquired at birth or by naturalization. *(A birth-citizenship requirement would be a distinction based on national origin under I.4.a.)*

### V.2.d — Term

The President and Vice President serve the term established by III.5.a and are subject to the term limits of III.5.b and III.5.c.

The term begins on the seventy-fifth day after the election.

## Section 3 — Vice President, Succession, and Inability

### V.3.a — The Vice President

The Vice President shall perform the duties assigned by the President or by law.

The Vice President shall exercise no legislative power.

### V.3.b — Succession to the Presidency

If the President dies, resigns, or is removed from office, the Vice President becomes President for the remainder of the term.

Congress shall by law establish the further order of succession. Every successor shall be a person who holds an office to which the person was elected or confirmed by the Senate, and who satisfies V.2.c.

Service as President through succession counts toward the term limit as provided in III.5.c.

### V.3.c — Vacancy in the Vice Presidency

When the office of Vice President is vacant, the President shall nominate a Vice President, who takes office upon confirmation by a majority vote of each House of Congress.

### V.3.d — Presidential Inability

The President may declare in writing to Congress that the President is unable to perform the duties of the office. The Vice President shall then act as President until the President declares in writing that the inability has ended.

If the Vice President and a majority of the heads of the executive departments declare in writing to Congress that the President is unable to perform the duties of the office, the Vice President shall immediately act as President.

If the President disputes that declaration, the President resumes the office unless, within twenty-one days, two-thirds of all members of each House vote that the inability continues.

## Section 4 — Appointments and Removal

### V.4.a — Senate-Confirmed Offices

The President shall nominate, and with the confirmation of a majority of the Senate shall appoint:

1. the heads of the executive departments;
2. ambassadors;
3. judges of courts inferior to the Supreme Court; and
4. other senior officers designated by law.

Congress may by law vest the appointment of other officers in the President alone or in the heads of executive departments.

### V.4.b — Acting Officers

An acting officer shall not serve in an office requiring Senate confirmation for more than one hundred twenty days unless a nomination for that office is pending before the Senate.

No person whose nomination the Senate has rejected may serve as an acting officer in that office.

### V.4.c — Removal

The President may remove any executive officer the President appointed, except where this Constitution provides otherwise.

The President shall not remove or direct members of the Election Authority (III.3.b) or the districting commission (III.4.c).

Congress may by law provide that the head of an office established to investigate or prosecute misconduct by government officers may be removed only for cause stated in that law, and that no official may direct the office in a particular investigation or prosecution. Such a law shall permit judicial review of a removal.

Judges may be removed only by impeachment, or for permanent incapacity under VI.4.f.

### V.4.d — Judges of Inferior Courts

A judge of a court inferior to the Supreme Court shall serve a single term of eighteen years.

No person shall serve more than eighteen years in total as a judge of courts inferior to the Supreme Court. A sitting judge appointed to another inferior court serves only the remainder of those eighteen years.

The compensation of such a judge shall not be reduced during the judge's term.

Such a judge may be removed only by impeachment, or for permanent incapacity under VI.4.f.

## Section 5 — Pardons and Commutations

### V.5.a — Pardon Power

The President may grant pardons, commutations, and reprieves for offenses against the United States, except in cases of impeachment.

**Definitions:**
* **Pardon** means an official act forgiving an offense and ending its criminal penalties.
* **Commutation** means an official act reducing a sentence.
* **Reprieve** means an official act delaying the execution of a sentence.
* **Family member** means a spouse, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, or grandchild, including by marriage or adoption.

### V.5.b — Limits

The President shall not grant a pardon, commutation, or reprieve:

1. to the President;
2. to a family member of the President or Vice President;
3. to an officer or employee of the President's own administration for an offense committed in connection with official duties;
4. for an offense not yet committed; or
5. in exchange for anything of value or any political or personal benefit.

A pardon, commutation, or reprieve granted in violation of this subsection shall have no legal effect.

### V.5.c — Transparency

Every pardon, commutation, and reprieve shall be made public, together with written reasons, within seven days after it is granted.

## Section 6 — Emergency Powers

### V.6.a — Declaration of Emergency

**Definitions:**
* **Emergency** means an armed attack, natural disaster, epidemic, or comparable catastrophe that presents an imminent threat to life or physical safety and that ordinary law is inadequate to address.
* **Emergency power** means a governmental power available only during an emergency declared under this Section.

The President may declare an emergency. The declaration shall be in writing and shall state:

1. the facts constituting the emergency;
2. the geographic area affected; and
3. each emergency power invoked and the law authorizing it.

### V.6.b — Duration

A declaration expires thirty days after it is made unless Congress extends it.

Congress may extend a declaration by a majority vote of each House, for no more than thirty days at a time.

Congress may terminate a declaration at any time by a majority vote of each House. A termination is not subject to veto.

The President shall not issue a new declaration based on substantially the same circumstances without the approval of Congress.

### V.6.c — Limits on Emergency Power

During an emergency, the President may exercise only emergency powers that Congress authorized by law before the emergency, and only within the area and for the purposes stated in the declaration.

No emergency declaration shall:

1. suspend or narrow a right established by Article I, II, III, or X beyond what I.4.c would permit without an emergency;
2. postpone, cancel, or alter an election, or extend any term of office (III.10.e);
3. adjourn, dissolve, or obstruct Congress or any court;
4. authorize spending without an appropriation (IV.10.a); or
5. create a criminal offense.

This Section is the only authorization for emergency powers in this Constitution, and it does not authorize altering an election for purposes of III.10.e.

### V.6.d — Judicial Review

Any person affected by an emergency declaration or emergency power may challenge it in court on an expedited basis.

A court may invalidate a declaration that does not satisfy V.6.a, or an action that exceeds V.6.c.

## Section 7 — Armed Forces and Foreign Affairs

### V.7.a — Use of Armed Forces

The President may use the armed forces without authorization from Congress only to repel an armed attack on the United States or its armed forces.

The President shall report any such use to Congress within forty-eight hours, and shall end it within sixty days unless Congress authorizes it to continue under IV.1.b.

The armed forces shall not be used to influence an election, suppress the exercise of a constitutional right, or enforce civilian law except as authorized by law in support of civilian authorities during an emergency declared under Section 6.

### V.7.b — Treaties

The President may negotiate treaties. A treaty takes effect only upon the approval of two-thirds of the Senators present.

No treaty shall override this Constitution.

---

## Section 8 — Executive Accountability

### V.8.a — Compensation and Conflicts of Interest

The compensation of the President and Vice President shall be fixed by law and shall not be increased or reduced during the term for which they were elected.

Neither the President nor the Vice President shall accept any payment, gift, office, or title from a foreign government, or any compensation from the United States other than that fixed by law.

The President and Vice President shall publicly disclose their financial interests each year.

### V.8.b — Compliance with Law and Courts

The President and all executive officers shall comply with final judicial orders.

The President remains subject to this Constitution and to generally applicable criminal law. Holding executive office shall not place a person above the law.

A sitting President may be criminally investigated, charged, and tried while in office. If a criminal proceeding or sentence prevents the President from performing the duties of the office, V.3.d applies.
