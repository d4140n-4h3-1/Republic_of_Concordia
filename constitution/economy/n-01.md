# ECONOMIC ACT — WORK AND COLLECTIVE BARGAINING

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Work and Collective Bargaining Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to let workers and employers set wages and working conditions together, sector by sector, as the Nordic countries do, so that pay is fair, conflict is rare, and the economy can adapt.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every employer and worker in Concordia whose work is in or affects interstate or foreign commerce, and to every government as an employer.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(6) and IV.1.b(17), and under IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.1.h against every level of government.

## Section 4 — Definitions

### 4.a Union

**Union** means an organization of workers, independent of any employer and any government, formed to represent its members in dealing with employers.

### 4.b Employers' Association

**Employers' association** means an organization of employers formed to bargain with unions.

### 4.c Sector

**Sector** means an industry or occupation, such as construction, retail, health care, or manufacturing, as defined by the National Bargaining Council.

### 4.d Collective Agreement

**Collective agreement** means a written agreement between one or more unions and one or more employers or employers' associations on wages and working conditions.

## Section 5 — Rights of Workers and Employers

Every worker may form and join a union, or decline to join one. Every employer may form and join an employers' association, or decline to join one. No person shall be dismissed, disciplined, or disadvantaged for joining, declining to join, or taking part in the lawful activities of either (Article I, Section I.1.h).

## Section 6 — Sectoral Bargaining

### 6.a Bargaining by Sector

Unions and employers' associations may bargain for an entire sector. A sectoral agreement sets minimum wages and conditions for the sector, which local agreements may improve but not reduce.

### 6.b National Bargaining Council

The **National Bargaining Council** is established, with equal numbers of members chosen by unions and by employers' associations, and a neutral chair chosen by both or, failing agreement, by lot from a list of qualified mediators. The Council shall define sectors, register agreements, provide mediation, and publish every agreement.

### 6.c Extension

Where a sectoral agreement covers employers employing at least **half** of the workers in a sector, the Council may extend its minimum wages and conditions to every employer in that sector, after public notice and a hearing.

### 6.d Minimum Wage Where No Agreement Applies

Where no sectoral agreement covers a worker, the Council shall set a minimum wage for that work each year, at no less than **sixty percent** of the national median wage.

## Section 7 — Industrial Action

### 7.a Strikes and Lockouts

Workers may strike to reach a collective agreement, or otherwise to advance their interests as workers (Article I, Section I.1.h), and employers may lock out to reach a collective agreement, in each case after **seven days'** notice and an offer of mediation.

### 7.b Peace Obligation

While a collective agreement is in force, neither side shall strike or lock out over matters it settles. Disputes over its meaning shall go to the Labor Court under Section 9.

### 7.c Essential Services

In health care, emergency services, and the supply of water and power, the parties shall agree, before any strike, on the minimum service needed to protect life and safety. If they cannot agree, the Council shall decide.

## Section 8 — Workers on Boards

### 8.a Board Seats

In every company with at least **twenty-five** employees, the employees may elect **one-third** of the members of the board of directors, and at least two members.

### 8.b Works Councils

In every workplace with at least **fifty** employees, the employees may elect a works council, which shall be informed and consulted before any major change in work, technology, or employment.

## Section 9 — Labor Court

The **Labor Court** is established to decide disputes over collective agreements and this Act, with judges appointed under Article V and lay members nominated equally by unions and employers' associations.

## Section 10 — Equal Pay

Every collective agreement and every employer shall pay equal pay for work of equal value, without regard to protected status (Article I, Section I.4.a; Equal Treatment Act).

## Section 11 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. Nothing in this Act requires any person to join or support any organization, or to express any view (Article I, Section I.1.a).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 12 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 13 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 8 applies to each company from its first annual meeting after that date.
