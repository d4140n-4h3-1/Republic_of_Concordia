# ECONOMIC ACT — OPEN MARKETS

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Open Markets Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to keep markets open and competitive, as the Nordic countries do: to stop businesses from fixing prices, dividing markets, or abusing their power, and to favor open trade while helping the workers it affects.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to competition and trade in interstate and foreign commerce.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(6) and IV.1.b(17).

## Section 4 — Competition Authority

The **Competition Authority** is established as an independent office. It shall prevent agreements that fix prices or divide markets, the abuse of a dominant position, and mergers that would substantially reduce competition.

## Section 5 — Open Trade

Concordia shall favor open trade, and shall support workers affected by trade through the rights in the Flexicurity Act, Section 6, rather than through barriers to imports.

## Section 6 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. Nothing in this Act permits the taking of private property without just compensation (Article II, Section II.1.c), or requires or forbids public ownership of any enterprise (Article IV, Section IV.1.d).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 7 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 8 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
