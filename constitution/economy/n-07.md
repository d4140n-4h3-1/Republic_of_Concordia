# ECONOMIC ACT — HOUSING

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Housing Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to ensure that every resident of Concordia has a secure and affordable home, by ending homelessness through Housing First, as Finland has done, and by building a large sector of nonprofit housing open to everyone, as Denmark has done.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every resident of Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(2), IV.1.b(3), and IV.1.b(17), and under IV.1.b(15), to carry out and enforce the right to housing in Article I, Section I.5.a against every level of government. The provision of housing remains with the states, territories, and local governments, which shall receive federal funding to carry out this Act.

**Federal fallback:** Where a state or territory declines federal funding under this Act, or fails to provide Housing First, housing allowances, and support for nonprofit housing to its residents as this Act requires, the federal government shall provide Housing First, housing allowances, and support for nonprofit housing directly to those residents, on the same terms, until the state or territory does so. No resident shall lose a benefit of this Act because of where they live.

## Section 4 — Housing First

### 4.a A Home First

Every person experiencing homelessness shall be offered a permanent home, with a lease in their own name, without first being required to become sober, find work, or complete treatment.

### 4.b Support

Every person housed under this Section shall be offered, but not required to accept, support with health, addiction, work, and daily living (Article I, Section I.3.a).

### 4.c Conversion of Shelters

Temporary shelters shall be converted, over time, into permanent supported housing.

## Section 5 — Nonprofit Housing

### 5.a Housing Associations

Nonprofit housing associations, owned and governed by their residents, shall be supported to build and run rental housing open to everyone, not only to persons in need.

### 5.b Rents

Rents in nonprofit housing shall cover only the cost of building, running, and maintaining it, and shall be set by the residents' democratic governance under rules set by law.

### 5.c Financing

A **National Building Fund**, financed by the repayments of nonprofit housing loans, shall lend to housing associations at low cost, so that existing housing helps pay for new housing.

### 5.d Mixed Communities

Nonprofit housing shall be built in every community, and no community shall be planned to separate residents by income.

## Section 6 — Housing Allowance

A household that pays more than **thirty percent** of its income for housing shall receive a housing allowance, set by law, to reduce that share.

## Section 7 — Tenants

Every tenant shall have a written lease, protection from eviction except for cause after notice and a hearing, and rent increases limited to once a year with notice.

## Section 8 — Planning for Supply

Every local government that receives funding under this Act shall plan for enough housing to meet expected population growth, and shall not use zoning to exclude nonprofit or multifamily housing.

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. Nothing in this Act permits the taking of private property without just compensation (Article II, Section II.1.c).

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 4 shall be fully in force within five years.
