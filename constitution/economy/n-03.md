# ECONOMIC ACT — UNIVERSAL HEALTH CARE

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Universal Health Care Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to give every resident of Concordia access to health care according to need, not ability to pay, through a tax-funded system run close to the people it serves, as in the Nordic countries.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every resident of Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(2), IV.1.b(3), and IV.1.b(17). The delivery of care remains with the states and territories, which shall receive federal funding to carry out this Act.

## Section 4 — Coverage

Every resident is covered for all medically necessary care, including primary care, specialist and hospital care, maternity care, mental health care, prescription medicines, rehabilitation, and long-term care.

Dental care is covered in full for every person under the age of **twenty-five**, and at reduced cost for others.

## Section 5 — Cost to Patients

### 5.a Fees

Care may carry small fees set by law. No fee shall be charged for care for persons under the age of majority, for maternity care, or for preventive care.

### 5.b Annual Ceiling

No person shall pay more than an amount set by law, no more than **two percent** of the national median income, in fees for care in any twelve months, and a separate equal ceiling for prescription medicines. After a ceiling is reached, care is free for the rest of the period.

## Section 6 — Organization

### 6.a Regional Delivery

Each state or territory shall organize care through public health regions, governed by elected regional boards.

### 6.b Providers

Care may be delivered by public, nonprofit, or private providers. Every provider that receives public funding shall accept every covered patient on equal terms, and shall not charge covered patients more than the fees in Section 5.

### 6.c Waiting Times

Every person shall have contact with primary care the same day, a medical assessment within **three days**, a specialist visit within **ninety days**, and treatment within **ninety days** after a decision to treat. If a region cannot meet these times, it shall pay for care elsewhere.

## Section 7 — Consent and Choice

All care under this Act requires informed consent (Article I, Section I.3.a). Every person may choose their primary care provider, and may seek a second opinion.

## Section 8 — Funding

This Act is funded from general taxes under the Fiscal Responsibility and Taxation Act. Federal funding shall be distributed to the states and territories by population, adjusted for age and need.

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. Nothing in this Act permits any treatment without consent, or limits any right under Article I, Section 3.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the second fiscal year following its enactment, and shall be fully in force within five years.
