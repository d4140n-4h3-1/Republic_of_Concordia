# ECONOMIC ACT — FISCAL RESPONSIBILITY AND TAXATION

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Fiscal Responsibility and Taxation Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to pay for a strong welfare state with a broad, simple, and transparent tax system, to keep public finances sound over the business cycle, and to keep markets open and competitive, as the Nordic countries do.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to the federal government's taxes, budget, and debt, and to competition in interstate and foreign commerce.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(2), IV.1.b(3), IV.1.b(4), IV.1.b(6), and IV.1.b(17).

---

**Part One — Taxation**

## Section 4 — Broad Base

Taxes shall apply broadly, with few deductions, exemptions, or special rates, so that rates can stay moderate and the system simple.

## Section 5 — Income from Work

Income from work and from pensions shall be taxed at progressive rates set by law, with a basic allowance below which no tax is due.

## Section 6 — Income from Capital

Interest, dividends, rents, and capital gains shall be taxed together at a single flat rate set by law, no lower than **twenty-two percent** and no higher than the lowest progressive rate on income from work plus ten percentage points.

## Section 7 — Companies

Company profits shall be taxed at a single rate set by law, on a broad base.

## Section 8 — Consumption

A value-added tax shall apply to goods and services at a standard rate set by law. Food, medicine, books, and public transport may be taxed at a reduced rate. Persons with low incomes shall receive a refundable credit to offset the value-added tax they pay.

## Section 9 — Tax Records of Officials

### 9.a Public Officials

The taxable income, wealth, and tax paid of every **public official** shall be published each year, in a public register that shows the totals only, for every year in which the person holds office and for **five years** after they leave it.

**Public official** means every elected official at every level of government, every judge, every official confirmed by the Senate, and every head of an executive department or independent office.

### 9.b Everyone Else

The tax records of every other person are confidential. They are stored personal information protected by Article II, Section II.6.e, and are exempt from disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act, Section 7(1).

## Section 10 — Simple Filing

The tax authority shall prepare a completed return for every person, using the information it already holds. A person may accept it, or correct it, without cost.

## Section 11 — Extreme Wealth

### 11.a Wealth Tax

Every person whose net worth exceeds **one thousand times** the median household net worth in Concordia shall pay an annual tax of **one percent** of the amount above that level, rising to **two percent** of the amount above **five thousand times** the median. No person below the first level pays this tax.

Assets shall be valued at market value. Shares in a company that is not publicly traded shall be valued by an independent valuation under rules set by law. A person whose wealth is mainly in such shares, or in other assets that cannot readily be sold, may defer payment, with interest, for up to **ten years**, secured against those assets, so that no founder is forced to give up control of their company to pay this tax.

### 11.b Gains at Death

When a person dies, any increase in the value of their assets that has not yet been taxed shall be taxed as income from capital (Section 6), except:

1. assets passing to a surviving spouse, for whom the tax is deferred until the spouse's death or sale;
2. the person's primary home; and
3. the first part of every estate, up to an amount set by law, so that ordinary estates owe nothing.

The tax on a family farm or family business passing to a family member who continues to run it may be paid in installments over **fifteen years**.

### 11.c Exit Tax

A person whose net worth exceeds the first level in Section 11.a, and who gives up citizenship or residence in Concordia, shall pay tax on any untaxed increase in the value of their assets as if they had sold them on the day they left.

### 11.d Anti-Avoidance

Assets held through a company, trust, foundation, or other arrangement that a person controls or benefits from, and gifts made in the previous **ten years**, count toward that person's net worth under this Section.

### 11.e Use of Revenue

The revenue from Sections 11.a and 11.c shall be used first to carry out the right to housing in Article I, Section I.5.a, through the Housing Act.

### 11.f Equal Application

This Section applies equally to every person above the levels it sets. No tax under it shall be imposed to punish any person for the exercise of a constitutional right.

**Part Two — Fiscal Responsibility**

## Section 12 — Fiscal Rule

### 12.a Surplus Target

The federal budget shall aim for a surplus of **one-third of one percent** of national income, on average, over each business cycle.

### 12.b Debt Anchor

Federal debt shall be kept below **thirty-five percent** of national income, except in a declared emergency or a deep recession, after which it shall be returned below that level within a period set by law.

A **deep recession** exists only while the Fiscal Council certifies that the national unemployment rate has risen by at least **two percentage points** within twelve months, or that national output has fallen for **two consecutive quarters**. The Council shall publish the test it applied and the data. The return below the debt anchor begins when the Council certifies that recovery has begun.

### 12.c Spending Ceiling

Each budget shall set a ceiling on total spending for the next three years, which may be exceeded only by a law that states why.

## Section 13 — Independent Fiscal Council

The **Fiscal Council** is established as an independent office, with members chosen for expertise and confirmed by three-fifths of the Senate. It shall assess, publicly and each year, whether the budget meets the fiscal rule, the realism of its forecasts, and the long-term sustainability of public finances. The government shall respond to each assessment in writing.

---

**Part Three — Open and Competitive Markets**

## Section 14 — Competition

The **Competition Authority** is established as an independent office. It shall prevent agreements that fix prices or divide markets, the abuse of a dominant position, and mergers that would substantially reduce competition.

## Section 15 — Open Trade

Concordia shall favor open trade, and shall support workers affected by trade through the rights in the Flexicurity Act, Section 6, rather than through barriers to imports.

## Section 16 — Integrity

Every public official shall disclose their income, assets, and interests each year. No official shall take part in a decision in which they have a personal financial interest.

## Section 17 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. No tax shall be imposed for the purpose of punishing the exercise of a constitutional right.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 18 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 19 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Part Two applies from the second budget after enactment.
