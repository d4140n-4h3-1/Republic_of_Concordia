# ECONOMIC ACT — FLEXICURITY

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Flexicurity Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to combine three things, as Denmark does: employers may hire and let go of workers with little difficulty; workers who lose a job receive generous income support; and every jobseeker receives active help to train for and find the next job. Security comes from the system, not from any one job.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every employer and worker covered by the Work and Collective Bargaining Act, and to every resident of Concordia who is seeking work.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(2), IV.1.b(3), IV.1.b(6), and IV.1.b(17).

## Section 4 — Flexible Employment

### 4.a Dismissal

An employer may end employment for economic or organizational reasons, or for the worker's conduct or performance, with notice under Section 4.b. No dismissal shall be made because of protected status, union activity, or any reason that this Constitution or a law forbids.

### 4.b Notice

Notice shall be at least **one month** for each year of service, up to **six months**. Collective agreements may improve notice.

### 4.c Severance

A worker dismissed for economic or organizational reasons after at least two years of service shall receive severance of **one week's pay for each year of service**, up to **twelve weeks**.

## Section 5 — Unemployment Insurance

### 5.a Coverage

Every worker shall be insured against unemployment. The insurance shall be run by public funds, or by funds run jointly with unions, which every worker may join whether or not they belong to a union.

### 5.b Benefit

An insured worker who becomes unemployed shall receive **ninety percent** of their previous wage, up to a ceiling of the national median wage, for up to **two years**.

### 5.c Funding

The insurance shall be funded by contributions from workers and employers, with the government covering any shortfall.

## Section 6 — Active Labor Market Policy

### 6.a Rights of Jobseekers

Every jobseeker shall have, free of charge:

1. a personal plan with a job counselor within **one month** of becoming unemployed;
2. training, retraining, or education, which may be paid for in part from a Lifelong Learning Account (Learning Funding Act, Section 6);
3. job matching and placement; and
4. subsidized work placements where needed.

### 6.b Duties of Jobseekers

A person receiving a benefit under Section 5 shall take part in the activities in their plan and accept suitable work. **Suitable work** means work reasonably related to the person's skills and experience, at the wage set by a collective agreement or under the Work and Collective Bargaining Act, within a reasonable commuting distance. After **six months**, suitable work includes any work the person is able to do.

### 6.c Fairness

A benefit may be reduced for refusing suitable work only after notice and a hearing, and is subject to review.

## Section 7 — Social Assistance

A person who is not entitled to unemployment insurance, or whose entitlement has ended, shall receive social assistance sufficient for a decent standard of living, as set by law, together with the rights in Section 6.a.

## Section 8 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution. Nothing in this Act requires any person to perform labor in violation of Article I, Section I.1.d.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 9 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 10 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment.
