# ARTICLE VI — THE JUDICIAL POWER

> **Draft note:** The election, qualifications, term, and vacancies of Supreme Court judges remain in Article III, Section 7, and the appointment and tenure of inferior-court judges remain in V.4; they are not repeated here.

## Section 1 — Vesting of Judicial Power

### VI.1.a — The Courts

The judicial power of the United States is vested in one Supreme Court and in such inferior courts as Congress establishes (IV.1.b).

### VI.1.b — Structure of the Inferior Courts

Congress shall establish trial courts and appellate courts sufficient to hear the cases within the judicial power.

Every final judgment of a trial court shall be subject to at least one level of appellate review. *(For criminal convictions, see also II.4.h.)*

Congress shall not abolish a court, or alter its jurisdiction, for the purpose of removing a judge or influencing the outcome of a pending case.

### VI.1.c — Scope of the Judicial Power

**Definitions:**
* **Case** means an actual dispute between parties with adverse legal interests that a court can resolve by a binding judgment.
* **Federal question** means a question arising under this Constitution, a law of the United States, or a treaty.

The judicial power extends to every case:

1. involving a federal question;
2. to which the United States is a party;
3. between two or more constituent jurisdictions (VII.2.a), or between a constituent jurisdiction and the United States;
4. affecting ambassadors or other representatives of foreign governments; and
5. otherwise placed within federal jurisdiction by this Constitution.

Courts shall decide only cases, and shall not issue advisory opinions.

## Section 2 — The Supreme Court

### VI.2.a — Composition

The Supreme Court consists of nine judges elected under Article III, Section 7.

The judge with the longest current continuous service is the presiding judge of the Court. Where two or more judges have equal service, the presiding judge shall be chosen among them by lot. *(This is the same judge who presides over a presidential impeachment trial under IV.9.f.)*

### VI.2.b — Staggered Elections

One seat on the Supreme Court shall be filled at the general election held every two years, so that each seat is filled once in each eighteen-year cycle.

For the first Court, lot shall assign the nine seats to terms ending two, four, six, eight, ten, twelve, fourteen, sixteen, and eighteen years after the Court first convenes.

A first-Court judge assigned a term of nine years or less may later be elected to one full term. A first-Court judge assigned a term of more than nine years has served the judge's single term under III.7.c. *(This follows the one-half rule of III.7.f.)*

A judge's term begins on the seventy-fifth day after the election.

A judge elected in a special election under III.7.f serves the remainder of the unexpired term, so that the staggered schedule is preserved.

### VI.2.c — Nonpartisan Elections

Supreme Court elections shall be nonpartisan.

No party name, symbol, or endorsement shall appear on the ballot for a Supreme Court election.

A candidate for the Supreme Court shall not:

1. be nominated by a political party;
2. campaign as the candidate or member of a political party;
3. accept a contribution, endorsement, or campaign assistance from a political party; or
4. promise or pledge how the candidate will decide a case or issue likely to come before the Court.

A candidate may discuss the candidate's qualifications, judicial philosophy, and past rulings or writings.

### VI.2.d — Jurisdiction

The Supreme Court has original jurisdiction in cases between two or more constituent jurisdictions, and between a constituent jurisdiction and the United States.

The Supreme Court has appellate jurisdiction over all other cases within the judicial power, and over any case in any court, including a state court, that involves a federal question.

Congress may regulate appellate procedure, but shall not remove from the Supreme Court or from every inferior court the jurisdiction to decide whether a law or governmental action complies with this Constitution.

### VI.2.e — Decisions

At least six judges shall participate in a decision of the Supreme Court.

The Court decides by a majority of the judges participating, except that an act of Congress may be held unconstitutional only with the agreement of at least five judges.

Where the judges participating are equally divided, the judgment under review stands.

Every decision of the Court shall be accompanied by a public written opinion stating its reasons. Any judge may publish a separate opinion.

## Section 3 — Judicial Review

### VI.3.a — Power of Every Court

Every court may determine, in a case before it, whether a law, regulation, executive order, or other governmental action complies with this Constitution, and shall not apply one that does not.

### VI.3.b — Finality of the Supreme Court

The decisions of the Supreme Court on questions of this Constitution and federal law are final, and bind every court and every governmental official.

### VI.3.c — Extent of Invalidity

A law or governmental action held unconstitutional has no legal effect to the extent of the violation (IV.12.a), and the remainder shall be given effect where it can operate independently.

The remedies available for a constitutional violation are those of II.9.c.

### VI.3.d — Rule of Interpretation

Where a provision of law can reasonably be read in more than one way, a court shall adopt the reading that preserves the greater protection of a constitutional right, as required by I.4.c and III.1.g.

## Section 4 — Judicial Independence and Conduct

### VI.4.a — Independence and Compensation

Judges shall decide cases independently and impartially, as required by II.9.a and III.7.d.

The compensation of a Supreme Court judge shall not be reduced during the judge's term. *(For inferior-court judges, see V.4.d.)*

The courts shall receive funding sufficient to perform their constitutional duties. Funding shall not be reduced or conditioned for the purpose of influencing a decision or retaliating against a court.

### VI.4.b — Recusal

A judge shall not participate in a case in which:

1. the judge, or a family member of the judge (V.5.a), has a personal or financial interest in the outcome;
2. a family member of the judge is a party or counsel;
3. the judge previously served as counsel or as a witness in the matter; or
4. the judge's impartiality might reasonably be questioned.

A party may ask the other judges of the same court to decide a contested recusal, or, where the court has no other judge, the court that hears appeals from it. On the Supreme Court, the remaining judges decide by majority.

### VI.4.c — Judicial Ethics

Congress shall establish a binding code of judicial conduct that applies to every judge, including the judges of the Supreme Court.

Every judge shall publicly disclose the judge's financial interests each year.

No judge shall accept a gift, payment, or other benefit from a party, counsel, or person with an interest in a case before the judge's court, or from a foreign government.

### VI.4.d — Judicial Conduct Commission

A Judicial Conduct Commission shall investigate and decide complaints of judicial misconduct against every judge, including the judges of the Supreme Court.

The Commission shall consist of nine members serving staggered six-year terms:

1. three judges of inferior courts;
2. three persons licensed to practice law for at least ten years who are not judges; and
3. three voters who are neither judges nor licensed to practice law.

Members shall be selected by public random drawing from the eligible applicants in each category, under the eligibility, verification, staggering, vacancy, service, and removal rules of III.3.f. One member of each category shall be selected every two years, and for the first selection, each group of three members assigned the same term shall include one member of each category. No more than three members may be affiliated with the same political party.

### VI.4.e — Discipline

After notice and a hearing that satisfy II.1.a, the Commission may:

1. dismiss the complaint;
2. issue a private or public reprimand;
3. require training or other corrective measures;
4. suspend the judge from hearing cases for a period not exceeding one year, without reduction in compensation; or
5. refer the matter to the House of Representatives for impeachment (IV.9).

Except under VI.4.f, only impeachment may remove a judge from office.

No judge shall be disciplined for the content of a lawful ruling. Legal error is corrected by appeal, not by discipline.

A disciplined judge may seek review by the Supreme Court, excluding the judge concerned.

The final outcome of every complaint, other than a dismissal, shall be made public.

### VI.4.f — Permanent Incapacity

If, after notice and a hearing that satisfy II.1.a, the Commission finds by clear and convincing evidence that a judge is permanently unable to perform the duties of office because of a physical or mental condition, the judge's office becomes vacant.

The judge may seek review of the finding by the Supreme Court, excluding the judge concerned. The office does not become vacant while that review is pending.

A finding of incapacity is not discipline and carries no penalty.

A vacancy on the Supreme Court is filled under III.7.f. A vacancy on an inferior court is filled under V.4.a.
