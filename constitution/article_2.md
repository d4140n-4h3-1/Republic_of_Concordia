# ARTICLE II — CUSTODY AND JUSTICE

## Section 1 — Justice and Due Process

### II.1.a — Right to Due Process

**Right:** Every person shall have the right to due process of law before the government may deprive them of life, liberty, or property.

**Definitions:**
* **Due process** means fair, lawful, and impartial procedures that include adequate notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before an impartial tribunal when the government imposes a deprivation of life, liberty, or property.
* **Legal deprivation** means a governmental action that takes, restricts, or substantially interferes with a person's life, liberty, or property.
* **Impartial tribunal** means a court or adjudicative body whose decision-makers have no personal or financial interest in the outcome and are not otherwise legally disqualified from deciding the matter.
* **Notice** means sufficient information concerning the governmental action, the factual basis for it, and the person's legal rights to permit a meaningful opportunity to respond.
* **Opportunity to be heard** means a meaningful opportunity to present relevant facts, evidence, arguments, and defenses before an impartial tribunal.
* **Lawful procedure** means a procedure authorized by this Constitution and applicable law.

No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law.

No person shall be subjected to an arbitrary governmental deprivation of liberty.

A governmental procedure shall not be considered due process merely because it is authorized by ordinary legislation if the procedure violates a right protected by this Constitution.

### II.1.b — Presumption of Innocence

**Right:** Every person accused of a criminal offense shall be presumed innocent until proven guilty according to law.

**Definitions:**
* **Accused person** means a person formally alleged by the government to have committed a criminal offense.
* **Presumption of innocence** means the legal requirement that an accused person be treated as innocent unless and until guilt is established according to law.
* **Conviction** means a final adjudication establishing criminal guilt.
* **Guilt** means legal responsibility for a criminal offense established according to the applicable standard of proof.

No accused person shall be punished as guilty before conviction.

Government officials shall not intentionally represent an accused person as legally guilty before conviction.

The presumption of innocence does not prohibit lawful investigative, protective, or custodial measures authorized by this Constitution.

### II.1.c — Taking of Private Property

**Right:** No private property shall be taken for public use without just compensation.

**Definitions:**

* **Taking** means a governmental acquisition of property, a permanent or temporary physical occupation or use of property, or a regulation that deprives the owner of all or substantially all of the property's economically beneficial use.
* **Public use** means use by the government or the public, or for a public purpose such as roads, utilities, public buildings, or relief in a declared emergency (V.6).
* **Just compensation** means the fair market value of the property at the time of the taking, or of its use for a temporary taking, together with any damage to the property and the reasonable cost of relocating a person or business displaced by the taking.

**Private transfer:** No property shall be taken in order to transfer it to a private person for private economic development. This does not prevent a taking for a public utility or common carrier that serves the public on equal terms.

**Payment:** Compensation shall be paid before or at the time of a taking. Where property is used in a declared emergency, compensation shall be paid within ninety days after the property is returned.

**Procedure:** The government shall give the owner written notice of a proposed taking and of its offer of compensation. The owner may challenge in court whether the taking is for public use and whether the compensation is just, and the court shall decide both questions independently of the government's determination. Where the final award exceeds the government's offer, the government shall pay the owner's reasonable legal and appraisal costs.

**Not takings:** The following are not takings:

1. taxes, fees, and fines lawfully imposed;
2. a seizure of property as evidence, or a forfeiture, permitted by this Constitution (I.3.b);
3. a law that prevents a use of property that would harm the health or safety of others, if the law applies equally to all comparable property; and
4. the destruction of property that is necessary to prevent imminent danger to life, such as stopping the spread of a fire.

## Section 2 — Categories of Custody

### II.2.a — Custody Classification

**Definitions:**
* **Custody** means governmental control over a person's freedom of movement or physical liberty pursuant to lawful authority.
* **Custodial category** means a legally defined form of custody distinguished by its purpose, legal basis, duration, and permitted restrictions.
* **Custodial status** means the specific legal condition under which a person is held.
* **Custodial authority** means the government authority legally responsible for a person's custody.

A person shall be held only under a custodial category authorized by this Constitution or by law consistent with this Constitution.

The government shall not use one custodial category to exercise powers reserved to another category.

The person's custodial category shall be recorded and communicated to the person as soon as reasonably practicable.

### II.2.b — Investigative Detention

**Definitions:**

* **Investigative detention** means temporary custody for the limited purpose of investigating a specific suspected offense or immediate threat.

Investigative detention shall be based upon reasonable suspicion supported by specific and articulable facts.

Investigative detention shall last no longer than reasonably necessary for its lawful purpose, and in no event longer than twenty-four hours without release, arrest, or lawful conversion to another custodial category.

Investigative detention shall not constitute punishment.

Investigative detention shall not be converted into another form of custody without the additional legal authority required for that category.

Investigative detention shall not be used merely to investigate a person's identity, beliefs, associations, protected status, or exercise of constitutional rights absent an independent constitutional basis.

### II.2.c — Arrest Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Arrest custody** means custody arising when a person is arrested based upon probable cause that the person committed or is committing a criminal offense.

Arrest custody shall be limited to lawful processing, investigation, prosecution, prevention of flight, protection from an identified legally sufficient danger, or bringing the person before a tribunal.

Arrest custody shall not constitute a criminal sentence.

### II.2.d — Pretrial Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Pretrial custody** means custody after arrest and before final adjudication.

Pretrial custody shall be imposed only on a ground permitted by II.3.d and only when no less restrictive measure is adequate for that ground.

A person held before trial shall retain the presumption of innocence.

Pretrial custody shall not constitute punishment.

Pretrial custody shall be subject to judicial review.

### II.2.e — Protective Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Protective custody** means temporary custody imposed to protect a person from an immediate or substantial threat to life or physical safety when voluntary protective measures are inadequate.

Protective custody shall be necessary and limited to the duration of the danger.

The least restrictive reasonably effective measure shall be used.

Protective custody shall be subject to prompt judicial review.

Protective custody shall not constitute punishment or serve as a substitute for criminal prosecution.

The wishes of a competent person placed in protective custody shall be given substantial weight and shall not be overridden except where this Constitution permits.

### II.2.f — Emergency Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Emergency custody** means temporary custody during an immediate emergency in which delay would create a substantial risk of death, serious bodily harm, or comparable catastrophe.

Emergency custody shall last only as long as reasonably necessary to stabilize the emergency or transfer the person to an appropriate lawful status.

The government shall document the factual circumstances requiring emergency custody.

Emergency custody shall be subject to prompt judicial review. Where the emergency itself makes review within that period impossible, review shall occur as soon as the emergency permits, and the government shall document why the delay was unavoidable.

Emergency custody shall not become a continuing custodial status merely through administrative continuation.

### II.2.g — Civil Protective Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Civil protective custody** means custody imposed for a noncriminal protective purpose, including protection of a person legally incapable of protecting their own immediate interests.

Civil protective custody shall not constitute punishment.

All constitutional rights shall remain in force except for restrictions necessary for the purpose of this custodial category that satisfy II.10.d.

Civil protective custody shall be subject to periodic judicial review.

### II.2.h — Public-Safety Detention

**Definitions:**

* **Public-safety detention** means pretrial custody based upon an individualized and substantiated determination that releasing a person would create a substantial and immediate danger to another person or the public.

Public-safety detention requires specific supporting facts and an individualized determination.

A court shall authorize or promptly review such detention.

Less restrictive alternatives shall be considered.

The seriousness of a criminal charge alone shall not establish dangerousness.

Public-safety detention shall be periodically reconsidered.

### II.2.i — Punitive and Post-Conviction Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Punitive custody** means imprisonment or another custodial restriction imposed as criminal punishment after valid conviction and pursuant to a lawful sentence.

Punitive custody shall exist only pursuant to a lawful sentence.

A person shall not be placed in punitive custody before conviction.

Restrictions imposed during punitive custody shall be limited to those authorized by the sentence, necessary for lawful custodial administration, or expressly permitted by this Constitution.

**Definitions:**

* **Post-conviction custody** means custody imposed after conviction for the purpose of carrying out a lawful sentence.

Post-conviction custody shall exist only for the period authorized by the sentence and applicable law.

Custody shall terminate when its lawful basis expires, subject to lawful modification, release, parole, pardon, commutation, or other constitutional termination of the sentence.

### II.2.j — Administrative Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Administrative custody** means temporary custody imposed for a specific lawful administrative purpose that does not constitute criminal punishment.

Administrative custody shall not be used to circumvent the constitutional requirements governing arrest, detention, prosecution, or punishment.

The government shall identify the legal authority and purpose of administrative custody.

Administrative custody shall last only as long as reasonably necessary for its lawful purpose and shall be subject to judicial review.

### II.2.k — Custody of Minors

**Definitions:**

* **Minor** means a person below the age of majority (I.4.a).

Custody of a minor shall have a lawful purpose authorized by this Constitution or by law consistent with this Constitution.

When a minor is accused or convicted of an offense, custodial systems shall prioritize safety, dignity, bodily integrity, education, development, rehabilitation, and lawful family or guardian relationships consistent with the rights and best interests of the minor.

A minor shall not be treated as an adult solely because the minor is accused or convicted of a criminal offense.

### II.2.l — Status-Based Custody

**Definitions:**

* **Status-based custody** means custody imposed because of immigration status, residency, citizenship, nationality, or another civil legal status rather than criminal conviction.

Status-based custody shall not constitute criminal punishment.

All constitutional rights shall remain in force except for restrictions necessary for the purpose of this custodial category that satisfy II.10.d.

Status-based custody shall be subject to prompt judicial review.

No person shall be held indefinitely without continuing lawful authority.

### II.2.m — Custody and Removal of Foreign Minors

**Right:** A foreign minor shall not be separated from a parent or legal guardian during removal when the minor and the parent or legal guardian are being removed to the same country.

**Definitions:**
* **Foreign minor** means a minor (II.2.k) who is not a citizen or national of the state exercising custody.
* **Parent** means the minor's legally recognized parent.
* **Legal guardian** means a person legally authorized to exercise parental or custodial responsibility for the minor.
* **Country of destination** means the country to which the government intends to remove the minor and the accompanying parent or legal guardian.
* **Removal** means the governmental transfer of a person from the territory to another country pursuant to immigration or other civil legal authority.
* **Governmental care** means civil protective custody (II.2.g) in which the government assumes responsibility for the minor's safety, welfare, basic needs, and legal interests.

A foreign minor and a parent or legal guardian subject to removal to the same country shall remain together throughout the removal process and shall arrive together in the country of destination.

The government shall not separate the minor from the parent or legal guardian for administrative convenience, punishment, deterrence, or immigration enforcement.

A minor shall not be removed separately from a parent or legal guardian when both are being removed to the same country.

If the parent or legal guardian is legally prohibited from accompanying the minor, is unable to accompany the minor because of circumstances beyond the government's control, or presents a constitutionally sufficient and individualized danger to the minor, the minor shall default to governmental care.

If no parent or legal guardian accompanies the minor to the country of destination, the minor shall default to governmental care. Governmental care shall be provided within the territory.

A minor in governmental care may later be removed only to a parent, legal guardian, or other lawful custodian able to receive the minor in the country of destination, and only after judicial review of the minor's best interests.

Governmental care shall not constitute punishment and shall be exercised according to the minor's best interests, safety, dignity, bodily integrity, education, and other constitutional rights.

Governmental care shall continue until the minor is lawfully reunited with a parent or legal guardian, transferred to another lawful custodian, or reaches the age of majority, as applicable under law.

Nothing in this subsection authorizes the removal of a minor or parent or legal guardian where such removal is otherwise prohibited by this Constitution, including by II.2.p.

### II.2.n — Custody Reclassification

**Definitions:**

* **Custody reclassification** means the lawful transfer of a person between custodial categories because the legal or factual basis for custody has changed.

Reclassification requires a lawful basis.

The government shall not reclassify a person solely to obtain powers unavailable under the person's existing custodial category.

Reclassification shall include notice as reasonably practicable, an individualized determination where constitutionally required, judicial review where constitutionally required, and compliance with the requirements of the new custodial category.

Reclassification shall not erase or cure a prior constitutional violation.

### II.2.o — General Custodial Principle

Custody constitutes a limited delegation of governmental authority over physical liberty and shall never constitute ownership of a person.

The government shall exercise only those powers reasonably necessary for the lawful purpose of the applicable custodial category.

Where two or more custodial categories could lawfully apply, the government shall use the less restrictive category unless it demonstrates that the more restrictive category is necessary.

No person shall be placed in undefined or unclassified governmental custody.

### II.2.p — Prohibition of Removal to Torture or Persecution

**Right:** No person shall be removed, extradited, or otherwise transferred to a country or place where there are substantial grounds to believe the person faces a substantial risk of torture or persecution.

**Definitions:**
* **Torture** means the intentional infliction of severe physical or mental pain or suffering for a purpose such as punishment, coercion, intimidation, or obtaining information or a confession.
* **Persecution** means severe harm, or a severe deprivation of fundamental rights, inflicted or knowingly tolerated by a government or other controlling authority because of a person's protected status (I.4.a), political opinion, or exercise of a right protected by this Constitution.

This right applies to every person regardless of citizenship, immigration status, criminal record, or custodial category, and admits no exception.

No person shall be transferred to a country or place from which the person is likely to be sent onward to a country or place described in this subsection.

Before any removal, extradition, or transfer, a person who claims a risk of torture or persecution shall receive an individualized determination and judicial review of that claim. The person shall not be removed while the claim or its judicial review is pending.

## Section 3 — Arrest and Detention

### II.3.a — Lawful Arrest

**Definitions:**
* **Arrest** means the governmental taking of a person into custody based upon lawful authority to initiate or pursue criminal proceedings.
* **Probable cause** means reasonable grounds, based upon known facts and circumstances, to believe that a person committed or is committing a criminal offense.
* **Arrest warrant** means a judicial order authorizing arrest based upon probable cause.
* **Warrantless arrest** means an arrest made without an arrest warrant under circumstances in which this Constitution or law permits arrest without prior judicial authorization.

An arrest shall not be arbitrary.

An arrest shall require an arrest warrant based upon probable cause or probable cause under circumstances in which obtaining a warrant is not reasonably practicable.

A person arrested without a warrant shall be brought before a tribunal without unnecessary delay, and in no event later than the period set in II.3.c, measured from the start of custody, including any investigative detention that preceded the arrest.

Arrest shall not constitute punishment before conviction.

### II.3.b — Notice of Arrest

**Right:** An arrested person shall have the right to know the basis for the arrest and the constitutional rights applicable to the person's custody.

**Definitions:**
* **Arrest notice** means information provided to an arrested person concerning the fact and basis of arrest and applicable constitutional rights.
* **Unnecessary delay** means delay not reasonably justified by lawful processing, emergency conditions, or other constitutionally sufficient circumstances.

As reasonably practicable, an arrested person shall be informed of:
1. The fact of arrest;
2. The offense or offenses alleged;
3. The right to remain silent;
4. The right to counsel;
5. The right to challenge the legality of detention; and
6. Other applicable constitutional rights.

Failure to provide notice shall not establish guilt or independently justify continued detention.

### II.3.c — Judicial Review of Detention

Every detained person shall have a prompt opportunity to challenge the legality and necessity of detention before an impartial tribunal.

**Definitions:**
* **Detention** means governmental custody restricting a person's liberty without constituting a final criminal sentence.
* **Judicial review** means examination by an impartial tribunal of the legality and constitutional sufficiency of governmental custody.
* **Release** means termination of governmental custody.

If the government cannot establish lawful grounds for continued detention, the person shall be released.

The government shall bear the burden of establishing the lawful basis for continued detention.

**Timing:** Judicial review of detention shall occur within forty-eight hours of the start of custody (including any investigative detention or arrest custody that preceded it) and shall be repeated at intervals of no more than thirty days for as long as detention continues. In this Article, "prompt" review means review within the first period, and "periodic" review means review at the repeated interval, unless a provision sets a different period.

### II.3.d — Pretrial Detention

**Definitions:**
* **Pretrial detention** means pretrial custody as defined in II.2.d.
* **Flight risk** means a substantial and factually supported likelihood that a person will intentionally evade lawful judicial proceedings.
* **Proceeding integrity** means protection of witnesses, evidence, jurors, or other essential components of a pending judicial proceeding from a specific and substantial threat.

Pretrial detention may be imposed only when no less restrictive measure is adequate and only when reasonably necessary to:
1. Protect another person or the public from a substantial and immediate danger, established under II.2.h;
2. Prevent a substantial and supported risk of flight; or
3. Protect the integrity of a judicial proceeding.

No person shall be detained solely because the person is generally considered dangerous, undesirable, unpopular, or likely to commit an offense based only upon an abstract or generalized prediction.

The seriousness of a criminal charge alone shall not establish the necessity of detention.

Pretrial detention shall be subject to periodic judicial review.

## Section 4 — Rights of the Accused

### II.4.a — Right to Counsel

**Right:** Every person accused of a criminal offense shall have the right to effective counsel.

**Definitions:**
* **Legal counsel** means a person lawfully authorized to provide legal representation.
* **Effective counsel** means representation reasonably competent and adequate to protect the accused person's legal rights.
* **Indigent person** means a person who lacks sufficient financial resources to obtain constitutionally required legal representation.

An accused person shall have a meaningful opportunity to consult with counsel without unreasonable governmental interference.

An indigent person facing imprisonment or a substantial deprivation of liberty shall be provided public counsel where constitutionally required.

Counsel shall have a reasonable opportunity to participate before a governmental decision materially affecting the accused person's defense is made.

### II.4.b — Right to Remain Silent

**Right:** No person shall be compelled to provide testimonial evidence against themselves.

**Definitions:**
* **Compelled testimony** means testimony obtained through governmental coercion or legal compulsion.
* **Testimonial evidence** means communication that reveals or communicates the person's thoughts, knowledge, or assertions.
* **Self-incrimination** means compelled communication that tends to establish the person's criminal liability.

The right applies during criminal investigation, custodial interrogation, prosecution, and trial.

No person shall be forced to confess to a crime.

No adverse inference of guilt shall be drawn solely from a person's lawful exercise of the right to remain silent.

This right does not prohibit lawful acquisition of physical evidence that does not constitute compelled testimony.

### II.4.c — Right to Notice of Charges

**Definitions:**
* **Criminal charge** means a formal governmental allegation that a person committed a criminal offense.
* **Particularized charge** means a charge stated with sufficient factual and legal specificity to permit meaningful preparation of a defense.

A person accused of a criminal offense shall receive sufficient notice of the charges to permit a meaningful defense.

No person shall be prosecuted without adequate notice except for lawful amendment of charges consistent with due process.

### II.4.d — Right to a Public and Speedy Trial

**Definitions:**
* **Speedy trial** means adjudication without unreasonable governmental delay.
* **Public trial** means a trial generally accessible to the public subject only to constitutionally sufficient restrictions.
* **Trial** means the judicial proceeding in which criminal guilt or innocence is adjudicated.
* **Unreasonable delay** means delay not justified by legitimate and constitutionally sufficient circumstances.

An accused person shall have the right to a speedy and public trial.

No person shall be subjected to indefinite prosecution through unreasonable governmental delay.

**Custody before trial:** An accused person held in custody shall be brought to trial within one hundred eighty days after being charged. If trial has not begun within that period, the person shall be released from custody pending trial unless a court finds that the delay was caused by the accused.

Public access may be restricted only when necessary to protect a compelling constitutional interest and no substantially less restrictive measure is adequate.

### II.4.e — Right to Confront Evidence

**Definitions:**
* **Evidence** means information lawfully presented to establish or challenge a material fact.
* **Material fact** means a fact that could reasonably affect the determination of guilt, innocence, or a legally significant issue.
* **Challenge** means a meaningful opportunity to question, rebut, test, or otherwise contest evidence.

An accused person shall have a meaningful opportunity to examine and challenge evidence used against them.

The government shall not knowingly rely upon evidence against an accused person without a meaningful opportunity to challenge it.

The accused shall have a reasonable opportunity to obtain relevant evidence, question opposing witnesses, and present a defense, subject to lawful evidentiary rules consistent with this Constitution.

### II.4.f — Standard of Proof

**Right:** No person shall be convicted of a criminal offense unless the government proves every required element beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Definitions:**
* **Element** means a fact or circumstance that the government must establish to prove a criminal offense.
* **Beyond a reasonable doubt** means a level of proof producing a firm conviction of guilt based upon the evidence, while recognizing that absolute mathematical certainty is not required.

The government shall bear the burden of proving criminal guilt.

An accused person shall not be required to prove their innocence.

**Facts that increase punishment:** Any fact, including a prior conviction, that increases the maximum punishment or requires a minimum punishment is an element of the offense. It shall be charged (II.4.c) and proven beyond a reasonable doubt to the trier of fact (II.4.j). A court may hear such a fact separately, after a finding of guilt on the other elements, to avoid unfair prejudice to the accused.

### II.4.g — Prohibition of Double Jeopardy

**Right:** No person shall be placed in jeopardy more than once for the same criminal offense after acquittal or conviction.

**Definitions:**
* **Jeopardy** means exposure to a lawful criminal proceeding in which a determination of guilt or innocence may be entered.
* **Same criminal offense** means the same offense as defined by law, including offenses that arise from the same legally protected conduct where separate prosecution would constitute a second prosecution for the same offense.

No person acquitted of an offense shall be retried for that offense, except where the prior proceeding was legally incapable of producing a valid judgment.

No person convicted of an offense shall be prosecuted again for the same offense.

**Multiple punishments:** No person shall receive more than one punishment for the same act or omission, unless each offense for which the person is punished requires proof of a fact that the others do not.

### II.4.h — Right to Appeal

**Right:** A person convicted of a criminal offense shall have a meaningful right to seek appellate review of the conviction and sentence.

**Definitions:**
* **Appeal** means review by a higher tribunal of a judgment or sentence for legal, constitutional, or other authorized error.
* **Appellate review** means examination of a lower tribunal's judgment or sentence according to law.

The government shall provide a meaningful appellate procedure for criminal convictions.

An indigent person shall receive counsel where necessary for a meaningful appeal.

### II.4.i — Interpreter and Language Access

**Right:** A person accused of a criminal offense who cannot adequately understand or communicate in the language used in the proceeding shall have access to a competent interpreter when necessary for meaningful participation.

The government shall not impose criminal liability or procedural disadvantage merely because an accused person lacks sufficient proficiency in the language of the proceeding.

### II.4.j — Trier of Fact

**Right:** A person accused of a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment of more than six months shall have guilt determined by an impartial jury drawn from a fair cross-section of the community.

A conviction requires a unanimous verdict.

Every juror shall have reached the age of majority (I.4.a).

## Section 5 — Custodial Treatment

### II.5.a — Humane Custody

**Definitions:**
* **Custodial person** means a person held under any governmental custodial category.
* **Humane treatment** means treatment consistent with human dignity, physical safety, bodily integrity, and basic human needs.
* **Cruel treatment** means intentional or reckless treatment causing unjustifiable severe physical or psychological suffering.
* **Degrading treatment** means treatment intended or reasonably likely to strip a person of basic human dignity without constitutionally sufficient justification.

No person in governmental custody shall be subjected to torture, cruel treatment, inhuman treatment, or degrading treatment.

The government shall be responsible for the basic safety and humane treatment of persons in its custody.

### II.5.b — Medical Care in Custody

**Right:** A person in governmental custody shall have access to medically necessary care.

**Definitions:**
* **Medically necessary care** means medical treatment reasonably required to prevent serious deterioration, address serious illness or injury, or protect life or essential bodily function.
* **Medical neglect** means unjustified failure to provide constitutionally required medical care.

The government shall not intentionally deny medically necessary care as punishment, and shall not commit medical neglect.

Medical treatment in custody shall remain subject to the bodily autonomy protections of Article I except where a restriction is expressly and constitutionally permitted.

### II.5.c — Separation of Custody and Punishment

Custody shall not constitute punishment unless it is imposed pursuant to a lawful criminal sentence.

**Definitions:**

* **Custodial restriction** means a restriction imposed for a lawful custodial purpose rather than as criminal punishment.

A person held before conviction shall not be subjected to punitive conditions merely because the person is accused of an offense.

Necessary security measures shall not constitute punishment solely because they restrict liberty.

### II.5.d — Use of Force

**Definitions:**

* **Custodial force** means physical force used by a government officer or agent against a person in custody.

Custodial force shall be used only when reasonably necessary for a lawful custodial purpose.

The degree of force shall be proportionate to the threat or purpose requiring it.

Deadly force shall be used in custody only when necessary to prevent an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm.

Force shall not be used for punishment, retaliation, humiliation, or coercion.

### II.5.e — Solitary Confinement

**Definitions:**

* **Solitary confinement** means confinement in which a person is substantially isolated from meaningful human contact for a continuous period.

Solitary confinement shall not be imposed as punishment for convenience alone.

It shall be used only when reasonably necessary for a specific and documented safety or security purpose.

The conditions and duration shall be reviewed within twenty-four hours of placement and at least every five days thereafter. This is a different period for purposes of II.3.c.

Solitary confinement shall not exceed fifteen consecutive days, and shall not be imposed on a minor or on a person whose mental or physical condition it would substantially worsen.

Solitary confinement shall not be imposed in a manner that constitutes torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment.

### II.5.f — Protection from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation

No person in governmental custody shall be subjected to sexual abuse, sexual exploitation, or sexual coercion by a government officer, employee, contractor, other person in custody, or other person exercising custodial authority.

The government shall take reasonable measures to prevent, investigate, and remedy custodial sexual abuse.

### II.5.g — Forced Labor

No person in custody shall be compelled to perform labor, except labor imposed by a sentencing court under I.1.d. No custodial official shall compel labor as discipline, retaliation, or exploitation.

Lawful work programs may be established in custodial institutions when consistent with human dignity, safety, applicable labor protections, and this Constitution.

No person shall be compelled to perform labor that constitutes cruel, degrading, or dangerous treatment without constitutionally sufficient justification.

### II.5.h — Custodial Oversight

**Definitions:**

* **Custodial oversight** means independent examination of governmental custody for compliance with this Constitution and applicable law.

Governmental custodial institutions shall be subject to lawful inspection and independent oversight.

Persons in custody shall have reasonable means to report abuse, neglect, unlawful conditions, or constitutional violations without retaliation.

## Section 6 — Search, Seizure, and Privacy

### II.6.a — Right to Privacy

**Right:** Every person shall have the right to privacy in their person, home, effects, communications, and personal information.

**Definitions:**
* **Privacy** means freedom from unjustified governmental intrusion into a person's protected personal sphere.
* **Personal information** means information reasonably connected to an identifiable person's private life, activities, communications, identity, or affairs.
* **Communication** means information intentionally transmitted between persons or systems.
* **Governmental intrusion** means governmental access, examination, monitoring, acquisition, or interference with a protected person, place, thing, communication, or information.

The government shall not intrude upon a person's protected privacy without constitutional justification.

### II.6.b — Protection Against Unreasonable Search and Seizure

**Definitions:**
* **Search** means governmental examination of a person, place, property, communication, or personal information that intrudes upon a protected privacy interest.
* **Seizure** means governmental taking, control, restraint, or substantial interference with a person or property.
* **Reasonable suspicion** means specific and articulable facts supporting a reasonable belief that unlawful conduct or a constitutionally sufficient circumstance exists.
* **Warrant** means judicial authorization for a specified search or seizure.
* **Particularized warrant** means a warrant identifying the person, place, property, information, or other subject of the authorized search or seizure with sufficient specificity to limit governmental authority.

Searches and seizures shall be reasonable.

General warrants shall be prohibited. Every warrant shall be a particularized warrant.

### II.6.c — Warrant Requirement

A search or seizure requiring a warrant shall be conducted only pursuant to a valid warrant issued by an impartial tribunal.

A warrant shall:
1. Be supported by sufficient constitutional grounds;
2. Identify the subject of the search or seizure;
3. Identify the offense, investigation, or lawful purpose supporting the warrant;
4. Limit the scope of governmental authority; and
5. Extend no further than reasonably necessary for its lawful purpose.

A warrantless search or seizure shall be permitted only where this Constitution expressly authorizes it or where obtaining a warrant is not reasonably practicable and sufficient constitutional justification exists.

### II.6.d — Consent and Exigent Circumstances

**Definitions:**
* **Consent** means voluntary and informed permission for a search or seizure by a person legally capable of granting that permission.
* **Exigent circumstances** means circumstances in which an immediate governmental action is reasonably necessary to prevent death, serious bodily harm, imminent destruction of evidence, escape, or another constitutionally sufficient emergency.

A search or seizure conducted pursuant to consent shall be limited to the scope of the consent.

Consent obtained through coercion or material deception shall not constitute voluntary consent.

A warrantless search or seizure based upon exigent circumstances shall be limited to the emergency requiring immediate action.

The government shall not manufacture or intentionally create exigent circumstances solely to avoid the warrant requirement.

**Samples from the body:** The natural fading of a substance from a person's body is not, by itself, an exigent circumstance. A sample of a person's blood, urine, or other bodily fluid or tissue may be taken only with a warrant or with consent. A breath test may be taken without a warrant only after a lawful arrest for impaired conduct that rests on evidence of impairment other than the odor or sight of a substance (I.3.b). The government shall provide a means of obtaining a warrant electronically, from an impartial tribunal, at any hour, so that a warrant for a bodily sample can be sought without delay.

### II.6.e — Digital and Personal Information

Governmental access to private digital data, electronic communications, or stored personal information shall be subject to the same constitutional protections against unreasonable search and seizure as other protected personal information.

The scope of any warrant or lawful warrantless access shall be limited to the information reasonably necessary for its lawful purpose.

## Section 7 — Sentencing and Punishment

### II.7.a — Lawful Punishment

**Definitions:**
* **Punishment** means a governmental penalty imposed for criminal conduct following lawful adjudication.
* **Sentence** means the judicially imposed legal consequences of a criminal conviction.
* **Valid law** means a law consistent with this Constitution and applicable at the relevant time.

No person shall be punished without a valid constitutional and legal basis.

**Clarity of criminal law:** No person shall be punished under a criminal law so vague that an ordinary person cannot reasonably understand what conduct it prohibits.

No person shall be punished for conduct that was not criminal when committed, except that a later law reducing or eliminating punishment may be applied retroactively where authorized.

### II.7.b — Proportionality of Punishment

**Definitions:**
* **Proportionate punishment** means punishment reasonably related to the seriousness and circumstances of the offense and the legitimate purposes of criminal justice.
* **Seriousness of offense** means the legally relevant degree of harm, culpability, risk, and other circumstances associated with an offense.
* **Legitimate purposes of criminal justice** means constitutionally permissible purposes including public safety, accountability, deterrence, rehabilitation, and lawful protection of others.

No punishment shall be grossly disproportionate to the offense.

This limit applies both to each sentence and to the total punishment imposed on a person for offenses sentenced in the same proceeding or arising from the same criminal episode, including consecutive sentences.

No punishment shall be imposed solely for vengeance, humiliation, or degradation.

### II.7.c — Prohibited Punishment

No person shall be subjected to torture, cruel, inhuman, degrading, or grossly disproportionate punishment.

No punishment shall intentionally impose suffering beyond what is reasonably related to a legitimate and constitutional purpose of criminal justice.

No person shall be subjected to collective punishment for the conduct of another person.

### II.7.d — Sentencing of Minors

**Right:** No person who committed a criminal offense while below the age of majority (I.4.a) shall be sentenced to death or imprisonment without a meaningful opportunity for parole.

**Definitions:**
* **Minor offender** means a person who was below the age of majority when the criminal offense was committed.
* **Age at offense** means the person's chronological age on the date the criminal conduct occurred.
* **Parole** means conditional release from imprisonment before the maximum lawful period of custody expires, subject to lawful conditions.
* **Parole eligibility** means the earliest point at which a person may petition for or receive consideration for conditional release.
* **Meaningful opportunity for parole** means a genuine opportunity for release based upon individualized consideration of maturity, rehabilitation, circumstances, culpability, and other constitutionally relevant factors.
* **Maximum parole-ineligible period** means the maximum period of imprisonment that may elapse before a minor offender becomes eligible for parole consideration.

A minor offender shall not:
1. Be sentenced to death;
2. Be sentenced to life imprisonment without parole;
3. Receive a sentence requiring more than fifteen years of imprisonment before parole eligibility; or
4. Receive an equivalent sentence that effectively denies a meaningful opportunity for release before fifteen years.

The maximum parole-ineligible period shall be measured from the commencement of the sentence.

Consecutive sentences arising from offenses committed during the same criminal episode shall not be used to circumvent the fifteen-year maximum parole-ineligible period.

Parole eligibility shall not guarantee release.

A minor offender may remain imprisoned after becoming eligible for parole if a lawful parole determination establishes that continued imprisonment is constitutionally and legally justified.

In sentencing a minor offender, the sentencing authority shall consider age, maturity, circumstances of the offense, culpability, rehabilitation, mitigating circumstances, and other relevant factors.

No later law, judicial interpretation, administrative practice, or custodial arrangement shall circumvent the protections of this subsection.

The protections of this subsection shall be determined by the person's age at the time of the offense, regardless of the person's age at arrest, conviction, sentencing, or imprisonment.

### II.7.e — Adult Capital Punishment

**Rule:** A sentence of death may be imposed only for aggravated murder and only in compliance with this subsection.

**Definitions:**
* **Aggravated murder** means the intentional and premeditated killing of another person where at least one of the following is proven beyond a reasonable doubt:
  1. the offender intentionally killed more than one person;
  2. the victim was a minor;
  3. the killing was committed as part of an act of terrorism; or
  4. the victim was a public official killed because of the official's office or official acts.
* **Act of terrorism** means violence intended to intimidate or coerce a civilian population or a government for a political, religious, or ideological purpose.
* **Public official** means a person holding elected or appointed governmental office.

A sentence of death shall require all of the following:

1. **Adult offender:** the person was at or above the age of majority at the time of the offense, as required by II.7.d;
2. **Unanimous jury:** an impartial jury drawn from a fair cross-section of the community unanimously finds guilt, unanimously finds each aggravating circumstance relied upon, and unanimously determines that death is the appropriate sentence;
3. **Heightened counsel:** the accused is represented by at least two counsel, at least one of whom has substantial experience in capital cases, provided at public expense where the accused is indigent; and
4. **Mandatory appeal:** the conviction and sentence receive appellate review, which the accused may not waive, and no sentence of death shall be carried out while that review is pending.

No method of execution shall constitute torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment.

This subsection is an express constitutional permission for purposes of I.4.c and is the only exception to II.7.c and I.3.a for capital punishment. No law shall extend capital punishment to any other offense.

### II.7.f — Excessive Fines

**Right:** No person shall be subjected to excessive fines.

**Definitions:**

* **Excessive fine** means a monetary penalty grossly disproportionate to the offense, the person's culpability, or the legitimate purpose of the penalty.

No fine shall be imposed primarily for governmental enrichment or punishment unrelated to legitimate criminal-justice purposes.

## Section 8 — Rights After Conviction

### II.8.a — Continued Constitutional Rights

Conviction shall not extinguish constitutional rights except where those rights are lawfully and necessarily restricted by this Constitution or by a lawful sentence consistent with this Constitution.

A convicted person shall retain every constitutional right not expressly or necessarily restricted by lawful custody or sentence.

Conviction shall not forfeit a person's humanity, dignity, or status as a person.

### II.8.b — Rehabilitation

**Definitions:**
* **Rehabilitation** means lawful efforts to reduce the likelihood of future criminal conduct and facilitate the person's return to lawful and productive participation in society.
* **Rehabilitative program** means an educational, vocational, therapeutic, behavioral, or other lawful program reasonably intended to support rehabilitation.

The government shall, where reasonably practicable, provide meaningful opportunities for rehabilitation.

A custodial authority shall not deny rehabilitative opportunities solely for humiliation or retaliation.

Rehabilitative programs shall respect constitutional rights.

### II.8.c — Restoration and Reentry

**Definitions:**
* **Completed sentence** means the lawful criminal sentence has expired or otherwise been fully satisfied.
* **Collateral restriction** means a legal restriction that continues after completion of a criminal sentence.
* **Reentry** means the process of returning a person who has completed a custodial sentence to ordinary civil life.

After completion of a sentence, no person shall remain subject to continuing punishment merely because of the conviction.

A collateral restriction shall have an independent lawful basis and shall not exist solely as continuing punishment.

The government shall facilitate lawful reentry where reasonably practicable.

**Parole:** Parole shall be available at least to the extent required by II.7.d. Where parole is available, release decisions shall be made through an individualized procedure that satisfies due process and gives written reasons for any denial.

## Section 9 — Judicial Integrity and Remedies

### II.9.a — Right to an Independent Judiciary

**Definitions:**
* **Judicial independence** means freedom of judicial decision-making from political command, improper influence, personal interest, or other unlawful interference.
* **Judicial proceeding** means a proceeding before a court or other constitutionally authorized adjudicative tribunal.

Every person shall have the right to an independent and impartial judiciary.

Judicial decisions shall be based upon law and admissible evidence rather than political command or personal preference.

Government officials shall not direct a court to reach a particular determination of guilt, innocence, liability, or sentence.

### II.9.b — Right to Challenge Governmental Custody

Every person in governmental custody shall have access to a judicial procedure capable of determining the lawfulness of that custody.

A court shall have authority to order release when the government lacks sufficient lawful authority to continue custody.

The government shall not prevent a person from challenging the legality of their custody.

### II.9.c — Remedy for Constitutional Violation

**Definitions:**
* **Effective remedy** means a judicial or lawful governmental measure reasonably capable of correcting or redressing a constitutional violation.
* **Constitutional violation** means governmental conduct that violates a right, limitation, or requirement established by this Constitution, or conduct by a private actor that violates a provision of this Constitution that expressly binds private actors.

Every person shall have the right to an effective legal remedy for a constitutional violation.

Depending upon the circumstances, an effective remedy may include:
1. Release from unlawful custody;
2. Exclusion of unlawfully obtained evidence;
3. Reversal of an unlawful judgment or conviction;
4. Injunctive relief;
5. Restoration of unlawfully taken rights or property;
6. Compensation, as provided by law, which legislation shall not eliminate for unlawful deprivation of liberty; or
7. Another lawful measure reasonably capable of correcting the violation.

No remedy shall be denied solely because a government official acted pursuant to an ordinary law that is inconsistent with this Constitution.

**Exclusion of evidence:** Evidence obtained in violation of this Constitution, and evidence derived from such a violation, shall be excluded from any criminal proceeding against a person whose constitutional rights were violated in obtaining it, except where the government proves that the evidence:

1. was also obtained from a lawful source independent of the violation; or
2. would inevitably have been discovered through lawful means already underway at the time of the violation.

No law shall create any other exception.

## Section 10 — Constitutional Limits on Custodial Authority

### II.10.a — Limited Nature of Custody

**Definitions:**
* **Custodial power** means governmental authority to restrict a person's physical liberty under a lawful custodial category. *(Custodial authority, meaning the responsible government body, is defined in II.2.a.)*
* **Custodial purpose** means the specific constitutionally permissible objective for which custody is imposed.

Custody shall confer only those governmental powers reasonably necessary to accomplish the lawful custodial purpose.

Custody shall not constitute ownership, general control, or sovereignty over a person.

No custodial power shall exist without a lawful custodial purpose.

### II.10.b — Least Restrictive Custodial Measure

**Right:** When multiple lawful measures can adequately accomplish the same legitimate custodial purpose, the government shall use the least restrictive measure.

**Definitions:**
* **Least restrictive custodial measure** means the lawful measure that adequately accomplishes the legitimate custodial purpose while imposing the least restriction upon the person's constitutional rights.
* **Adequate protection** means protection sufficient to accomplish the legitimate governmental purpose without unnecessary additional restriction.

Custodial necessity shall not be presumed merely for governmental convenience.

### II.10.c — Individualized Custody

**Right:** Custodial restrictions shall be based upon the individual circumstances of the person whenever individualized determination is reasonably practicable and constitutionally required.

**Definitions:**
* **Individualized determination** means a determination based upon facts and circumstances specific to the person and relevant to the lawful custodial purpose.
* **Blanket restriction** means a restriction imposed upon an entire class of persons without consideration of relevant individual circumstances.

Custody status alone shall not establish that every restriction is necessary. A blanket restriction is permitted only where individualized determination is not reasonably practicable and the restriction otherwise satisfies II.10.d.

### II.10.d — Constitutional Standard for Custodial Exceptions

Any restriction upon a constitutional right imposed because of custody shall satisfy the constitutional exception requirements of Article I, Section I.4.c.

No custodial authority shall create a broader constitutional exception than this Constitution permits.

The government bears the burden of establishing the constitutional basis, necessity, proportionality, and continuing factual basis for any custody-based restriction.

A restriction shall cease when the circumstances authorizing it cease.
