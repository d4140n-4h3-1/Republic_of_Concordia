# EDUCATION ACT — NATIONAL EDUCATION SYSTEM

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **National Education System Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

Article I, Section I.2.a guarantees every person access to free public education, from primary through higher and vocational education. The purpose of this Act is to give that guarantee a common structure across Concordia, modeled on a system that combines high standards, strong teaching, and many paths to success, while leaving schools to the states, territories, and local governments that run them.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every public school and public institution of education in a state, territory, or local government that accepts federal education funding, and to every school run by the federal government.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Section IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.2.a against every level of government; under IV.1.b(3), to appropriate public funds; and under IV.1.b(17).

The operation of schools remains with the states, territories, and local governments (Article VII, Section VII.3.a). This Act sets common standards, not a federal school system.

## Section 4 — Stages of Education

### 4.a Early Childhood

Every child may attend two years of free public early childhood education, beginning at age four. Attendance is voluntary.

### 4.b Primary

Primary education lasts **six years**, beginning at age six. It is compulsory.

### 4.c Secondary

Secondary education lasts **four or five years**, depending on the student's pace. The first two years are compulsory.

### 4.d Post-Secondary

After secondary education, every student may continue, free of charge (Article I, Section I.2.a), at:

1. an **academic college**, preparing students for university over two years;
2. a **polytechnic**, offering applied and professional diplomas over three years; or
3. a **technical institute**, offering vocational and trade certificates over two years.

A student who completes any of these may continue to a university, a polytechnic, or further technical study.

### 4.e University

Every public university shall admit students on neutral academic criteria (Article I, Section I.2.a). Graduates of polytechnics and technical institutes shall have a recognized route to university admission.

## Section 5 — No Dead Ends

No student shall be placed on a path from which there is no route to further education. Every pathway in Section 4.d shall lead to at least one route to a university degree, and a student may move between pathways at any stage.

## Section 6 — Private and Home Education

Every person may choose private or home education (Article I, Section I.2.a). The only minimum standards that may be required are:

1. instruction in literacy, mathematics, and science sufficient to reach the core standards of the Curriculum and Languages Act at the end of primary and lower secondary education; and
2. an assessment of those core standards at those two points, which a home-educated student may take at any public school, free of charge.

No private or home program shall be required to follow the national curriculum beyond these core standards.

## Section 7 — Equal Access

No student shall be denied admission to, or placement in, any public school or pathway because of protected status (Article I, Section I.4.a). Placement shall rest only on neutral academic criteria, the student's choice, and available places.

## Section 8 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution, and in favor of access to education.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 9 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 10 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect at the start of the first school year beginning two years after its enactment, so that schools have time to prepare.
