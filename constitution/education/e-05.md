# EDUCATION ACT — FUNDING AND LIFELONG LEARNING

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Learning Funding Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

The purpose of this Act is to fund schools fairly, so that a child's education does not depend on the wealth of their community, and to give every person the means to keep learning throughout life.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

This Act applies to every school covered by the National Education System Act, Section 3, and to every resident of Concordia under Section 6.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(3), IV.1.b(15), and IV.1.b(17).

**Federal fallback:** Where a state or territory declines federal education funding, the federal government shall still provide Learning Accounts, Lifelong Learning Accounts, and the support in Section 7 directly to its residents. The duty of every state and territory to provide free public education under Article I, Section I.2.a continues whether or not it accepts federal funding.

## Section 4 — School Funding

### 4.a Equal Base

Every public school shall receive the same base funding for each student, adjusted only for differences in local costs.

### 4.b Added Funding for Need

Schools shall receive additional funding for each student from a family in need, each student with a disability, and each student learning an official language for the first time. This is a benefit addressing need under Article I, Section I.4.a.

### 4.c No Local Shortfall

Where local funding for a school falls below the national base, federal funding shall make up the difference for any state, territory, or local government that accepts federal education funding.

## Section 5 — Student Learning Accounts

### 5.a Deposits

Every student in primary and secondary education shall have a **Learning Account**, into which the government shall deposit an amount set by law each year, with a larger deposit for students from families in need.

### 5.b Use

A Learning Account may be used only for enrichment, such as courses, books, music, sport, trips, or technology for learning, at any approved provider, public or private.

### 5.c Carrying Forward

Any unused balance shall carry forward, and shall transfer to the person's Lifelong Learning Account under Section 6 when they leave secondary education.

## Section 6 — Lifelong Learning

### 6.a Lifelong Learning Accounts

Every resident of Concordia who has reached the age of majority shall have a **Lifelong Learning Account**, into which the government shall deposit an amount set by law at age twenty-five, and again every ten years after that.

### 6.b Use

A Lifelong Learning Account may be used for any approved course of study or training, at a university, polytechnic, technical institute, or other approved provider, including part-time and online study.

### 6.c Free Higher Education

Nothing in this Section limits the right to free public higher and vocational education under Article I, Section I.2.a. Lifelong Learning Accounts are in addition to that right.

## Section 7 — Support for Students in Need

Every student from a family in need shall receive, free of charge, school meals, uniforms, books, transport, and the technology needed for schoolwork.

## Section 8 — Transparency

Every government shall publish each school's funding per student, and how it was spent, each year (Freedom of Information Act, Section 9).

## Section 9 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 10 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 11 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the first day of the next fiscal year following its enactment. Section 4 shall be phased in over five years.
