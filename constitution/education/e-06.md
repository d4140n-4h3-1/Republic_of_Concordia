# EDUCATION ACT — CONSTITUTIONAL LITERACY

## Section 1 — Short Title

This Act may be cited as the **Constitutional Literacy Act**.

## Section 2 — Purpose

A constitution protects only the people who know what it says. The purpose of this Act is to make deceit harder: to ensure that every person learns the Constitution and how to use it, can check what they are told against the text, and is told plainly when the government acts against them, so that no official, candidate, or campaign can mislead them about their rights.

## Section 3 — Scope and Authority

Part One applies to every school covered by the National Education System Act, Section 3. Part Two applies to every government in Concordia.

Congress enacts this Act under Article IV, Sections IV.1.b(3) and IV.1.b(17), and under IV.1.b(15), to enforce Article I, Section I.2.a, Article II, Sections II.6.d and II.9.c, and Article VIII, Section VIII.1.b against every level of government.

**Part One — In Schools**

## Section 4 — Constitutional Instruction

### 4.a What Every Student Learns

Before completing secondary education, every student shall be taught:

1. the full text of the Constitution, read from the source, and the purpose of each Article;
2. the rights it protects, and the limits on every exception to them (Article I, Section I.4.c);
3. how to use those rights: refusing a search without a warrant, remaining silent, requesting public records, challenging an election, and seeking a remedy in court;
4. how the powers of government are divided and checked, including elections, courts, impeachment, the limits on emergencies, and the right of resistance and its limits (Article X, Section 4); and
5. how to read a law or a bill and compare it with the Constitution.

### 4.b Stages

Basic rights and the structure of government shall be taught in primary education. The full text, and the use of rights in practice, shall be taught in secondary education.

### 4.c Completion

Every student shall complete a civic project before finishing secondary education: an analysis of a real law, public record, or government decision against the Constitution, in a form the student chooses. A student who does not complete it shall have unlimited free chances to do so. No student shall be denied a diploma for the views expressed in the project.

The project shall be graded only on the accuracy of its citations, its use of evidence, and the quality of its reasoning, and never on its conclusion. A student may ask for an independent second marker.

### 4.d Home and Private Education

A student in home or private education is not required to complete the civic project (National Education System Act, Section 6), but may complete it at any public school, free of charge, under the same standards.

## Section 5 — Information Literacy

Every student shall be taught to:

1. find and read primary sources, including the text of the Constitution, laws, court decisions, and public records under the Freedom of Information Act;
2. tell a fact from an opinion, and a claim from its evidence;
3. recognize common techniques of manipulation, such as quoting out of context, false choices, appeals to fear, misleading statistics, and fabricated or altered images and recordings;
4. read basic statistics and charts; and
5. check a claim about the Constitution or the law against its source.

## Section 6 — Neutrality

### 6.a No Party

Instruction under this Part shall teach what the Constitution says and how to examine claims, not which party, candidate, or policy to support. Examples of misleading claims shall be drawn from across the political spectrum. Balance shall be measured across each course. Each school shall keep a record of every example of a misleading claim used in instruction, with its source, open to students and their families.

### 6.b Contested Questions

Where the meaning of a provision is contested, teachers shall present the main reasonable readings and the arguments for each.

### 6.c No Grading of Views

No student shall be graded on, or required to profess, any political view or belief (Article I, Sections I.1.a and I.1.b).

### 6.d No Official Truth List

No government shall designate any publication, outlet, or person as a trustworthy or untrustworthy source for purposes of instruction (Article I, Section I.1.e). Students shall learn to judge sources by their evidence.

### 6.e Religious Objection

Where a student or the student's family has a sincere religious objection to particular materials, the student may meet the same learning goals with alternative materials of equal rigor (Article I, Section I.1.b).

### 6.f Open Materials

Every curriculum and teaching material used under this Part shall be published, free of charge, under the Curriculum and Languages Act.

**Part Two — By Government**

## Section 7 — The Constitution for Everyone

### 7.a Official Text and Guide

The federal government shall publish, free of charge, in print and online:

1. the official text of the Constitution; and
2. a plain-language guide that explains each provision and cites the text for every statement it makes, so that every reader can check it.

Where the meaning of a provision is contested, the guide shall say so and present the main reasonable readings and the arguments for each. The guide shall be written and revised by the Curriculum Council (Curriculum and Languages Act, Section 4), not by any executive official, and reviewed at least every six years.

The text and guide shall be available in every language required by Article I, Section I.1.f, and in accessible formats for persons with disabilities.

### 7.b Copies

Every person shall receive a copy of the text and the guide on reaching the age of majority, on naturalization, and on request. Every public library and school shall keep copies.

### 7.c Lifelong Learning

Free courses on the Constitution shall be offered at public libraries and online, and may be paid for from a Lifelong Learning Account (Learning Funding Act, Section 6).

## Section 8 — Training for Officials

Every person who swears the oath of office under Article VIII, Section VIII.1.b, and every law enforcement officer, shall complete training on the Constitution, and on the limits of their own authority under it, before exercising that authority, and again at least every four years.

## Section 9 — Plain Statements of Authority

### 9.a Statement Required

When a government officer orders, requests, or demands that a person do or give up something, including to submit to a search, answer questions, leave a place, or surrender property, the officer shall, if asked, state plainly the law or constitutional provision that authorizes it.

### 9.b Consent

Before asking a person to consent to a search or seizure, an officer shall tell the person that they may refuse. Consent given without that notice is not voluntary and informed consent (Article II, Section II.6.d).

### 9.c Written Notice

Every written order, decision, or denial by a government that affects a person's rights shall state the authority relied on, the person's right to challenge it, and how.

## Section 10 — Misstating Rights

### 10.a Prohibited

No government officer shall knowingly misstate a person's constitutional rights, or the officer's authority, to obtain the person's compliance, consent, or waiver.

### 10.b Consequences

Any consent, statement, or waiver obtained by a knowing misstatement is invalid, and evidence obtained from it shall be excluded (Article II, Section II.9.c). A knowing misstatement is misconduct subject to discipline, and the person misled may bring a civil action for any harm it caused.

## Section 11 — Constitution Day

The day on which this Constitution took effect shall be **Constitution Day**. Every public school shall teach about the Constitution on that day, and public offices shall hold public readings and open sessions explaining their duties under it.

## Section 12 — Constitutional Construction

This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution.

No provision of this Act shall be interpreted to:

1. require any person to profess a belief, or punish any person for a view;
2. permit any government to declare what is true in public debate; or
3. restrict any right under Article I, Section 1.

If an application of this Act conflicts with a constitutional right, the constitutional provision controls.

## Section 13 — Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to any person or circumstance is held unconstitutional, the remaining provisions and applications shall remain in effect to the maximum extent permitted by the Constitution.

## Section 14 — Effective Date

This Act takes effect on the same date as the National Education System Act, except that Sections 9 and 10 take effect on enactment, and the guide under Section 7 shall be published within one year after enactment.
